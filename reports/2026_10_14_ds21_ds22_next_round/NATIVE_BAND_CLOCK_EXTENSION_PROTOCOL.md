# Prespecified native-time band-clock extension: DS22-001

The preceding reference-free capacity audit found 59.63 Hz RMS mismatch when projecting separately fitted half-band receiver corrections onto the current shared calibration design. This experiment adds the 21 columns needed to represent those functions: 18 receiver-by-band clock interactions, two receiver band drifts and one band RF contrast. Geography and satellite timing remain shared. It does not identify a hardware effect or assume that increased calibration capacity improves localization.

All views retain the complete training frame, native branch timestamps, candidate bank, receiver lifts, clock nodes and centering fractions. The original 20 nuisance coordinates retain their order; the 21 extension coefficients are appended. Their initial values are exactly zero for each of the same three training-derived seed states and its original 25 km disk. The likelihood is unchanged except for the additional correction function. All/low/high masks score the corresponding rows while preserving the identical full-frame parameterization.

At zero extension, the objective, every original physical/nuisance gradient and all likelihood responsibilities must reproduce the original model exactly. Extension gradients are checked by finite differences and need not vanish. The original common-clock, group and satellite-timing priors remain unchanged. Receiver band-clock contrast precision is `f_r*(1-f_r)*P_r`, matching the extra penalty from the training-fraction-weighted low/high effective clock functions. The added drift and RF terms receive no new Gaussian prior, matching the original bounded-only slope/RF treatment. This is one prespecified model, not hyperparameter selection by reference.

Bounds apply to effective calibration in both bands: clock coefficients ±2000, receiver slopes ±60 Hz/s, and fitted `c` ±5000. Independently loose common/contrast boxes cannot replace those coupled constraints. Existing group-offset alias bounds, regional and local disks, and satellite timing constraints remain. Joint KKT residuals use all physical and nuisance coordinates and the full cone of active constraint normals. Acceptance remains at 0.001; no separable numerical finish is used.

Qualification is bounded to 120 seconds and 72 objective evaluations, with no optimizer fits. It checks all three original seeds under every view, exact zero-extension replay, coupled feasibility and three extension gradient directions per seed/view. Component tests additionally cover every extension coordinate, coupled constraint Jacobians, active-bound KKT and a real bounded synthetic fit. Exact original nodes, clock precision and the two-receiver frame are required.

After qualification, nine fits run in three bounded view chunks, using the same seeds and disks as the original matched pilot. Each optimizer admits objective evaluations for at most 20 seconds and reserves its 600th actual evaluation for a final independent gradient/feasibility audit. That reserved audit follows the optimizer deadline; `complete_elapsed_s` is reported and is not claimed to be a hard 20-second invocation limit. Each complete view chunk has a 150-second internal cap. No failed fit is retried. Within each view, training objective selects among strictly accepted outcomes; all nine must seal before a configured-reference comparison.

This is a conditional shared-discovery channel ablation, not independent half discovery. Its common-clock interactions distinguish it from the earlier negative DS21-039 three-column band-calibration trial. The current DS22 reference is still configured-unconfirmed, and no target claim is permitted from that reference alone.
