# Cooperative receiver positioning from Bellaterra’s measured ORBCOMM Doppler

A new geographic site produced real position estimates from six ORBCOMM satellites, with mixed results. The held-day Doppler-shape method published **three of four containing offers with 9.68–10.59 km error**. It also published **two of four near-omitted offers**. Its better frequency residuals and day-to-day agreement failed to guarantee proximity to the reported receiver. The matched absolute-carrier control put three of four containing fits within 5 km, while falsely publishing all four omitted offers. The ordinary shape model used real CFO ordering and reported low held pass-balanced residuals of 3.24–4.31 Hz on the containing offers with independent days agreeing within 0.26–1.28 km. The absolute control recovered better positions at substantially higher held RMS of 26.63–27.81 Hz. The two false held-shape publications were approximately 9.37 and 9.68 km from the surveyed site and both lay well inside the offered region. A near-face rule alone could not catch these. The labels-only control retained the publishable source's original site-filtered satellite IDs and timestamps while reversing the real measured values in each pass. Its guarded 0/8 publications and RF residuals exceeding 3.5 kHz show that the received carrier time order is contributing information. This does not remove the archive’s original geographic conditioning. ## Receiver and historical orbit authority

The public owner deposit contains 615,738 UTC-tagged bladeRF carrier-offset observations from one station in Bellaterra, Spain. Six ORBCOMM satellites occupy six nominal carriers in 137.2875–137.8000 MHz. The owners describe VHF SDPSK carrier acquisition and a geographic ±500 Hz filter used for publication and attribution. Only the detector’s published `doppler_measured_hz`, recorded UTC, nominal RF and cooperative satellite ID went into the position worker. The five-column release lacks predicted Doppler, rejected observations and raw IQ. This is an attributed narrowband position experiment on a surveyed measured site. It cannot qualify anonymous Ku Qin acquisition or wideband positioning. The reference uses the article’s rounded 41.5002°N, 2.1129°E and 198.5 m. Position fitting uses fixed ellipsoidal height 0 m; the deposit’s more precise coordinates agree within a few metres. [Owner dataset](https://doi.org/10.34810/DATA3383) · [Owner data article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dib.2026.113387) · [Downloaded object receipts](publisher-receipt.json)

Four frozen, disjoint two-day blocks from March 30 to April 6 supplied 23,049 unique admitted measurement rows after uniform pass downsampling. Each opaque 25 km offer received an identical block-specific input in isolation. The one-constant-per-pass model projects out the RF-coefficient column exactly, with a measured maximum residual column of zero in every case. Its fitted-`c` endpoint is unavailable and position charts here use the fixed `c = 0` arm. All three numerical fits for one offer ran in 0.94–2.32 s wall on the host, including namespace launch. [Support and timing](support.json) · [Per-offer scores](scores.csv) · [Blinded requests and exact source policy](input-lock.json)

Each block used the latest owner-declared orbital element that both had an earlier physical epoch and was already cited in a genuine owner observation by the six-hour cutoff. Checked against the owners’ acquisition-linked elements, only 1,240 of 23,049 retained measurements used the exact same TLE as the publisher had used to attribute them. Independent IAU SatChecker collection histories additionally corroborate the exact CelesTrak bytes before cutoff for 19 of 24 selected block-orbit choices. Every orbit in three day pairs was independently corroborated. Five choices in the April 1 pair retain earlier-use authority from the original measurement logs only. The independent lookup took place after prediction and changed no solver input. TLE epoch, documented earlier use, and independent historical archive collection are recorded as separate evidence. ## Reproduction

The frozen program is `python -m tools.pipeline_eval.qualification.external_orbcomm_trial`. Use a new `--out` with `PROTOCOL.md`, the Dataverse v2 JSON response, and original `measurements.csv` and `tles.csv` in `--source`. Run `prepare`, `solve`, then `score` with the same ext4 work directory. The numerical worker sees only the fixed packet and one offered tile. The scorer releases the station and offer labels after the prediction lock. The separate availability program verifies the two published station metadata objects and reproduces byte-level joins with the six IAU snapshot bodies.
