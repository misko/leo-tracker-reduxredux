# Portable v5 competing-region protocol

The first held-Doppler gate published at both omitted historical tiles. The competing-region test uses these examples for development. Its prospective evidence consists of one independently drawn containing offer, its corresponding omitted offer and a within-lane shuffle. The source binds before generating that new draw. The two original sites' four offers replay as a labeled historical diagnostic.

The receiver uses the same native observations, partition, causal catalogue, candidate orbit set, training shape model and withheld observations as the first gate. It trains the best inside solution with the 25-point grid and two refinements. It trains an independently selected outside solution using a staggered grid with the offered tile's width as its step. Coarse points lie within four supplied-tile widths of the center per axis. The two strongest starts each receive four local refinements. Refinements stay outside the offered tile. The halo uses the same training objective as the tile and the same offer-screened orbit bank. It does not extrapolate the orbit interpolation beyond the causal bank's time margin.

Both trained solutions predict the same held validation arcs. The per-sample Doppler comparison mixes an 80% Gaussian component at the fixed 150 Hz noise scale and 20% uniform alias noise. Average log signal-to-null ratios per arc, keep the strongest positive score per proposed orbit, and give unselected tracks the neutral value. In addition to the original diversity requirements, require the inside score to exceed the training-selected outside score by log(20). The score is a composite over dependent Doppler measurements. The margin is not a calibrated statistical confidence threshold. The outside hypothesis can veto the containing offer. It never supplies a published or forced position. This wide but finite halo restricts inference to the offered region and its nearby alternatives.

All four historical offer packets retain their input hashes. The fresh site uses the same archive collection and fixed simulator protocol, with different random draws. Run one worker per CPU on 10 and 11, use 195 s capped candidate runtimes and preserve all timeouts and other failures. Score only after binding predictions. The original primary and retries remain separate.
