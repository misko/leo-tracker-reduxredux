# Can two points on the same Doppler track predict the next pilot epoch?

The raw detector estimates a frame start modulo 1/750 s as well as a carrier frequency. A common sample-clock rate or a constant transmitter clock error gives a linear change in the received epoch. The change in propagation delay per second is minus Doppler divided by the RF carrier. Test both facts on the winning waveform peaks that the existing frequency-only Graph already grouped into tracks. Graph uses the complete recording to form those groups. This is an optimistic test of timing continuity conditional on track membership.

Use the two previously diagnosed waveforms DS26-0036 and DS29-0082 and the two prespecified confirmations DS26-0237 and DS29-0174. Use every Graph track from both the archived capture-era and the freshly measured four-start wide detector. Verify input SHA-256, receiver, channel, physical RF, sample counter, consecutive Graph times and observation IDs. Exclude geographic truth, orbit labels and any position fit from this timing test. Keep both detector sources and the two groups of recordings separate.

Take three consecutive points of a single track. Each interval must be positive and at most 2.6 s, with the second between half and twice the first. A first-interval sample-rate error within 50 ppm is the sole historical timing gate. Count all eligible triples and the subset passing that gate, including failures of the third-point forecast. Compute counter differences before conversion to floating point. Carry the first interval's wrapped epoch slope into the second. In the physical variant forecast the change in mean track Doppler from the slope between the two earlier frequencies and the separation of the interval midpoints. Subtract this change divided by the actual RF, multiplied by sample rate and the second interval. Compare with the same extrapolation without the Doppler term. Neither forecast reads the third frequency or the third local epoch.

Use the actual winner on the third probe for scoring. As a negative control rotate just the third point's measured local pilot epoch among other eligible targets from the same recording, receiver, channel, RF and 30 s counter block, sorting by time and shifting by half the group size. Preserve counters, forecasts and all input histories. Singletons have no matched shuffle and are reported separately. Score absolute circular errors at one, two and twenty samples. The primary comparison is within two samples on the identical set with a permutation partner, among candidates admitted by their history. Report the full eligibility and history-gate denominators as well as the primary rate, split by recording and detector. Treat the projected and control estimates as paired on each target.

A later position trial using this timing rule is warranted only if the Doppler-corrected predictor covers at least 25% of the eligible targets after the history gate, lands within two samples on at least half those predictions, and exceeds the matched shuffled rate by at least ten percentage points on each confirmation waveform in the wide detector. This marks a candidate acquisition aid on this roof. It cannot measure a position, establish continuous real-time tracking or validate the receiver at another site.
