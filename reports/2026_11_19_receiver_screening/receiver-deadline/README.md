# Which pilot measurements reach the position solver in time?

The original GLRT gives the compact solver enough information to match v5 closely on the roof benchmark. Here we replayed two five-minute recordings at their measured arrival times and gave a fast four-region detector and the original detector one host core each. The position solver could use only packets that had arrived by the recording's last scheduled visit. It then ran on a separate measured clock. On DS26-0237 the fast and original detectors delivered 6,275 and 593 visits by close, out of 6,766 valid. On DS29-0082 they delivered 4,088 and 592, out of 4,868 valid. Every emitted two-antenna packet matched its independently verified full-waveform counterpart. [Acquisition clocks and source charges](parallel-acquisition/manifest.json) · [Position comparisons](parallel-position/README.md)

Filling a missed antenna from the original detector cut fitted error from 0.534 to 0.268 km on DS26 and from 3.480 to 2.329 km on DS29. Original no-repair v5 gave 0.271 and 0.517 km respectively. Only DS26 met the +10% allowance. Requiring a reply within 20 ms per visit gave 1.136 and 5.189 km. The original worker alone withheld on both paced recordings. [Both calibration arms and all four original v5 options](parallel-position/charts/index.html) · [Time to a usable position](parallel-position/timing.csv)

The exact optimization and the acquisition schedules answer different questions. A simpler row-wise peak selector preserved all detector candidates on 16 real two-antenna windows from four recordings. Across five repeated measurements per window and method it reduced median complete detector CPU by 17.9–19.8% relative to the preceding FFT-optimized original detector. Reusing another 11.2 MB of fine FFT buffers gave changes between a 0.8% slowdown and a 3.4% speedup. [Held waveform timings, parity, and both variants](prepared-hotpath/README.md)

The saved-I/Q receiver now accepts `--peak-selector row-wise`. On a matched DS29-0082 chunk both implementations reproduced all 442 ordered candidates and 77 passing gates. The new selector used 33.93 detector CPU seconds against 37.50. The complete waveform comparison has its own duration and position scores. [Receiver command and chunk receipt](receiver-command/README.md) · [Whole-recording replay](selector-waveform/README.md)

Across the entire DS26-0237 recording the row-wise selector reproduced all 60,306 ordered candidate records. With eight host workers it used 2,478 detector CPU seconds, down from 2,915, a 15.0% saving. Median detector CPU per valid stereo visit fell from 372 to 315 ms. The earlier and new raw processing clocks measured 483.7 and 458.6 s on a shared host. All 6,766 valid visits exceeded the 20 ms target. From raw launch through the compact position, elapsed time fell from 540.8 to 514.1 s for the five-minute recording. The zero- and fitted-calibration fixes were identical to the preceding receiver, with errors of 0.353 and 0.262 km. [Full waveform, both positions, and latency tails](selector-waveform/README.md)

We also moved both original search squares 30 km east, leaving a 5 km gap between each correct and wrong square. The early rule withheld both wrong offers. Its full-recording fallback then published on both, with fitted errors of 12.485 and 12.543 km. A receiver that falls back this way needs a final decision about whether the true position is inside its offered region. [Genuinely omitted regions and both calibration arms](sequential-fix/omitted/charts/index.html)

At a separate UK receiver we tested whether short Doppler ridges in measured Starlink beacon spectra could identify the correct region. Across four held half-hour windows the algorithm withheld all four correct-region offers and all four omitted-region offers. Three windows yielded enough evidence to rank locations. Their unrestricted guesses missed by 44, 230 and 555 km. Shifting the observation times gave comparable frequency residuals. The source supplies one receiver and one RF band. ## Reproduce the comparisons

```sh
MPLBACKEND=Agg .venv/bin/python -m tools.pipeline_eval benchmark \
  --receiver receiver-deadline --out /var/tmp/receiver-deadline
```
