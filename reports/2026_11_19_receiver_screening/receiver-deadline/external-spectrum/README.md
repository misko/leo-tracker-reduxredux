# Position from passive power scans at a different station

The Watson archive tests whether a receiver can infer geography at a physically independent installation from short, unlabeled Doppler ridges. The estimator withheld all four 30-minute offers containing the station and all four offers omitting it. Three windows supplied 12 measured ridges. Their best broad-grid positions missed the reference by 43.9, 230.4, and 554.7 km. The fourth window had zero usable ridges. ## Source and measurement boundary

Robert A. Watson recorded March 2023 data in Altrincham, UK. The passive setup used a level cylindrical transit antenna with its long axis north–south. The author reports an approximately 40° wide beam in one direction. The receiver logs one-second `rtl_power` scans at a nominal 11.325 GHz radio-frequency center. Row headers contain the UTC date and time, intermediate-frequency bounds, bin step and integration count. The measurement is power in dB. The tested rows have 1,024 powers and a duplicated edge value, which the reader drops. The data contain neither measured arrival angles nor hardware clock calibration. The antenna variant uses a stated 18° north-component bound as a modeling assumption. The principal fit uses a 5° visibility mask and no beam geometry. [Watson paper](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1743921324001042) · [Acquisition command](https://github.com/DrWhatson/Starlink_Monitoring_station/blob/main/launch_SDRs.py) · [Release format](https://zenodo.org/records/8400658/files/readme.txt?download=1)

The release stores the preceding night's CelesTrak group downloads. The author’s download script requests the complete Starlink and OneWeb groups without a site argument. All 3,780 Starlink records on March 14 and all 3,820 on March 23 met the epoch cutoff. The evaluator matched visibility at every proposed location. Its archive inputs include only the two group TLE files and two preselected Kerberos CSVs per day. It never reads the precomputed transits or labeled plots. A dedicated scorer uses the WGS84 station coordinate declared in the author’s source after the predictions and tile offers are locked. That coordinate has no independently published survey. [Catalogue download script](https://github.com/DrWhatson/Starlink_Monitoring_station/blob/main/bin/get_TLEs) · [Station reference](https://github.com/DrWhatson/Starlink_Monitoring_station/blob/main/bin/get_Transits) · [Byte-range manifest](source.json)

The March 16 development windows supplied zero and five ridges, so the clock policy fixed the nominal zero offset and left clock calibration unresolved. The held windows supplied 7,200 spectra. Two March 2 control windows supplied a further 3,600 spectra on a recorded 914.5–915.5 MHz intermediate-frequency grid. They replayed through the same shape-only logic as an off-frequency control. The earlier March 11 archive literature reported an estimated time shift of +19 seconds in one setting and −5 or +22 seconds in another. Those experiments used several hours at once and a city-level location for initial association. This experiment held separate days and avoided importing a city coordinate or estimated shift. An earlier local test at the same station found zero qualifying March 11 beacon combs. The current ridges use individual power peaks and a different set of days. [Clock-shift study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02377) · [Clock-estimation study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01021) · [Earlier local test](../../../2026_11_18_blind_regional_receiver/field2_private/provenance.md)

The result is limited to the tested power-scan representation, one site and four held windows. No IQ phase, pilot correlation, decoder output, surveyed bearing, or per-RF calibration enters the search. One RF center leaves the RF-dependent calibration coefficient confounded with the fitted frequency offsets. The broad search uses a UK-area prior. It supplies no worldwide or second-station performance estimate.

Two other public measurement candidates have different evaluation boundaries. The University of Texas at Austin release supplies decoded frames and a short wideband excerpt from one associated satellite. It can check signal acquisition but offers little independent multi-pass position evidence. The Australian EDA2 release supplies genuine measured directions, but the authors accepted candidates only within a small window around site-specific orbital predictions. The selected table therefore incorporates geographic information even if a later reader drops the reference and label columns. [Austin release](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21404931) · [EDA2 selection method](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202554787) · [EDA2 table](https://zenodo.org/records/15089853)

## Reproduce from the range manifest

Watson under CC BY 4.0. `source.json` records SHA-256 for the compressed ranges and extracted members, and the prediction lock binds the retained excerpt and its decompressed digest. [Source release](https://zenodo.org/records/8400658) · [Retained real power scans](first-minute-230314-0600.csv.gz) · [Extracted ridges](ridges.json.gz)

From the repository root, use a new local input directory and an empty output directory containing the published `PROTOCOL.md`. The fetcher requests only ZIP directory records, local member headers, and the precise byte ranges for the selected CSVs and TLEs. It validates every HTTP range, ZIP member length and CRC. The prediction step checks the input digests. Run geographic scoring after the prediction step writes its lock.
