# Finding a missing pilot from raw samples

On sixteen recorded visits where the standard wide GLRT missed a capture-era pilot, a timing estimate from **two adjacent pilot symbols** recovered five. On eight later, disjoint visits it recovered two more. All seven became the native detector’s winning candidate with absolute carrier frequency within 44 Hz of the original measurement. The altered detector also kept all eight previously matched controls. This is a deliberately enriched sample of known misses on one roof. Complete recordings and absolute position accuracy use their own denominators below. [Raw-I/Q evidence and timing](differential-pilot/README.md) · [Individual modification charts](index.html) · [Offline chart pack](modification-charts.zip)

At 10 MS/s the pilot’s symbols are 44 samples apart. Multiplying a sample by the conjugate of the one 44 samples earlier leaves the same carrier-induced phase rotation across the known symbol products. Correlating those products with the pilot code locates the start even when the receiver does not yet know the frequency. This also works when only one frame contains the pilot. The previous three-frame estimator recovered none of the original sixteen misses. A direct replay of the original capture detector explains why its first coarse guess is insufficient. Nine misses surfaced only in its second, deeper search, and just one of sixteen was recovered by passing the top initial guess to wide GLRT. ## Did the recovered pilots improve position?

Keeping the compact solver fixed, the new timing hint improved both recordings used in development and worsened both fresh recordings. All four runs started at the saved waveform and returned positions. Each cell below is horizontal error in kilometres, with `c = 0` first and fitted `c` second. The four-recording fitted median went from **0.765 to 1.248 km** with the timing hint. At both the 5% and 10% thresholds, each method equalled or beat the v5 allowance on two of the four recordings. With zero calibration the 10% count went from three to two, and both met 5% on two. The native FFTW and original NumPy implementations produced byte-identical detector outputs on both fresh recordings. The native full runs took 146 and 185 seconds, and 5,555/6,774 and 50/6,756 detector calls met the 20 ms deadline. Core assignments and load differed across implementations. Across those two fresh recordings, 250 of the 802 newly positive probes matched a gated pilot in the original detector. Counting all 8,514 originally positive probes, the number of winning pilots aligned in time and frequency rose from 7,730 to 7,987. On the same recordings the fitted frequency residual rose from 70.87 to 84.76 Hz and 95.48 to 98.59 Hz. A separate rule kept every original stock positive and used the hint only to rescue negatives. It gave fitted position errors of 1.473 and 0.797 km. Both meet the historical v5 target, but each stock result on the same input was closer. The tracker reassigned some original measurements when the new ones arrived. We also refit the position using just the additional pilots corroborated by the archived capture detector. Fitted errors on DS26 and DS29 went from 0.796 and 0.734 km to 0.841 and 0.950 km. Feeding just the remaining candidates gave 0.903 and 0.981 km. Combining the two groups hurt DS26 more than either group alone and gave DS29 a better result than either group alone. On DS26, the combined data also changed whether the solver attempted its final recovery step. This retrospective experiment used archive information unavailable to a running receiver. ## Can an independently simulated receiver recover its site?

Two synthetic sites about 15,746 km apart now have original no-repair v5 and compact results on the **same** native GLRT output from generated complex waveform samples. The second site used revised transmitter orbits for all twelve emitters, signal gaps and a separately generated interferer. Its predictions from all three position programs were sealed before unblinding. These are regional, 25 km offers. The two roof datasets used for the recorded-I/Q acquisition comparisons share one physical installation. [Original and compact, both sites](waveform-v5/README.md) · [Second site and simulation limits](waveform-holdout/README.md)

| Containing region | Original v5, zero / fitted error | Compact, zero / fitted error |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| First synthetic site | 1.033 / 0.764 km | 1.017 / 0.751 km |
| Second synthetic site | 0.642 / 1.086 km | 0.650 / 1.093 km |

Both ordinary receivers issued six false publications across the three omitted offers and two calibrations. On the second site the fixed 20 m boundary rule retained both valid arms and withheld all four omitted arms, including the far offer where the unguarded position missed by 44.255 km. On its containing offer, fitting the calibration reduced the compact receiver’s conditional RF RMS from 17.213 to 17.199 Hz while increasing position error by 0.442 km. Original v5 took 73.5–154.0 s from factors to a position under a 3 GiB cap. Compact took 20.2–21.9 s under 512 MiB. These are host measurements.
