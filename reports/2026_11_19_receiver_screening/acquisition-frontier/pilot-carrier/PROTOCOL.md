# Carrier frequency from the differential timing measurement

At 10 MS/s a 44-sample lag accumulates one cycle per 227.273 kHz, coinciding with the detector’s pilot-frequency ambiguity. At a nominated start, take the argument of the known pilot’s complex correlation before the timing scout squares its magnitude. This estimates frequency modulo that interval. To choose among aliases within the stock ±800 kHz band, use a second 4-sample lag at that same epoch, with an unaliased interval of ±1.25 MHz. Choose the long-lag alternative nearest the short-lag frequency. This comparison does not narrow the native search.

Use the same 30 MS/s Qin reference samples, three fractional-frame phases, local support and known-pilot code as the adjacent-symbol scout. Subtract the full-window mean product in each lag. Use all samples for the 44-sample phase, and every fourth supported ADC sample for the short lag. Evaluate the already frozen top four timing centres from actual raw IQ on the designated receiver, plus the first centre shifted by half a nominal frame. The primary estimate uses the top centre only. All other ranked centres are diagnostics. The two lag lengths, weights, frequency signs and alias-choice rule are fixed before scoring held radio data.

First test the formula on both signed pilot sidebands, strong and weak pilots, alternating frame signs and a single live frame. Include noise, tones and zero samples. Then make a prediction for every scheduled visit in the parent’s 24-window primary and eight-window confirmation panels. Verify the raw digest, sample clock and frozen timing nominations. Save the estimates before comparing them with capture-era pilot frequencies. For zero input a missing phase gives no carrier estimate. Noise or a tone can have a numerical phase; the existing GLRT gate is the independent test for signal presence.

Report all denominators and the subset whose nominated integer epoch is within two circular samples of the capture-era pilot. Score 1, 10 and 40 kHz tolerances for the modulo and unfolded frequency, signed median and absolute errors, and the fraction covered by the nearest original 80 kHz grid bin or its two adjacent bins. Give the half-frame control the same error calculation. This analysis measures frequency information in raw samples at the proposed epoch and leaves geographic position unmeasured.
