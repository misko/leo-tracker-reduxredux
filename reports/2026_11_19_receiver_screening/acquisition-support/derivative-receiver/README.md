## Derivative and identity model

The native entry point uses the frozen orbit-agnostic chronological linker with the original raw held and guard intervals excluded. Within each linked arc, cubic fits turn actual elapsed sample times and measured CFO into second-derivative coefficients in Hz/s² at two times. The projected covariance includes a 95 Hz noise floor and an inflation factor of two. For each arc, five initial tile locations nominate at most 16 causal orbit IDs. The exact geometric search evaluates a 5-by-5 WGS84 regional grid and seven prospective orbit phase nodes. A normalized phase likelihood scores each orbit on early derivatives. Its committed phase predicts separate late arc derivatives and competes against three same-site orbital alternatives. The late test is conditional on the earlier anonymous track linking. [Frozen protocol and gates](PROTOCOL.md) · [Full arc and orbit diagnostics](positions.jsonl.gz)

The candidate additionally compares the **slope of the CFO difference** for two arcs with identical timestamps, receiver ID, channel and actual RF. This preserves an RF shape term even in the presence of a shared linear oscillator error. The support stays disjoint across pairs. At each grid point the pair considers the four strongest identities for either member and requires distinct satellites. A half-weighted standardized differential loss keeps the likelihood composite. The control uses exactly the same inputs and grid with independent curvature alone. The earlier shape-grid minimized centered full-curve mismatch, independent arcs decoded per-orbit native peak paths, and the blind-track clock method predicted arcs using a provisional clock from other noncoincident tracks. Here the independent feature projects drift exactly and the added slope exists only with physically coincident equal-RF data.
