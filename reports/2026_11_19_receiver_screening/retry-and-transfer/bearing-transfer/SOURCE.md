# StarLoc channel-phase provenance

All eleven previously staged `tracked_tones.mat` files matched the digests in the sealed field release. Their `main_tone_dict` cells contain 371,441 rows across 81 prepared tracks and 156,663 distinct within-capture timestamps. Each row retains three nonzero-valued complex FFT channels where our extractor admits it. Columns one and two supply measured elapsed time and Hz. Columns three through five supply the three complex FFT samples. The pinned guide calls these phases. The authors' extraction stores the complex transform results, and their reproduction takes `angle()` on those columns. The public processing describes an earlier satellite-window split and harmonic tracking. The window selection procedure remains undocumented in the staged release. The authors used a 13.25 cm nominal horizontal L-shaped array at 11.325 GHz with a shared clock across SDRs and LNBs. Their data reproduction estimates raw RX0–RX1 and RX1–RX2 phase, subtracts a reference injected downstream of the LNBs, and separately applies on-air phase from the known receiver coordinates and first identified satellite. Its orientation comes from the capture's auxiliary attitude log, with zero pitch and roll in the released processing helper. Our model adopts the published physical baseline lengths and orthogonality. It estimates an independent unknown yaw for each offer from training phase increments. Endpoint differences remove a constant phase per baseline without reading the on-air offsets, auxiliary attitude, or reference position. The eleven small `calibration.mat` files each supply exactly three arrays, `ref_sig_idx`, `phase_offset_01` and `phase_offset_21`. The 83- or 163-reference recordings have median reference intervals of 10.63 or 5.42 s. Unwrapped wire phase changes over an entire capture range from 0.17 to 0.65 rad on RX0–RX1 and 0.01 to 0.04 rad on RX2–RX1. This inventory establishes that time-varying instrument phase cannot be assumed zero. The raw-phase workers do not consume either the wire corrections or any survey-assisted calibration. The author-ID ceiling provides the separately stored categorical labels only.
