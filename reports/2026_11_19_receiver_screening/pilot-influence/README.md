# Which pilot measurements are worth computing?

The experiments trace changes from the antenna samples through the GLRT pilot search to position. DS26 and DS29 use the same roof. Each roof figure compares the original four v5 options on the same recording IDs. The two calibration views use identical observations, candidates, priors and search budgets within each receiver. ## Receiver choices on the roof

Using four timing regions on only one antenna per valid visit halved the detector time in a held host test. Both antennas are installed and the primary alternates independently at each RF. In the full 22-recording comparison, this receiver met the individual +10% allowance on eight recordings. Keeping both antennas met it on thirteen with fitted calibration. Both settings returned all 22 fixes. Two separate full waveform runs gave identical native measurements and geographic endpoints to the independently reconstructed one-antenna streams. [Position distributions](one-lane-benchmark/README.md) · [Whole measured runs](one-lane-whole/README.md) · [Single-channel CPU clocks](one-lane-arm/README.md)

To check whether the half-antenna result came from the faster GLRT itself, we applied the same antenna schedule to original capture-era measurements and ran the position solver again. Among six known difficult recordings, thinning the original data worsened all six positions in both calibration settings. It improved both comparison recordings. The original two-antenna detector and the fast two-antenna detector therefore both carry useful second-antenna observations. [Matched detector and antenna comparison](one-lane-capture/README.md)

The host benchmark joins each fix to the duration of the raw replay that produced it. Full four-region replays finished within the recording span on all 22, and 13 also met their individual v5 accuracy allowance. Five-region replays finished within the span on 20 of 22, and 13 met both criteria with fitted calibration. The two directly timed alternating-fifth replays missed the span despite accurate fixes. [Per-recording joint outcomes](one-lane-benchmark/accuracy-and-time.csv) · [Time versus position chart](one-lane-benchmark/budget.png)

Checking the partner antenna only when the primary missed preserved every one of the 63,982 visits with a stock detection. It also dropped 31,323 detections where both antennas had succeeded. On 22 recordings it scheduled 16.3% fewer antenna calls and raised the number within 10% of original v5 from eight for one antenna to eleven. Both antennas on every visit reached thirteen. The conditional receiver’s median / P95 position error was 0.867 / 1.755 km with zero calibration and 0.893 / 1.752 km with fitted calibration. Its median conditional frequency residual was 90.90 and 90.82 Hz respectively. Two independent full-waveform runs reproduced the measured input bytes and all four geographic and RF results. Their median detector times of 24.9 and 25.7 ms exceeded the 20 ms visit budget. [All 22 paired positions](second-on-miss-roster/README.md) · [Two measured raw replays](second-on-miss-whole/README.md)

To find out which partner detections helped, we compared all four choices on the same six difficult recordings and two controls. Adding the partner after a primary **hit** improved seven of eight geographic fixes over the primary alone in both calibration settings. The median paired improvement was 220 m at zero calibration and 246 m with fitted calibration. Adding the partner after a **miss** as well worsened all six difficult cases. The fitted medians on these eight were 0.890 km for primary alone, 0.758 km for partner after a hit, 1.141 km for partner after a miss, and 0.814 km for both antennas throughout. These are real solver runs on reconstructed detector inputs. The work counts are scheduled antenna calls, and the eight recordings were selected to diagnose errors. [Four-way distributions, work and individual exceptions](partner-on-hit/README.md)

In four recorded-pace host runs on the same DS29 waveform, both execution orders favored four search regions for queue coverage and an alternating fifth region for position. In the pair with all buffers available on time, four regions emitted 6,601 of 6,704 valid visits and gave a fitted error of 0.884 km. The alternating fifth emitted 6,327 and gave 0.471 km. An earlier run on that waveform, using the original compressed source, had favored four regions for position. The emitted visit lists differ across runs. [Paced positions, drops and matched per-visit CPU](paced-controlled/README.md)

The standalone x86-64 receiver now accepts `--detector second-on-miss`. Its compressed package grows by 2,756 bytes from stock and reuses the same three native libraries. In a fresh installation it reproduced detector packets and both position estimates on three complete recordings spanning both pilot edges. The three host replays took 131–164 s under 512 MiB with zero swap. The current 10 MS/s workload still lacks a measured run on physical ARM hardware. In a complete DS26 replay, skipping the dense grid writes saved 6.7 host CPU seconds and produced identical packets on all 6,509 visits emitted by both runs. The optimized run dropped 134 visits, and the dense control dropped 118. This single run order supports the CPU saving. Its 20 ms deadline coverage and P95 emission latency both worsened. ## Geographic transfer

At eight measured StarLoc locations, the complete Doppler validation rule withheld on all 24 omitted offers and all eight containing offers. Keeping only a 1 km interior check published all eight containing offers at a median error of 4.07 km. It also published on two of eight near-face omissions. Each test used three fixed geographic decoys per site. Their symmetric placement gives another method a geographic cue. The frozen receiver ranked tiles using the Doppler measurements. Four sites had been used in earlier development. Each recording has one beacon frequency, which leaves fitted RF calibration unidentifiable. [Independent locations, new offers and withholding](independent-site/README.md)

Another receiver started from anonymous simultaneous GLRT detections and causal orbits. On two roof recordings the proposed physical delay sign failed on all 42 held pieces. An independently measured relative receiver-clock slope was usable in the lower band at both, yet the bounded geometry fits withheld on all six real and simulated offers.
