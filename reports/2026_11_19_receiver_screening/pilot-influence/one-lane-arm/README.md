# One physical receiver per visit

On 12 held dual-IQ windows from two recordings, selecting one physical receiver roughly halved total detector CPU. Native x86 median wall fell from 16.40 to 8.15 ms with the current packing adapter and to 7.90 ms with direct strided input. The one-lane methods completed all 24 x86 replay calls within 20 ms, versus 23/24 for dual stock. Cortex-A53 QEMU showed a similar ratio in CPU and completed zero calls within 20 ms. Physical timing for this 10 MS/s workload remains unmeasured.

## Exact detector and packet boundary

The same stock wide-frequency fractional detector ran on the same interleaved dual CI16 IQ in all three modes. `packed` extracts only the chosen physical receiver and invokes the existing one-receiver entry. `strided` directly prepares that physical lane from the dual buffer with the existing single-lane allocator, and invokes the same physical-lane proposal folded from the interleaved input. It computes exactly one lane and makes no 800,000-byte packed copy. Both retain two output slots. The actual native row remaps logical receiver 0 to the chosen physical antenna, and the companion has the corresponding physical ID, original probe clock and an empty candidate list. Both packed and strided still read the original 1,600,000 bytes per visit. The downstream loss of the skipped lane's factors is evaluated in the separate positioning study.

The selected lane comes from the recorder's valid visits by RF center. It alternates independently on each of eight frequencies, starts with receiver 0, and advances only on valid visits. The benchmark reconstructs the schedule from original visit zero, independently of subset selection, wall clock, CPU counters or candidate outcomes. Held visits were 12–17 from DS26-0197 lower and DS29-0316 upper. Difficult visits are DS26-0197 4 and 92, and DS29-0316 39 and 77, selected from a fixed early prefix because the physical stock lane had no positive whereas the previous independent fifth-center archive did. These four stress inputs and both-edge signed/null controls are outside the timing medians. [Frozen protocol](PROTOCOL.md) · [Input clocks, raw digests and selected physical IDs](input-lock.json)

Across both architectures and repeats, each of 192 one-lane runs matched the same-architecture stock selected row byte-for-byte with diagnostic timing excluded. This includes emitted candidates, signed carrier and fractional epoch, gate scores, proposal status and deterministic work counters. The omitted row stayed empty at its original probe clock. In all 24 partner-zero checks, replacing the skipped receiver's waveform with zeros left the selected output unchanged. Dual stock reproduced all 32 held-plus-hard archived two-lane candidate packets on the host. The exact installed whole-run library, using the same Python single-lane producer adapter, matched all 24 corresponding decoded native rows. C and Python printed integral doubles differently in 22 of those rows. The zero, signed strong and one-frame controls reproduced the original decisions for each selected lane. ## Cost per held visit

The C replay ran both detector architectures on host CPU 7 with one calling thread and a separate persistent context per mode. Total includes staging, native detection and physical-ID remapping. Reads, context setup and JSON formatting have separate accounting and are outside the total below. There are 23 warm calls and 24 calls including cold starts per mode. RSS is the larger of the two corresponding process high-water values, inclusive of the harness and QEMU overhead where applicable.

Strided input removed the intermediate copy and 936 KiB of peak RSS on x86, with 0.12 ms median CPU saved over the packed one-lane path. The QEMU median difference was 0.64 ms CPU and 0.35 ms additional wall. The packed lower-edge emulator process had 4.16 s CPU over 5.88 s wall. Its slower tail reflects time outside CPU and gives no evidence of a large strided-kernel speedup.
