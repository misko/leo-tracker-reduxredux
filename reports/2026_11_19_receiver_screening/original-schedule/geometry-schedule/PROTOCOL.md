# Spend original detector calls on projected geographic information

Use the same six held recordings and matched full fast and original compact endpoints as adaptive visits. Use DS26-0036 and DS29-0174 to choose one threshold. Preserve both calibration arms and all four original v5 reference roles. The selector receives a truth-free offered square, exact recording clocks, complete fast factors, and the latest authenticated TLE snapshot already available when the recording starts. Do not use original factors, held locations, future fast factors, or published result coordinates to select slots.

Within each 30-second block, associate anonymous RX0 fast winners into short tracks by folded CFO and circular absolute frame phase. Keep at most three tracks per physical RF with at least five points and five seconds of support. Keep the most recent 24 points of each. At the offered center, match each track against orbital Doppler from its preceding block. Fit a carrier offset and a linear clock nuisance per track and candidate. Penalize large implied clock slopes. Weight candidate satellites by prediction shape and retain the five largest weights with their original normalization. Require orbital visibility throughout the fitted track. Extrapolate each hypothesis with its frozen nuisance fit into the next block. Once the block has started, its new fast observations are available for the following block only.

Compute analytic east/north Doppler gradients. Project the same constant and linear nuisance columns out of each candidate's past gradients. Extrapolate that projection into the next block and average the candidate gradients with their original posterior weights. Set the information matrix from the past projected rows plus a 25 km isotropic ridge. Rank the available RF/time slots by the log determinant gain of the predicted measurement using a residual noise floor. This estimates a local marginal improvement in geometry for a plausible tracked observation. It is a forecast based on uncertain associations.

Apply the same hard per-RF token rule as adaptive visits. Earn one token at each 16th valid same-RF stereo visit. Spend an available token at the first above-threshold forecast, or at its expiry. Forecasts use a one-second deterministic time grid and never inspect actual future visits. The capture clock closes after 300 seconds. A block or RF with no trained hypothesis uses token expiry. The hash control matches every spent RF/token window and selects independently within that window. Both methods replace exactly the selected whole stereo packets with authenticated original outputs.

On development, compare score quantiles 0.50, 0.75 and 0.90 of the forecast-only time grid per RF/block. To measure held local discrimination, compare the current RX0 fast winner with both the preceding-block forecast and a 30 km east-displaced offer. Both use the same candidate satellite identities and their omitted-observation nuisance fits. Convert paired residuals into their signed absolute advantage, clip it at 3 kHz, and assign zero when neither forecast is within 3 kHz or the held winner is absent. Pick the quantile that maximizes mean signed advantage over the hash control on the two development recordings. Tie-break to the higher quantile. Evaluate the analogous held score only after the policy's slots are locked. Positive advantage favors the nominal offered center at this forecast boundary, before site truth is opened.

Run both policies through separate 150-second compact services using the same controls, memory, priorities, catalogue and region. Preserve terminal misses and timeouts. Measure selector CPU, wall and association/projection work on archived factor inputs. Compare modeled detector work including all fast visits and selected original calls.
