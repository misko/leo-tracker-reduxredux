# Pilot timing, processor load and position error

The receiver analyses 20 ms radio windows from two antennas. The hardware delivered successive windows about 43.5 ms apart on the two original test recordings. A receiver that adds an extra timing search can find more signals, but it can also lose later windows while the processor is occupied. These comparisons follow the saved radio samples, the actual admission decision and the resulting position. The position charts count every offered recording and give separate results for zero and fitted RF calibration. ## Vary the search when the worker is busy

The new recordings also ran at their original arrival times, with the order of the two policies reversed between recordings. Adaptive search lowered fitted error from 0.717 to 0.553 km on DS26-0026 and from 0.528 to 0.489 km on DS29-0047 relative to always searching five regions. Total drops were nearly equal across the pair (555 adaptive, 553 fixed), and each recording lost more visits during its first run. The complete four-region control that processed every valid visit gave 0.553 and 0.355 km. Each policy beat original v5 on both fresh recordings with either calibration. [Fresh position distributions](fresh-adaptive/charts/fitted_c/fresh-paced-adaptive.png) · [Matched zero-calibration distributions](fresh-adaptive/charts/c0/fresh-paced-adaptive.png) · [Arrival times, CPU and frequency-fit results](fresh-adaptive/README.md)

In real-time replays of DS26-0197 and DS29-0316, a worker that varied the search region count returned both positions. Its fitted errors were 1.058 and 0.948 km. Fixed four-region search gave 0.970 and 0.704 km, and fixed five-region search gave 1.113 and 0.202 km. All three beat original v5 on DS29 and missed the 10% allowance on DS26. On each core the first policy run lost the most visits. [Positions and host timing](adaptive-arrival/README.md) · [Error distribution](adaptive-arrival/position-charts/fitted_c/adaptive-arrival-position.png)

The adaptive rule searches five timing regions when idle and four when an earlier visit is still outstanding. On the original two recordings, a controlled model held the arrival clocks fixed and gave every call 2.25 times its measured native CPU duration. Four regions and the adaptive rule each retained every valid visit. Five regions lost 64 DS26 and 74 DS29 visits. The actual position receiver then fitted the authentic GLRT packets selected by those schedules. Its fitted errors for four regions, adaptive search, and five regions were 0.992, 0.917 and 1.205 km on DS26, and 0.852, 0.315 and 0.235 km on DS29. These are position fits on a modeled workload. ## Measurements from another receiver

The stock detector recovered the authors’ frame timing in six blind edge/window searches on the University of Texas 50 ms recording. The largest disagreement was 67 ns. Two disjoint time windows were measured on both frequency edges, followed by one overlapping window on each edge. The excerpt is from the same researchers who described the pilot template. It tests pilot timing on another recording system. A geographic position would require additional signal history, surveyed receiver coordinates and a usable capture-time orbit catalogue. [Measured timing and controls](austin-waveform/README.md) · [Qin and colleagues’ paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02627)

Mixing these real signals into two independent samples of recorded roof background produced 20 controlled trials. The stock search and the fifth-region search both missed all eight targets at the two weakest power ratios and recovered all twelve at the other three. The additional search cost a median 6.10 ms per mixture. [Recovery by power, timing and frequency](austin-timing-transfer/README.md)

| Nominate pilot times by agreement between two sets of code symbols | Out of 48 candidates found by the original GLRT, the two-panel search retained 17. The ordinary four-region detector retained 42 at 25.9 ms median CPU, compared with 1,038 ms for the two-panel search. Both used the actual recorded input. Full rescoring matched the reference on all 753,102 comparable cells. [Candidate ranking, verification and measured cost](anchor-race/README.md) |
| Make the stock and fifth-region search available through one native call | On 96 real dual-receiver windows, the new x86 code reproduced all 1,530 stock and 1,533 fifth-region candidates field for field. Median fifth-region wall time fell from 35.05 to 33.68 ms. None of the 288 fifth-region calls finished in 20 ms with either implementation. [Native source, measured stage times and controls](native-hotpath/README.md) |
| Intersect Doppler constraints from different satellite passes | The from-scratch receiver found intersections inside all six offered regions. Even its best in-region pairs had 42–53 km of estimated uncertainty along their weakest axis, above the predeclared 35 km limit. All six receivers withheld their positions, including the two recorded containing offers. The computation used 1.68 CPU seconds at the median. [Containing offers, omissions and conditioning](pass-hypotheses/README.md) |
| Give the GLRT four waiting slots | On the earlier DS29 recording, four-slot stock retained every input. Four-slot fifth-region search lost 757 visits. The 95th-percentile time from scheduled arrival to a fifth-region packet was 357 ms. With four slots the fifth-region fitted error reached 0.537 km, versus 0.682 km in the earlier one-slot run and 0.218 km with all visits. Host scheduling and decoder readiness changed between runs.
