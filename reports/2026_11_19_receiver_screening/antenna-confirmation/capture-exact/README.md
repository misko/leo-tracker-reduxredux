# Faster full capture-era GLRT

Batched carrier FFTs and previously qualified compiled refinement reduced median full detector CPU from **2.17–2.50 s to 0.56–0.57 s** on the three legacy producer cases and from **0.83 s to 0.56 s** on the already-screened producer. Every complete public payload matched the installed detector in **16 held comparisons and eight control comparisons per variant**. The coarse score maps differed by up to **2.53 × 10⁻⁸** across 2,239,944 independently checked cells. Full-payload parity is measured on this panel. Near-tie dispatch on unseen waveforms remains unqualified. [Stage chart](stage.png) · [Raw clocks](stage.csv) · [Full payload audit](parity.json)

## Largest original cost

On genuine legacy windows, complete coarse correlation took 1.60–1.97 s per dual visit at the median, roughly 74–79% of detector CPU on those three cases. Individual 12-anchor receiver attempts consumed about 0.55–0.69 s. The accepted 22-anchor fallback took up to 1.20 s. The original primary and fallback sets share only two anchor symbols. Caching their final score arrays would save little. On the screened DS29-0519 producer, the original screened coarse operation including its selection took 0.48 s at the median, 58% of detector CPU. Removing the legacy coarse stage alone could only approach a 3.8–4.7× case-median speedup at these clocks if every other stage stayed fixed.

The new implementation builds one forward spectrum per original IQ block. It batches the inverse transform dispatch for every carrier at each anchor, reusing the forward transform and the common energy normalizer. It still evaluates every original coarse cell for every anchor and frame and keeps the same integer/fractional refinement and margin gates. The FFT products and inverse transforms use binary32. The per-anchor scores accumulate in binary64. Relative to the previous FP64 overlap-save implementation, this saves a factor of **1.46–1.91** in median coarse CPU here. The compiled composition also uses the existing conditioned-CFO moment bank and fused GLRT correlations, with original operations on their established fallback branches. DS29-0082 median fractional CPU fell from 229 to 33 ms and conditioned CPU from 89 to 51 ms.

## Exact emitted support on the held panel

The eight actual dual windows are separate from the earlier matched capture-runtime benchmark and include four prior hard misses of the four-region detector. All original timed probe rows reproduced the previous capture-starts authority. Each FFT variant preserved the public candidate inventory, acquisition nominees, calibrated CFO, fractional epoch, gate ordering and scores, including rejected candidate fields. Across its held and control repeats each variant maintained all 100 gate-positive candidate records. Both signed ±720 kHz planted windows matched on both physical receivers and every variant, and the zero windows emitted none. Across eight independently checked real primary grid maps, RMS grid differences were 1.36–1.38 × 10⁻⁸ and every map had some floating differences. ## Separate peak-selection check

The original Python heap still consumed 82–102 ms per held visit at case medians after the coarse replacement made those costs important. A separately frozen repeat on the same eight valid windows used the existing vectorized selector. It repeatedly finds the same maximum among supported local peaks with the original absolute-CFO and epoch tie rules and circular suppression distance. All 32 heap/vector attempts agreed with the installed held payloads and with their freshly measured counterpart. Median selection CPU fell from 87–98 ms to 16–18 ms across the four cases. Full detector CPU improved on three cases and regressed from 789 to 822 ms on DS29-0082 during that repeat, with much larger variation in unchanged fine-search CPU.
