Sample-rate versus satellite-candidate Doppler RMS, 2026-10-03

Finding
10 MS/s has lower typical held-out Doppler RMS than 2.5 MS/s in this week's
recordings. There is no clear improvement over 5 MS/s. This is an observational
association, not proof of better acquisition probability or confirmed identity.

Scope and metric
Capture window: 2026-09-26 08:58:03 through 2026-10-03 08:58:03 America/Los_Angeles
(15:58:03 UTC on both dates). Filter by capture time, not analysis creation time.
All target-rate recordings use radio_pluto_5d4d, manual 40 dB gain and nominal
120 ms dwell. No IQ was decoded or new RF collected for this review.

Of 964 recordings at the requested rates, 885 have a comparable v14 product with
tracks: 359/373 at 2.5 MS/s, 141/151 at 5 MS/s, and 385/440 at 10 MS/s. Exclusions
are 77 without a v14 product and two v14 no-trajectory products. Missing products
are not classified as failed locks. New v15 products are excluded rather than
mixed or counted twice. All included v14 products share configuration digest
sha256:f54e5ab518dae837df03eaf7a4d54b9d30807f0a5edc872f1b9351c70ec2c054.

For each reviewed track, use the training-ranked first TLE candidate and its
randomized_evaluation_rms_hz. The implementation fits constant frequency offset
and chooses satellite/time shift using the 60% training subset, then evaluates
the other 40%. This is offset-only RMS: no learned RF-dependent coefficient c
or position fit is introduced in this comparison. It is not position accuracy,
an independent future-pass validation, or proof of correct satellite identity.
Track observations and scan observations are dependent; do not treat 49,948
tracks as independent satellite passes. No eligible reviews were deferred in
these included v14 products.

The pooled median improvement of 10 over 2.5 is 13.74%; over 5 it is 1.15%.
The 10 MS/s error tail is worse, not better: 12.92% exceed or equal 200 Hz,
versus 11.18% at 2.5 and 11.90% at 5. Retained-track selection and different
track durations can influence both the median and tail.

Sensitivity checks
Within the common capture-time interval shared by all three rate cohorts,
compute one median per scan and give each scan equal weight. Medians are
116.38 Hz (159 scans, 2.5), 98.05 Hz (141 scans, 5), and 98.43 Hz (288 scans, 10).
The bootstrap ratio 10/2.5 has median 0.845 and percentile interval 0.815-0.875;
10/5 has median 1.005 and interval 0.974-1.040 (4,000 scan resamples, fixed seed).
These intervals condition on the observed scans and ignore temporal dependence
between adjacent scans; they are descriptive uncertainty, not causal bounds.

Group tracks by Pacific date, channel, edge, 10-second duration bin, and
20-observation support bin, retaining cells with at least five tracks at both
rates. The median cell-wise RMS ratio is 0.834 for 10/2.5 (480 cells) and 1.002
for 10/5 (331 cells). This supports the typical-RMS finding after coarse support
and capture-condition adjustment. It does not control signal strength or orbit
geometry completely. The 10 versus 2.5 advantage occurs at both band edges.

An attempted strict pairing on the same candidate ID/channel/edge, within
15 minutes, with duration and observation counts within a factor of two yielded
zero pairs. Consequently this review does not establish a same-satellite,
same-pass rate effect. Candidate labels remain tentative.

Conclusion
The data support about 14-17% lower typical residual RMS at 10 versus 2.5 MS/s,
with essentially the same typical RMS at 5 and 10 MS/s. They do not establish
that 10 MS/s locks more reliably, nor that the larger rate improves positioning.
A causal comparison would require matched existing IQ replay through the same
detector at all three effective bandwidths, with acquisition failures counted.
No production rate settings were changed.

Reproducibility and checks
review.py reads only published JSON metadata. summarize.py creates tracks.csv,
scans.csv, recordings.csv, summary.json, provenance.json and rms-comparison.png.
provenance.json binds each used product to its source path and SHA-256.
validation.json records nine full-manifest timestamp/rate checks (three per
rate) and all excluded-product counts. Every included recording's sample rate
was cross-checked against its tracking product. Counts and medians in tracks.csv
were independently recomputed using Python's standard-library statistics module.
No manifest parse errors occurred. The chart was visually inspected.
