## Fixed schedule, declared before any search

Each source/c case receives two policies, each capped at **600 charged work units and 60 s**, with the same complete starting vector/nuisance. The domain is the current broad regional research disk for both policies; do not silently restore or remove the original production extra-25 km disk in only one policy. Preserve any otherwise identical bounds. A work unit is one actual full objective evaluation **or** one additional explicit paired orbital-prediction/elevation projection used by the profile. Count actual objective calls and profile projections separately as well as their sum. This is a conservative transparent work convention, not a claim that their runtimes are equal.

The proposed profile policy always attempts the following two passes; no window-dependent branch or second start is allowed:

1. Construct a fresh complete-residual profile at the current incumbent: one objective evaluation and one additional current-orbit projection. Include the original timing as an eligible incumbent for every candidate.
2. Make exactly 41 coarse requests: integer timings −20,−19,…,+20 s, clipped separately to each candidate's feasible interval. Then make exactly 11 refinement requests at each candidate's own best coarse timing plus −0.5,−0.4,…,+0.5 s, also clipped. Keep the original timing eligible throughout. These are 52 vectorized requests over the entire fixed bank, without a candidate/arc cap. Record duplicate/clipped requests and cache hits; conservatively charge every requested projection if computed, and do not conceal additional projections behind a cache label.
3. Candidate feasibility uses the original full orbit-support limits and ±20 s timing bound. For a one-coordinate change Δ, also impose `−10 ≤ current_common + Δ/K ≤ 10`. Intersect these constraints before scoring; no clipping of an accepted physical vector after its score. Actual observation support times determine the orbit limits. Feasibility may use known metadata outside a scored mask, but never excluded measured CFO. Reconstruct the full gauge and verify the exact existing physical/nuisance constraints before acceptance.
4. Select the largest objective reduction over all candidates, with deterministic ties by ordered bank index and then smallest absolute timing change. Only one coordinate can be accepted in a pass. Require a predicted improvement greater than 1.0 objective unit and timing displacement greater than 0.05 s. If eligible, make one direct full-joint objective evaluation at that single-coordinate trial; require profile/direct agreement within 1e-7 and actual improvement. Otherwise retain the incumbent. No likelihood-selected q or band update is hidden in this direct check.
5. After pass one, call the existing joint-band `common.refit` with at most 150 objective calls, returning unused allowance to the remaining pool. It jointly optimizes geometry, all timings, clock, c where free, q and absolute band contrasts under the same original objective/priors. Then rebuild the second profile from this resulting complete state; never reuse stale residuals, q, visibility or candidate scores after a coordinate/calibration change.
6. After pass two, spend the remaining charged allowance on one final joint refit. Preserve the original seed and every lower-objective feasible incumbent. Report the best feasible endpoint and its unchanged strict KKT audit; do not relax tolerance, silently substitute a preferred checkpoint or add cleanup work. Intermediate refit nonstationarity is recorded and does not become a final strict-pass claim.

A completed pass costs at most **55 units**: one construction objective, one baseline projection, 52 profile projections and one direct verification objective. Two completed passes therefore leave at least 490 objective-call units: up to 150 for the intermediate refit and at least 340 for the terminal refit. At the maximal allocation, the ledger is **494 actual objective calls + 106 explicit profile projections = 600 units**. The number of vectorized column comparisons is 104K. There are no extra band-profile initialization calls: these seeds already contain their frozen joint-band solution. Reuse of the existing sealed endpoint is recorded, not charged as a new optimization. Setup, hash checking, independent validation and evaluation costs are separately reported.

Stop before each operation if its time/work allowance is exhausted. Accept a profile proposal only after its entire coarse-plus-fine panel is complete; an interrupted panel cannot select an early grid winner. If time ends, retain the current best feasible state and report the incomplete panel/strict status. If no proposal qualifies, the scheduled refits still run within the remaining allowance; unused verification calls return to that allowance. No retries or source-specific budget extensions are permitted.

The matched control performs **two staged joint refits** at the same seed, first capped at 150 objective calls and then spending the remainder of its 600-call/60 s allowance. This mirrors solver restarts in the profile policy and isolates the value of the profile-guided timing move more closely than a single control restart would. It uses the same incumbent retention, bounds, final strict audit and stopping rules. If the first refit uses fewer than 150 calls, the remainder transfers to its terminal refit. The comparison is the whole profile-guided policy versus matched extra optimization, not pure causality of one new parameter, and not wall-clock equivalence of individual work units.
