# Native 25 MS/s PSS timing versus paired 2.5 MS/s GLRT

**Study date:** 2026-09-02 UTC

**Status:** complete
**Scope:** five paired 60-second recordings selected from the fixed eight-hour interval ending
2026-09-02 02:36:07.288954 UTC

## Executive summary

This study compares two genuinely different observables from paired recordings:

- native 25 MS/s PSS correlation timing, which measures a repeating 750 Hz template-relative
  frame-phase coordinate; and
- dual-receiver 2.5 MS/s GLRT, which measures a dense complex-baseband CFO trajectory at the
  channel edge.

The five recordings were selected only from the strength of their already-published 2.5 MS/s
GLRT products. PSS results were not inspected during selection. This prevents selecting captures
because their PSS answer looked favorable.

The comparison preserves several distinctions that are essential to a truthful interpretation:

1. The 25 MS/s and 2.5 MS/s streams start 191–247 ms apart and use separate radio/LNB chains.
   Every comparison therefore uses first-sample UTC estimates; stream-relative seconds are not
   treated as interchangeable.
2. Native-25 PSS is searched at the actual 25 MS/s rate. The projection has decimation factor one,
   zero edge trim, and no translated subband.
3. Counter-proven native-25 gaps are never searched. The device counter preserves the logical time
   axis across each gap, and the figures shade those missing intervals.
4. GLRT CFO aliases are representation branches separated by exactly 2.5 MHz / 11 =
   227,272.727 Hz. They are canonicalized before a trajectory is fitted.
5. The physical arrival-delay sign of PSS and the receiver IQ/mixer sign of GLRT are reported
   explicitly. Opposite-sign fits are controls, not silently substituted answers.
6. Local PSS frame phase can be precise even when the absolute UTC frame cycle is not. Each
   native-25 first-sample uncertainty interval is wider than one 1.333 ms PSS period.

The completed five-capture PSS, common-rate, and satellite-association results are populated from
machine-readable evidence generated by the scripts cited in Reproduction.

The principal results are:

- all five 25 MS/s searches ran at the recorded sample rate, without decimation, translation,
  edge trim, clipped blocks, or cross-gap blocks;
- three captures publish independent PSS tracks spanning 35.98–53.38 seconds, with quadratic
  block-median RMS of 0.217–0.378 µs and 11,508–16,447 deduplicated strong frame epochs;
- the two default no-track results still contain coherent PSS families and fail at the final hard
  association gate, so they are algorithmic near misses rather than evidence of absent PSS;
- the PSS curvature magnitude agrees with both paired 2.5 MS/s GLRT receivers, but its current
  physical arrival-delay label has the opposite sign; and
- until that sign is calibrated, the only defensible satellite candidates are the independent
  dual-receiver GLRT rankings. Mirrored-PSS agreement is a strong convention diagnostic, not a
  physical identity result.

The 25 MS/s stream is centered 7.8125 MHz inward from its paired 2.5 MS/s channel-edge center.

| `d8af337f67f7` | +0.247171328 s | 2.448640 ms | 1.000192 ms |

PSS times and GLRT times are transformed onto the native-25 time axis using their respective
first-sample UTC estimates. Absolute phase or CFO offsets are not transferred between radios.

The PSS period is 1/750 s = 1.333333 ms. Every native first-sample timing interval is wider than
that period. Consequently:

- the local device-axis PSS phase modulo one frame is reportable;
- its drift and curvature can be fitted with sub-microsecond residuals when the track is strong;
- but the first-sample evidence does not choose a unique absolute UTC frame-cycle integer; and
- the PSS template epoch is not automatically the full physical OFDM frame boundary because no
  SSS or payload structure is decoded here.

### Frame-offset estimator

For the independently selected PSS track, block phases are unwrapped on the 1/750-second circle.
Huber-IRLS linear, quadratic, and cubic polynomials are fitted around the track midpoint. Residuals
are always evaluated circularly. The quadratic supplies the primary phase, phase rate, and phase
acceleration at the common reference epoch; the cubic is a model-adequacy check.

Adjacent 250 ms blocks share 125 ms of IQ. They are not independent samples. The analysis fits
even and odd block-index grids separately and plots both against the full fit. A small difference
between those two grids is evidence that the apparent precision is not merely overlap reuse.

Every strong locally refined per-frame window beneath the selected modes is then deduplicated by
its global 750 Hz frame epoch. This exposes the actual frame-level evidence that is hidden when one
block median is plotted per 125 ms stride. The report gives both:

- associated complete-block fraction; and
- unique strong-frame fraction within the observed (not zero-filled) source support.

The earlier `5dc618208b54` control validates the method: 124 of 137 complete blocks (90.5%) and
12,486 unique strong frame epochs (about 92.0% of observable frames) are aligned. Its block-median
cubic RMS is 0.295 µs, individual-frame RMS is 0.823 µs, and even/odd fit sensitivity remains below
about 0.24 µs. Individual PSS measurements are noisier than their block medians; their number is
what improves the fitted trajectory, not a claim that every individual point is sub-microsecond.

## Native-rate PSS search

The published PSS is a 4.4 µs waveform: 1056 native samples at 240 MS/s. Its deterministic
rate-specific projection has approximately 110 samples at 25 MS/s, versus about 11 samples at
2.5 MS/s. Native-25 therefore retains materially more timing structure while using the wider
captured subband.

For each complete 250 ms block the blind bank searches −1.2 to +1.2 MHz in 100 kHz steps. Up to
four coarse peaks with robust z at least four receive ±50 kHz refinement in 25 kHz steps. A folded
epoch candidate must have:

- at least four frame repetitions;
- peak/median at least 1.15;
- robust z at least 6; and
- separation of at least two PSS-symbol durations from another retained candidate.

Each accepted frame is locally refined within ±2 µs and retains fractional-sample timing,
normalized match power, local peak/median, and correlation phase. A block mode uses the median of
windows whose local peak/median is at least five, falling back to all qualified windows only when
none meet that strong threshold.

PSS track association is independent of GLRT. It fits circular quadratic hypotheses from PSS modes,
requires at least six blocks and two seconds of span, accepts at most ±2 µs phase residual and
±150 kHz CFO deviation, and emits at most eight tracks. The primary track in this report is the
published PSS track with the longest span, then most modes, then lowest RMS.

### Why 62.5/31.25 ms is a refinement, not the primary blind search

The shorter geometry produces roughly four times as many block epochs and is useful after a blind
track has supplied CFO and phase targets. In an unconstrained blind search, however, the current
top-96 seed limit can crowd the seeds into the earliest high-score interval; temporal diversity is
lost and a valid full-span track can fail association even when thousands of good modes exist.
A 250 ms block also contains about 187.5 nominal 750 Hz repetitions, whereas a 62.5 ms block
contains only about 46.9. Short blocks therefore provide four times the cadence but one quarter of
the coherent/repetition evidence per blind decision, increasing the importance of a previously
acquired phase/CFO corridor.

The defensible architecture is therefore:

1. blind acquisition at 250/125 ms;
2. native 62.5/31.25 ms refinement conditioned on the acquired PSS phase/CFO corridor; and
3. deduplicated per-frame timing for the final fit.

This is measured, not only architectural preference. On the independent `5dc618208b54` control,
the unconstrained 62.5/31.25 ms replay produced 3,316 retained modes in 1,050 candidate blocks but
published zero tracks because the global top-96 seeds crowded one part of time. Applying the
coarse PSS track's phase/CFO corridor—without using GLRT—recovered 503 of 568 complete short blocks
(88.6%) on common support. Its cubic block-median RMS improved from 0.295 µs at 250/125 ms to
0.203 µs, while the quadratic RMS remained essentially unchanged (0.515 versus 0.524 µs). This
distinguishes added sampling density from a claim that shorter windows remove real higher-order
curvature. See
[native25-pss-window-geometry-comparison.png](figures/2026_09_01_5dc618208b54_pss_glrt/native25-pss-window-geometry-comparison.png)
and its
[machine-readable comparison](figures/2026_09_01_5dc618208b54_pss_glrt/pss-window-geometry-comparison.json).

GLRT may narrow a PSS CFO search, but a result using such a target is marked `glrt_conditioned` and
is not presented as an independent PSS detection.

## GLRT aliases, episode shifts, and fitting

GLRT observations carry a raw CFO and integer `alias_index`. The canonical coordinate is

```text
canonical_CFO = raw_CFO - alias_index × (2,500,000 / 11) Hz
```

The alias branches are estimator representations, not physical 227.273 kHz Doppler jumps. In the
weaker receiver they can switch hundreds of times; in the best RX1 products they usually switch
zero to 38 times. Canonicalization restores smooth within-episode Hough tracks. See
[glrt-cfo-alias-canonicalization.png](figures/2026_09_02_five_paired_pss_glrt/glrt-cfo-alias-canonicalization.png).

These are switches between `alias_index` values *inside* already-published Hough episodes. They
must not be counted as counter gaps, transmitter handovers, or independent measurements. The
published per-episode residual range is included to show that canonicalization is local evidence:
it does not by itself prove that two separately seeded episodes belong to the same transmitter.

A second distinction is required: independently seeded Hough episodes can themselves land one
integer alias class apart, or can be genuinely incompatible simultaneous tracks. The analysis
anchors the largest GLRT episode, tests only integer 227.273 kHz shifts, iteratively fits a robust
smooth trajectory, and retains an episode only when its median residual is within 0.015 alias
spacing (3.409 kHz). This stitching uses GLRT alone—never PSS, a TLE, or a desired rate. The
tolerance is deliberately much wider than the typical tens-to-hundreds-of-hertz within-episode
line residual and can still admit a nearby compatible transmitter. Rejected episodes are retained
in the evidence as plausible other transmitters rather than forced into the dominant pass; every
TLE rank is repeated on the single largest episode as the declared stitching-sensitivity check.

## Common-rate Doppler comparison and sign

PSS frame phase is a timing coordinate in seconds. If it is interpreted as arrival delay
\(\tau(t)\), physical fractional Doppler is

```text
fractional Doppler = -dτ/dt
fractional Doppler rate = -d²τ/dt²
```

GLRT supplies complex-baseband CFO, so its common unit is

```text
fractional CFO rate = d(CFO)/dt / RF_reference
```

These have the same dimensions (s⁻¹), but their signs are not automatically comparable. The PSS
implementation’s template-relative phase orientation and the receiver IQ/LNB mixing sign have not
both been calibrated to a common physical convention. The report therefore gives:

- physical arrival-delay PSS sign;
- the older repository same-sign PSS diagnostic;
- recorded GLRT IQ sign; and
- opposite-GLRT-IQ control.

Agreement under a control sign is evidence of a convention mismatch to resolve, not permission to
rename the control “physical.” Absolute PSS phase and absolute GLRT CFO intercepts are also excluded
from the cross-radio comparison; only time variation is meaningful.

The comparison also uses more than one instantaneous derivative. For each independently stitched
GLRT family, CFO/RF is integrated over time and compared with the independently selected PSS phase.
Only a constant phase and linear inter-radio clock drift are fitted. Physical `−∫CFO/RF` and the
same-sign `+∫CFO/RF` control are scored with bidirectional 60/40 holdout against an affine-only null.
This tests whether the two observables share the same curvature over their common support without
using PSS to choose a GLRT episode or using GLRT to choose a PSS mode.

The candidate filter requires usable propagation, plausible altitude, and geometric-horizon
visibility during the fixed capture interval. No time shift, Doppler scale, TLE slope, or TLE
curvature is fitted. Ranking uses each declared first-sample UTC *estimate* directly; the
earliest/latest timing interval is retained as evidence but is not searched to improve a candidate.

Independent PSS ranking uses

```text
observed unwrapped phase(t)
  = geometric range(t)/c + constant clock offset + constant clock drift × t
```

It uses the independently selected block medians, not every overlapping refined frame. The dense
frames improve and audit the local timing fit, but are not treated as thousands of independent TLE
votes.

Independent GLRT ranking uses

```text
observed alias-stitched CFO(t)
  = physical TLE Doppler(t) + constant receiver/LNB CFO offset
```

Both use bidirectional 60/40 holdout: fit nuisance terms on the first 60% and predict the last 40%,
then reverse. Scores are normalized by an affine-only PSS null or constant-only GLRT null. The joint
score is the squared normalized PSS ratio plus the equal-weight mean of the squared physical-sign
GLRT ratios from RX0 and RX1. Per-receiver and best-receiver-only results remain explicit diagnostics;
the primary joint result therefore cannot silently ignore receiver disagreement.

Physical and opposite-sign rankings, cross-ranks, top/second separation, and the number of candidates
within five percent of best are all retained. A low score alone is not an identification. This report
calls a result a candidate association and explicitly reports when the ranking is non-specific. The
mechanical `specific` flag requires the runner-up score to be more than 20% worse than best; cross-
receiver and single-episode stability are additional evidence, not replaced by that threshold.

## Artifact interpretation guide

### Sparse clusters in the PSS plot

The apparent clusters are the intersections of a continuous logical 60-second axis with native-25
observed continuity segments. Gray spans are missing IQ. Within an observed segment, one plotted
track mode represents a 250 ms block median, not one frame. The dense frame-offset figure exposes
the underlying 750 Hz measurements.

The count difference from GLRT is mostly geometry, not inferior native sampling. Each gap-free
2.5 MS/s receiver schedules 5,999 overlapping 20 ms GLRT windows. After excluding native-25 gaps
and every incomplete 250 ms interval, these captures have only about 270 PSS blind-search blocks,
and the track plot posts at most one selected mode per block. The retained strong per-frame PSS
windows are separately deduplicated and reported in the thousands. At 25 MS/s the projected 4.4 µs
PSS has about 110 samples rather than about 11 at 2.5 MS/s, so the native stream improves local
timing information even though the conservative posted block-median series is much sparser.

### Vertical spikes or sprays

Unassociated modes can occur at several phase/CFO hypotheses in one block. They are folded-correlation
alternatives, sidelobes, weak-signal peaks, or another transmitter—not timing samples from the
selected track. The track fit uses at most one mode per block. Complete-block scheduling removes the
earlier start/end clipping artifact.

### Piecewise arcs and curvature

PSS points occur only where native IQ exists, so a smooth timing trajectory appears as short arcs.
The device counter spans gaps, which is why one polynomial can continue across them. Quadratic-vs-
cubic improvement measures real unmodeled curvature over the track span; it is not automatically
proof of a satellite without the independent TLE test.

### GLRT color/branch changes

Within a Hough episode, raw branch changes by exactly 227.273 kHz disappear after the declared alias
correction. Between episodes, an additional integer shift may be needed because seeds are independent.
A non-integer-incompatible episode is rejected from the dominant family and remains evidence of a
second transmitter or a bad association.

### Higher PSS RMS than GLRT

PSS residuals are timing errors in microseconds; GLRT residuals are frequency errors in hertz. Their
raw RMS values are not comparable. PSS can have a larger displayed number while providing a stronger
constraint on timing curvature. Comparison is performed only after converting derivatives to a
common fractional-rate dimension or after normalizing each TLE fit by its own nuisance-only null.

### TLE ranking bars and fit residuals

The ranking bars are holdout RMS divided by the corresponding nuisance-only null, so lower is
better. They must be read together with top/second separation, near-tie count, cross-receiver rank,
and the absolute residual shown in the fitted-trajectory figure. A score of 0.005 with a 300 Hz
absolute residual means the TLE explains essentially all of a tens-of-kilohertz Doppler sweep while
leaving smaller systematic structure; it does not mean the remaining residual is zero. Physical
and opposite-sign panels are kept side by side. A control that is null-like and has hundreds of
near ties is evidence against that sign, not an alternative identification.

## Operational recommendations

1. Keep 250/125 ms as the default blind PSS acquisition geometry and continue to suppress every
   clipped or cross-gap block.
2. After an independent PSS track exists, run a PSS-conditioned 62.5/31.25 ms refinement. Preserve
   its targeting provenance so it cannot be mistaken for an independent acquisition.
3. Publish both the conservative one-mode-per-block series and deduplicated strong per-frame
   evidence. The former is easier to audit; the latter carries the timing density needed for a
   precise phase trajectory.
4. Retain even/odd stride fits and quadratic-versus-cubic residuals as routine overlap and
   model-adequacy checks.
5. Calibrate PSS template phase orientation and each receiver/LNB IQ sign with a known injected or
   geometrically authoritative source. Until then, retain physical and mirrored/opposite controls
   side by side.
6. Reduce first-sample timing uncertainty below 1/750 second before claiming an absolute UTC PSS
   frame-cycle integer. SSS or decoded payload timing is still required to promote a PSS-template
   epoch to a protocol frame boundary.
7. Treat causal-TLE output as candidate ranking and add site authority, antenna/beam evidence,
   known pilots, and TLE covariance before promoting it to satellite identification.

## Limitations

- The native-25 streams retain only about 60.5–61.1% of the logical source timeline.
- PSS block medians overlap by 50%; even/odd grids are the declared sensitivity check.
- The two sample rates come from separate radios/LNB chains with independent clocks and offsets.
- Absolute UTC frame-cycle selection is prevented by the current first-sample uncertainty.
- No SSS, payload, known-pilot, beam schedule, or antenna-pattern evidence is used.
- PSS/GLRT may observe different simultaneous transmitters even within one paired recording.
- TLE association is limited by catalogue age, site authority, unknown boresight, and the short
  60-second observation.
- Overlapping PSS blocks and dense GLRT observations are autocorrelated. Holdout ratios and
  runner-up separations are ranking diagnostics, not probabilities or independent-trial p-values.
- Ranking searches hundreds of visible catalogue elements. The mechanical specificity flag is not
  corrected for multiple candidates, and TLE covariance is not propagated into a calibrated
  identity posterior.
