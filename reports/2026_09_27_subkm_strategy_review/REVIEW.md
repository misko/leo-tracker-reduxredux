# Sub-kilometre positioning: evidence and approach ranking

Review date: 27 September 2026. Online repository snapshot: `75b76f66974c78588c6599822e8007aaa767466b` (remote HEAD when reviewed). Local checkout: `a887eec1560606dc7f4ffcbc5bbded1bd798f1c8`, with substantial unrelated untracked work preserved.

**Recommendation:** retain the corrected pooled static-site Doppler estimator as the baseline; prioritize independent orbit information and identifiable clock/orbit corrections; treat better frequency extraction and association as supporting work. A surveyed reference receiver is the strongest alternative architecture. Current short-baseline phase and directional reception are research aids, not demonstrated routes to repeatable sub-kilometre accuracy.

This review inventories all 1,287 Markdown assets beneath online `reports/`, screens their text for positioning and measurement evidence, and closely reads the relevant consolidated results, underlying ablations, numerical corrections, negative experiments, and qualifications. These are report assets, not 1,287 independent experiments: 213 are figure/provenance documents, and some are protocols or archived copies. There are 1,280 distinct content hashes. `report_inventory.tsv` records every asset and its GitHub URL, hash, heuristic category and screening excerpts. Screening is not a line-by-line numerical audit. No scientific pipeline was rerun; test counts below are reported by their source publications. The review also checks primary external literature. It does not change runtime code or collect RF.

All repository links below are pinned to the reviewed online commit. Local drafts were read as context; newer online consolidated publications govern superseded claims.

DS6 spans approximately 6.2 elapsed hours of already-recorded data. Its pooled result is not a five-minute fix or proof of how much data is necessary. The site reference is operator supplied, with unknown survey uncertainty and altitude. The pooled solve is conditional on a local ±12 km search and inherited candidates. Training-only selection and numerical convergence are valuable, but do not establish blind global acquisition or external accuracy. The two subset estimates are 626 m apart. Pooling also **worsens held prediction in 39/43 scans**: the signal model still prefers displaced individual locations. This is evidence of model mismatch, not a reason to discard a geographically useful pooled baseline.

Historical corrections matter. The September 7 nominal 669 m result became 1.758 km when evaluated against the corrected reference. Several later attractive estimates were boundary solutions or lost qualification. DS1 iteration 15's 576 m result was invalidated by a later audit; iteration 20's 267 m result was unqualified. These should not enter a leaderboard of validated methods. See [historical reconciliation](https://github.com/misko/leo-tracker-reduxredux/blob/75b76f66974c78588c6599822e8007aaa767466b/reports/2026_09_24_subkm_reconciliation/REPORT.md) and [DS1/DS3 status table](https://github.com/misko/leo-tracker-reduxredux/blob/75b76f66974c78588c6599822e8007aaa767466b/reports/2026_09_25_ds1_ds3_all_methods/REPORT.md).

## Ranking for existing hardware and recordings

Ranks express recommended engineering priority, balancing evidence, plausible accuracy impact, identifiability, and implementation cost. They are judgments, not probabilities or measured head-to-head rankings. The approaches are complementary.

| Rank | Approach | Prospects and evidence | Cost / limiting issue | Next decisive test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Joint static-site Doppler over multiple recordings | Only current corrected DS6 estimator consistently below 1 km across full and complementary pooled subsets | Low incremental cost; static assumption, latency, common bias and conditional acquisition | Freeze complete estimator; compare matched 1/2/4/8/16/all recording budgets on disjoint existing groups; preserve all failures |
| **2** | Better independent orbital inputs and causal element selection | Clear physical leverage; newest causal elements improved all five affected DS6 scans | Low for provider fix, medium for new products; historical availability and frame/time conventions | Compare identical observations/candidates/search with causal GP, SupGP and source ephemerides where archived |
| **3** | Constrained satellite orbit-error hierarchy | Strongest earlier matched ablation: 3.137 km → 328 m | Medium/high; identity conditioning and position–orbit confounding; weak transfer evidence | Training-only association, externally learned age-dependent priors, shared per-satellite corrections and zero-correction control on new groups |
| **4** | Independent timestamp, sample-clock and receiver-frequency calibration | Timing sensitivity is large; can break confounding that fitting cannot | Medium; host brackets do not certify UTC; a stable SDR reference does not automatically stabilize an independent LNB | Audit recorded authority; inject known timing/sample-rate errors; compare constrained physical states against current nuisance model |
| **5** | More informative CFO measurements from existing IQ | Full-frame/pilot processing can improve noise and track support; useful for shorter fixes | Medium/high; orbit bias may dominate even precise CFO; bandwidth and model applicability | Matched native-IQ estimator comparison with disjoint samples, retained failures, code/carrier divergence and downstream location checks |
| **6** | Joint source association, alias accounting and cross-channel dynamics | Can suppress catastrophic wrong modes; 28–31 Hz cross-channel agreement motivates shared-source diagnostics | Medium; shared receiver drift can mimic shared source; overconfident shortlists | RF-only grouping with separate channel offsets; independent catalogue search; source/null and wrong-association controls |
| **7** | Better geographic search and candidate completeness | Essential to avoid 12.5 km grid artifacts and truncated edge winners | Low/medium; finer optimization can find a better-fitting wrong location | Multi-basin search, matched coordinates, exact final evaluation and interior/convergence gates |
| **8** | Correlation-aware likelihood and information-based geometry budgeting | Necessary for honest confidence and economical use of data | Medium; smaller residuals repeatedly fail to improve geography | Project out nuisance parameters; choose complementary geometry without reference coordinates; validate whole-group coverage |
| **9** | Simple position averaging or objective-surface fusion | Cheap, useful baseline; DS5 1.459 km full-set best did not transfer below 1 km | Low; biased/grid-quantized inputs and RMS is not position variance | Keep unchanged as control for true joint raw-observation fitting |
| **10** | Existing two-LNB detection/strength direction evidence | Predicts reception; small inconsistent geographic gains | Medium; uncalibrated beam response, correlated tracks, provisional mapping | Require independent predictive success beyond geometry-free regularization before another location comparison |
| **11** | Existing 8 cm differential phase as direct position evidence | Qualified local phase exists, but current matched pooled gain is negligible | High calibration burden and weak position information after free offsets | Target training-predicted nonlinear geometry; distinguish it from slow response drift on held visits |

Element freshness is more than checking download time. In DS6 a mirror downloaded two seconds later carried older elements; changing per-object selection improved one scan from 9.053 to 1.998 km. The same correction left 38/43 scans unchanged and did not make any affected scan sub-km. [Element freshness report](https://github.com/misko/leo-tracker-reduxredux/blob/75b76f66974c78588c6599822e8007aaa767466b/reports/2026_09_27_ds6_element_freshness/README.md).

Do not transfer the historical 328 m expectation to today's unknown-identity model. DS5's portable orbit-rate method reached 7.286 km on full42. The implementations, inputs, searches and conditioning differ; this prevents a causal comparison, but demonstrates that adding an orbit-rate parameter is not sufficient. DS6's proposed satellite-specific slope transfer had **zero qualifying target tracks**, despite 703 eligible tracks, because the required repeated-satellite donor support was absent. It did not test a correction successfully. [Transfer coverage](https://github.com/misko/leo-tracker-reduxredux/blob/75b76f66974c78588c6599822e8007aaa767466b/reports/2026_09_27_ds6_satellite_slope_transfer/README.md).

## Alternative architectures and additional observables

These ranks apply among alternatives requiring new calibration, sensors or external assistance. They are not directly comparable to the software priority above.

| Rank | Approach | Evaluation | Principal requirement / risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A1** | Surveyed reference receiver: differential Doppler / FDOA | Strongest hardware route; cancels shared transmitter terms and reduces common orbit errors | Same-source observation, matched epochs, calibrated local receiver terms, useful baseline and overlap |
| **A2** | Independent orbit/clock corrections from a reference service or station | Supports standalone rover processing with externally constrained satellite states | Correction coverage, age, covariance and transfer; never calibrate and evaluate on the same known site data |
| **A3** | Calibrated antenna array or measured DOA fused with Doppler | Adds an observable with different degeneracies; experimentally supported in literature | Surveyed orientation/phase centres, sufficient aperture, phase ambiguity and multipath control |
| **A4** | Wideband TOA/TDOA with clock corrections | High potential for ranging; more bandwidth can sharpen delay | Transmit-time authority, frame ambiguity, per-satellite clock steps and delay calibration |
| **A5** | Carrier phase / time-differenced carrier phase with coherent hardware | High precision once continuity, ambiguities and physical response are established | Cycle slips, retunes, beam changes, independent oscillator drift and orbit errors |
| **A6** | Multi-constellation and sensor fusion | More independent geometry; IMU/odometry helps moving receivers, altitude helps observability | New RF front ends or sensors, inter-system biases; external position aid changes the scientific claim |
| **A7** | Learned correction or fingerprint model | Potential compact nuisance prior if physical models leave repeatable structure | Requires trustworthy labels and held-site/day transfer; one-site learning easily memorizes the answer |

The differential Doppler literature reports 5.6 m error over a 1 km base–rover separation and 2.6 m over 9 m using three Starlink satellites. These are demonstrations under their own conditions, not a forecast for our apparatus. The roof's two nearby LNBs do not constitute that surveyed base–rover experiment. “Sacramento” and “Reno” in our reports are independent search priors, not receiver installations. [Primary differential study](https://people.engineering.osu.edu/sites/default/files/2022-10/Kassas_First_results_of_differential_Doppler_positioning_with_unknown_Starlink_satellite_signals.pdf).

External ephemerides deserve a concrete trial before a large orbit-fitting framework. CelesTrak publishes Starlink SupGP derived from SpaceX's public ephemerides, but neither SupGP nor its fit residual is ground-truth orbit accuracy. Compare correctly dated products on identical observations. Retrospective products and causal products must remain separate. Its current documentation also flags the six-digit catalogue-number transition: audit catalogue completeness and identifier handling without assuming a defect in this repository. [CelesTrak source](https://celestrak.org/NORAD/elements/supplemental/).

Reference-assisted ephemeris/clock compensation has a physical basis distinct from unconstrained track detrending. Published work explicitly uses a known-position stationary receiver to constrain satellite temporal/spatial errors. That external authority must be included when comparing its accuracy with our standalone system. [Primary compensation study](https://people.engineering.osu.edu/media/document/2024-12-05/kassas_modeling_and_compensation_of_timing_and_spatial_ephemeris_errors_of_non_cooperative_leo_satellites_with_application_to_pnt.pdf).

The 2025 DOA/Doppler study uses electronically steered holographic antenna arrays and recorded Starlink/OneWeb observations, with Monte Carlo initializations. It supports DOA as a distinct path, not treating the two broad roof reception patterns as a calibrated angular sensor. [Primary DOA study](https://people.engineering.osu.edu/media/document/2025-09-26/kassas_direction_of_arrival_and_doppler_based_positioning_with_starlink_and_oneweb_leo_satellites.pdf).

Qin's full-frame ML work reports post-fit Doppler residuals of 6.34 Hz versus much larger pilot-only residuals. These are estimator residuals, not independently measured position errors. Its full-frame bandwidth and waveform assumptions must be checked against each stored recording; existing edge-pilot processing is not the same estimator as its PSS/SSS comparator. The 2026 predictable-elements paper provides further waveform structure worth evaluating, without presuming the narrowband corpus contains the full waveform. [ML Doppler/TOA paper](https://radionavlab.ae.utexas.edu/wp-content/uploads/qin_ML_Precise_TOA_PLANS.pdf), [predictable elements](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02627).

TOA precision is not absolute range. The timing study finds nanosecond-level stable periods but timing jumps and only loose synchronization to GPS, with potential correction assistance. One kilometre corresponds to about 3.34 microseconds of one-way travel time; that is only a scale, not a sufficient positioning specification. Unknown transmitter clocks and frame ambiguities can dominate a very sharp correlation peak. [Timing study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.05302).

## Why more phase or lower residuals may not help

For an illustrative 550 km slant range, a 1 km transverse position change subtends approximately 0.104 degrees. At 11.2 GHz an 8 cm baseline spans about three wavelengths, giving at most roughly 2 degrees of absolute single-source phase change for that angular perturbation under favorable projection. Unknown source offsets remove much of this absolute information; nuisance drift can remove its temporal variation as well. These are geometric scale calculations, not a measured precision budget.

The actual DS6 phase sensitivity study is more decisive: with independent free pair offsets and illustrative 1-degree observation noise, training-only weak-axis uncertainties were 10.1–184.7 km for one scan and 37.7–1328.5 km for another. The large values indicate weak local information, not valid predictions of global errors. An 18-second phase trend predicted held visits better than a constant but essentially tied slow linear drift. Dense phase points cannot fix that ambiguity by themselves. [Sensitivity](https://github.com/misko/leo-tracker-reduxredux/blob/75b76f66974c78588c6599822e8007aaa767466b/reports/2026_09_27_ds6_phase_sensitivity/README.md), [geometry versus drift](https://github.com/misko/leo-tracker-reduxredux/blob/75b76f66974c78588c6599822e8007aaa767466b/reports/2026_09_27_ds6_long_phase_geometry/README.md).

The same issue affects Doppler. With unknown track offsets, receiver clock and satellite corrections, useful location information is the part of the position derivative that cannot be explained by those nuisances. A smaller frequency residual can mean better measurements, better physical correction, or simply more freedom to hide a wrong position. Inspect the nuisance-projected information matrix and its weak directions, then test real held-group geographic error. Kalman filters, smoothers, factor graphs, robust losses and mixture models are implementation choices within this problem; none supplies missing absolute information.

## Approaches to retain as controls, not lead investments

- **Independent linear/quadratic detrending:** often improves prediction while worsening geography; can remove the distinguishing orbital shape. Constrain physical shared states instead.
- **Choosing longest or cleanest tracks:** DS6's >=30-second rule improved two and worsened two development cases, none sub-km. Select independent information and support, not the cutoff nearest truth.
- **Hard antenna cones or more RX likelihood weight:** current mapping/response is insufficiently calibrated, and the latest disjoint progression gate failed.
- **Fine grid alone:** necessary for resolving an optimum, but a finer biased optimum remains biased. Boundary results remain unresolved.
- **Higher sample rate or more RF alone:** current rate comparisons mix time and geometry. Compare derived bandwidths from the same native IQ before claiming causality.
- **More compute / cached acquisition alone:** useful for latency and coverage; test weak-track retention, controls, association and final positions. A faster detector is not a new navigation observable.
- **Tuning altitude to reduce truth error:** measure/independently constrain altitude and datum; DS6 trial positive heights worsened all four tested development errors.
- **Same-site fingerprint or free learned correction:** no evidence of cross-site generalization; use independently grounded corrections, not a model trained to return the roof coordinate.

## Proposed bounded evaluation sequence

1. **Freeze definitions and baseline.** Distinguish single-recording, pooled static, continuing-station and blind regional/global products. Use corrected stationary-offset profiling and per-object causal elements. Record reference authority, altitude, prior, shortlist generation, compute budget and all historical exposure.
2. **Measure how much existing data is needed.** Replay fixed recording-count budgets and complementary sky geometries on existing cohorts, with a joint raw-Doppler baseline and cheap aggregation controls. Keep all correlated receivers/tracks together. Report elapsed support, not just sample count.
3. **Isolate orbital versus receiver mismatch.** Compare better archived orbit inputs first; then constrained satellite corrections and receiver clock states with matched observations and search. Use cross-channel/common-receiver contrasts. Do not assume DS6 supplies repeated-satellite correction donors.
4. **Test better measurements on a small frozen IQ panel.** Include failed/weak cases. Compare known-pilot, available full-frame/partial-frame ML and source-sharing models at identical epochs. Preserve code/carrier divergence and discontinuity alternatives. Advance only if downstream inference benefits without excess false support.
5. **Freeze the complete policy and evaluate disjoint groups.** Randomized whole correlated groups must include candidate selection, hyperparameter fitting and calibration within the training boundary. Reused DS6 observations are development evidence. Reserve another already-recorded cohort with audited exposure if available; otherwise state that fresh confirmation is unavailable.
6. **If single-recording accuracy still fails, prioritize reference assistance.** Design common-source timing/visibility and calibration requirements before any hardware purchase or collection. Any future RF acquisition needs explicit authorization and a <=30-minute bound under repository rules.

For every arm report median, p90/p95, fraction below 1 km, maximum error, availability/abstentions, initialization sensitivity, boundary/convergence status, latency, and uncertainty coverage. Single-site repeated windows do not establish geographic generalization. A proposed operational target is at least 95% of predeclared trials below 1 km with failures and abstentions separately visible, tested on independent sites/sessions; this is a proposed criterion, not a current achievement or an agreed requirement.

The most useful immediate deliverable is a small matched experiment matrix around the corrected pooled baseline: **orbit input × constrained error model × recording budget**, with reference-free selection and group-level evaluation. It directly tests the strongest evidence while avoiding another broad campaign.
