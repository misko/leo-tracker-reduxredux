What we learned about one-method discovery
The current 100/50/25/12.5 hierarchy found and retained [-87.5,-87.5] km itself,
and that region supplied both winning c arms. No T1AT regional discovery,
T1AT likelihood fit, imported published winning region, true-position start,
or special paired-clock initializer was used. Unlike the earlier replay, this
experiment also uses V16 for the receiver calibration likelihood. Existing
generic track bootstrap and discrete timing-mode association routines remain
shared numerical algorithms. Verified original bootstrap checkpoints may be
reused only at a coordinate independently requested by the new search; they
contain no other method's ranking in the consumed data.

The initial three comparisons demonstrate that finer grids alone did not improve
this capture. The original 50/20/5 search came no closer than 13.444 km in its
evaluated centers; the 50 km halving search reached 3.911 km but ranked that
particular center 126th. Center proximity alone does not determine final accuracy.

Specific edge-priority issue
The deployed hierarchy gives initial cells whose centers are outside the allowed
circle an artificial score of zero, while retaining them because their squares
intersect the circle. Real negative-log-likelihood scores here are positive,
around tens of thousands. These unmeasured cells therefore receive unconditional
priority. Their unmeasured descendants inherit this optimistic score.

With 50/20/5 this caused 212/400 evaluations (53%) within the outermost 10 km of
the 250 km prior disk; 198 were at the 5 km level. Replaying the original search
from its frozen scores reproduced every evaluation and ordering exactly and
identified 208 evaluations introduced by an unmeasured parent. The same mechanism
exists for halving: the 100 km and 50 km halving searches spent 45 and 44 points,
respectively, in that outer 10 km band. The finer final grid amplified it.

Controlled edge-priority follow-up
Only the initial unmeasured edge priority changed: it inherits the score of the
nearest actually evaluated coarse center (deterministic tie breaking). Inside
cells retain their measured scores and descendants retain the existing inherited
score rule. This is a heuristic, not a proven lower bound on unexplored cells.
Boundary regions remain eligible; the tests verify that an attractive boundary
region can still be refined. No reference coordinate or distance is consulted.

On the same 50/20/5 geometry and same 400-point budget:
  - Evaluations in the outer 10 km fell from 212 to 41 (53% to 10.25%).
  - 207 shared point scores were exactly identical between the two runs.
  - The closest evaluated center was 1.579 km from the reference and ranked first.
  - The selected center was [-87.5,-82.5] km, discovered by the new search.
  - Final errors improved from 9.451 to 3.633 km (zero-c) and from 13.659 to
    4.038 km (fitted-c). All three retained regions passed calibration.

This isolates an avoidable search-priority bias. It does not establish an
unbiased estimator or guarantee complete coverage. The repaired 50/20/5 run
still had worse position accuracy than the 100 km hierarchy on this capture.

Should every grid divide by two?
Halving is a good default because each square has four equal children with one
parent, no overlap, and simple budget accounting. Other integer subdivision
factors can also tile exactly. The tested 50/20/5 implementation handles the
noninteger 50/20 ratio with globally aligned overlapping child squares; coverage
and duplicate-evaluation tests pass. Halving itself does not remove score-driven
search bias, the edge-priority issue, or dependence on grid origin and budget.
For an exact 5 km final level with halving, 40/20/10/5 is a possible next test;
it was NOT evaluated in this experiment. No global claim about the best grid
schedule follows from one capture.

Cost and validation
Initial centers within the same prior disk: 100 km = 16; 50 km = 80;
20 km = 484; 5 km = 7,860. Uniform 20 km and 5 km starting grids were not run,
because their initial pass already exceeds the matched 400-point budget.

Four 400-point search receipts and 66 final optimizer calls were completed.
Identical point evaluations and identical retained regions can be cached across
variants; the logical point budgets still charge each requested position.
First three searches/refinements completed in 804.9 s with eight worker processes;
the edge follow-up took 236.5 s with shared cached evaluations. These concurrent
and cached wall times are not standalone production latency measurements.

All point evaluations returned finite scores, but only 307/301/311/305 of the
400 short coarse fits converged for the four schedules respectively. This limits
confidence in coarse rankings. One retained region in the 50 km halving run
failed calibration prefit convergence and was excluded; it remains explicitly
recorded. Three of the original 48 final fit attempts were nonstationary and did
not win. The eight score-selected final outputs passed independent repricing,
feasibility and constrained-stationarity audits. Ten regression tests passed,
including exact legacy search parity, noninteger coverage, deduplication,
budget limits, slope-bound stationarity, and edge exploration.

The c=0/fitted-c ablation is in the final position fit: each arm shares that
schedule's V16 fitted-c discovery, calibration, and association. Observations,
candidate subsets, priors, starts, domains, and final budgets are matched within
each comparison. The arms never share optimized final outputs. Reference
coordinates are introduced only after fitting and score-based selection.
