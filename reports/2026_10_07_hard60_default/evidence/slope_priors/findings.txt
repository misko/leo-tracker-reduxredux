Completed three Sol-agent slope-prior prototypes
==============================================

Coverage correction from subsequent review: the original 296.82 km and
189.44 km V16 fitted-c failures were NOT in this six-case experiment. Both
archived wrong states already satisfy +/-10 Hz/s. See
original_failures_review.txt and original_failures_review.json. The results
below do not establish a slope-prior benefit for those original two examples.

Further coverage note: the earlier 14:39 paired-clock repair yielding 4.50 km
used a V16 search region absent from this experiment's V16 basin pool. These
runs do not test slope limits on that repaired branch. See anchor_case_review.txt.

Tested independent hard windows +/-60, +/-30, and +/-10 Hz/s on RX0/RX1's
additional frequency slopes, alongside an unrestricted control. The extra
slope is added after the frozen calibration. Both methods use relative timing
sigma = 1 s. All comparisons include matched c=0 and fitted-c arms. No production
settings changed. No new RF was collected.

Execution: six fixed retrospective cases, frozen observations/candidates and
calibration, same original starts and budgets. A 64-call screen per option was
followed by 32 own-option/own-arm continuations because early results were not
stationary. Total: 384 fitting calls across all four options. No fitted output
was passed between arms or windows. Reference positions were used only after
score-based selection. The frozen protocol and one bounded continuation plan
are in protocol.txt and polish_protocol.txt.

Main finding
------------
There is no established winning prior width. Every selected unrestricted fit
already has |additional slope| <= 9.5375 Hz/s, so all control selections are
feasible in all three windows. Most position estimates are unchanged. Different
answers under wider inactive bounds expose optimizer path dependence, not an
identified physical clock restriction benefit.

At the difficult Oct 6, 14:39 PDT (21:39 UTC) capture:

V16 zero-c error, km            24.628        24.960        24.860       24.603

Fitted-c timing penalty       1402.700      1514.443      1581.056     1510.393
Fitted-c KKT residual            .000390       .000129       .022395      .004876

KKT convergence threshold is 0.001. The +/-30 and +/-10 fitted-c results remain
nonstationary. +/-60 and +/-30 have no selected slope-bound hits anywhere in
the six cases. +/-10 hits RX1 = +10 only in that unresolved V16 fitted-c result.
The +/-60 selected fit there has additional slopes [-8.326, 11.133] Hz/s but
total linear slopes [12.817, -16.239] Hz/s after adding calibration. Nonlinear
piecewise calibration is separate. Therefore a small additional slope is not
evidence of a small total modeled clock trend.

The earlier Oct 6, 12:39 PDT failure stays approximately 6.99 km in V16 under every
window. The Oct 6, 15:52 PDT case provides a counterexample: V16 zero-c error
worsens from 0.921 km to 1.754 km under all three windows, despite frequency RMS
improving from 136.49 Hz to 135.28 Hz and data NLL improving by 1.068. Frequency
agreement alone is not localization accuracy.

Validation and limits
---------------------
Thirteen solver/runner tests passed, including unrestricted parity and correct
KKT handling at both receiver bounds. All 96 saved published fits used for input
validation repriced exactly. The common numerical adapter checked 256 initial
candidate evaluations. Independent comparison checked all 96 final selections'
scores, feasibility, KKT, selection ordering and position errors. Continuation
seeds were verified against their own first-stage receipts. All 384 fit calls
completed without recorded fit exceptions.

Final selected KKT-converged counts out of 24: control 24, +/-60 24, +/-30 23,
+/-10 23. All 96 selected results are feasible. The audit records 25 width-nesting
gaps above 1e-6 score units; a narrower feasible set beating a wider search
indicates incomplete optimization, even if both returned local stationary
points. These local stationary points are not certificates of global optimality.

Both c arms retain the shared fitted-c upstream calibration, so this is a
final-stage ablation. Searches are conditional on published winning-basin unions
and a shared 25 km local radius; they do not explore a new global candidate set.
The test deliberately includes failures plus two previously accurate guard
scans; it is not representative of all 22 completed scans or future captures.
Fresh control scores use sigma = 1 s, so improvements versus original published
sigma = 0.15 s V16 results cannot be attributed to these slope bounds.

Next useful plan
----------------
Keep the prototype experimental. First investigate why the same feasible model
finds different stationary solutions from the same original starts when only
inactive bounds change. If the intended physical constraint is receiver-clock
stability, separately test a prior on total calibration-plus-correction drift
or on paired-receiver differential drift, with its definition frozen first.
Then evaluate any justified candidate over the full frozen 22-scan cohort and
chronologically held-out scans. Do not select a production width from the best
single nonstationary position result.
