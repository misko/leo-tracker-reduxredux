Review of the quoted 14:39 receiver-clock repair
==============================================

Capture: scan-fw-e3bebc228093a125.
First sample: 2026-10-06 14:39:41.786744 PDT = 21:39:41.786744 UTC.
Research artifact label: 2139 (UTC label).
Original published V16 errors: zero-c 305.642557 km, fitted-c 43.619118 km.
This is a different capture from the 296.817735 km fitted-c failure at 11:38 PDT.

The roughly 5.9 km branch
------------------------
The frequency-width continuation reached 5.899131 km zero-c error, with extra
receiver slopes RX0 -2.627075 and RX1 -129.237886 Hz/s. Its paired successor,
used as the anchor experiment's source state, had 5.939969 km error, RX0
-2.558288 and RX1 -129.154834 Hz/s. Its 236 admitted receiver pairs had median
absolute residual 5999.946750 Hz and robust residual slope +125.576855 Hz/s.
These are nearby stages of the same bad branch; the precise values should not
be conflated into one exact vector. The quoted approximately 5.9 km / 6000 Hz
description covers this branch, not a measured -129 Hz/s hardware clock drift.

The repair
----------
The same-RF paired observations estimated the additional relative RX1-minus-RX0
frequency slope as -1.054801 Hz/s after accounting for frozen calibration.
Preserving RX0's initial -2.558288 Hz/s and setting RX1 to -3.613089 Hz/s made
the initializer agree with that differential estimate. Frequency offsets were
adjusted analogously. Position, satellite timing and c were initially preserved.
Both anchor choices were tested with equal budgets and selected by model score;
the known reference position did not select the anchor.

Then ordinary optimization released the fitted parameters under the unchanged
independent zero-c likelihood, with relative satellite timing sigma = 1 s.
No hard slope bounds were used in this repair.

Selected result: position error 4.502847 km; median absolute paired residual
67.230545 Hz; converged (KKT 0.000156, threshold 0.001); additional RX0/RX1
slopes -2.064829/-2.523213 Hz/s. Pair residual p90 is 134.809618 Hz and robust
residual slope -0.561307 Hz/s. Better receiver consistency still leaves a
multi-kilometre localization error.

What the three slope windows establish
-------------------------------------
Every +/-60, +/-30 and +/-10 Hz/s window would reject the bad source vector
with RX1 near -129 Hz/s. Every window permits the repaired final vector.
That is a fixed-state feasibility statement, not proof that bounded optimization
recovers the repaired solution or its 67 Hz pair residual.

The later slope-prior screen did use this same capture and matched evidence
hashes. However, V16 was limited to the union of its two original winning
regions: point:-68.75:-106.25 and point:225:25. The earlier clock repair used
point:-106.25:-81.25, radius 25 km, with that region's bank and calibration.
The later experiment included this region only for T1AT, not V16. It also used
original association starts rather than the paired-clock initializer.

Therefore the later V16 zero-c errors (unrestricted 24.628 km; +/-60 24.960 km;
+/-30 24.860 km; +/-10 24.603 km) do not show that adding slope limits worsened
the 4.50 km repair. They test different V16 search regions/starts. Nor do they
demonstrate a useful gain from slope restriction over their own matched control.

Answer: the quoted improvement came from measurement-informed clock
initialization, not from a frequency-slope limit. A direct bounded-vs-unbounded
test of that exact repaired region and initialization has not been performed.
Any such comparison should freeze the same region, candidate bank, baseline,
paired measurement set, sigma = 1 s, starts and budgets, retain both c arms,
and report paired consistency separately from frequency fit and position error.
