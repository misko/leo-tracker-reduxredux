Root-cause investigation: the 2026-10-06 18:38:49 V16 297 km failure
Capture scan-fw-6bf407cfe8158445. All work is offline and read-only against the
original source/checkpoint/TLE ports; only this research report tree is written.

Finding
The original pipeline found a near solution but scored it worse than a distant
solution because it retained costly satellite-specific timing modes. The final
continuous optimizer was genuinely trapped in a local minimum. Its numerical
convergence was not evidence that it had adequately searched alternative timing
hypotheses. Sharing the zero-c solution exposed this failure but did not explain
it; the following audits identify and intervene on the mechanism directly.

1. Reproduction rules out timeout or changed inputs for the near failure.
The trajectory audit reproduces all four original V16 near-basin fits exactly,
from the original association/zero-timing starts, with the original 20-second,
300-iteration budgets. Input, analysis, TLE and deployed source hashes match.
Both associated arms finish with optimizer-success and pass stationarity checks.

2. Upstream association and downstream refinement prioritize different things.
The T1-AT discrete selector prioritizes assigned-window count minus a per-satellite
charge; absolute timing and Gaussian residual terms break selection ties. It
does not select modes using V16's 0.15-second relative timing prior. V16 then
receives that selected bank and timing initialization. Responsibilities remain
soft and can change, but no explicit search over individual timing alternatives
occurs during the local continuous fit. The alternate original zero-timing start
resets all timings together; it also fails to recover the lower-score solution.

3. Four concrete weakly supported timing modes survive almost unchanged.
Catalogue hypotheses 67112, 62144, 58803 and 62753 retain their upstream absolute
timings within 0.10 seconds. Their final relative shifts are +6.568, -6.485,
+4.821 and -4.445 seconds, with effective posterior support only 53, 56, 61 and
23 windows. Relative to sigma=0.15 seconds, these are very expensive. They are
model hypotheses; this analysis does not independently establish their identities
or prove physical clock/ephemeris errors of those magnitudes.

The original near fitted-c solution has BETTER data NLL than the far winner:

4. The optimizer is stuck for a measurable reason.
336 finite-difference checks over 16 starts/fits found no gradient defect or
visibility crossing. Interpolation-knot secant errors contract at smaller steps.
The near bad solution has a positive local Hessian at two finite-difference
scales and is interior to the bounds. Along a feasible line toward the saved
lower-score zero-c solution, the objective initially rises by 471.635 points and
effective signal mass drops by 183 before the lower-penalty alternative wins.
The full fit must shed/reassign some satellite support to escape this timing
configuration; repeatedly starting at the same stationary point cannot do that.
This diagnostic path does not establish that every possible route has the same
barrier. The positive local curvature independently supports a local minimum.

5. Simple rescaling is not a supported repair.
Matched tests from the original associated starts changed the optimizer's c
scale from 200 to 20, or reduced all parameter scales by ten. The first improved
the free objective but still lost to the distant published winner; the second
worsened both arms. No scaling change is recommended from those tests.

Causal intervention: reconsider timing modes without cross-arm warm starts.
mode_escape.py takes each arm's OWN original associated fit. In the same frozen
catalogue order, it tries setting one satellite's absolute shift to the mean of
the other shifts, re-expresses the common/zero-sum timing coordinates, and keeps
the change only if the complete unchanged V16 objective decreases. Every original
observation and satellite remains, and RF/receiver/position parameters are held
fixed during this sweep. Both arms receive 31 trials and one 20-second/300-iteration
refinement under the same bounds. No reference position enters those decisions.

The discrete sweep alone beats the original far solution's objective, without
moving position at all. Subsequent free refinement beats the original far
selection score by 1649.345 points and locates at 4.796 km error. The major score
improvement comes from lower timing penalties even though aggregate frequency
data NLL worsens. Lower posterior RMS reflects a changed responsibility set;
it does not establish better prediction. The experiment took 27.961 seconds
including reconstruction; final continuous fits used 6.50 and 6.35 seconds.

This is a mechanism test and a promising targeted search change, not a validated
production fix: it adds continuation work to the original fits, considers one
capture and basin, and reuses upstream full-capture calibration/association. No
same-total-budget end-to-end or held-out comparison has been performed. The
roughly 5 km residual error and large timing strain on some strongly supported
satellites remain unexplained physically. A timing search improvement does not
validate the catalogue identities, timing priors, or measurement covariance.

Recommended implementation direction
Make discrete association/timing decisions aware of the final model's score,
and explicitly allow individually costly modes to be reconsidered. Test the
bounded timing-sweep idea from original pipeline starts across the frozen
capture cohort with equal total budgets and matched c0/free controls. Preserve
data-fit, total-score and position-accuracy metrics separately. Independently
check the physical explanation for the remaining many-sigma timing corrections
before treating these outputs as calibrated Kalman measurements. No production
implementation or deployment was made in this investigation.
