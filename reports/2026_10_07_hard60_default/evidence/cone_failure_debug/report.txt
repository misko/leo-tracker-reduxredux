1. WHY THE ORIGINAL CONES DID ALMOST NOTHING
The learned cone describes which receiver sees a signal GIVEN that at least one
receiver sees it. It is not an absolute probability that the satellite is visible
to the equipment. Both absolute reception propensities can be tiny while the
conditional receiver probabilities are both0.5. The additional0.1 floor limits
rejection, and preserving total candidate detection mass cancels a uniform
penalty across all satellites. These are verified semantics, not a coordinate
rotation or derivative bug. The nominal70-degree-elevation axes also overlap
near zenith: opposite east/west is not equivalent to behind the antenna.

We tested removing normalization and applying a sharper absolute cone gate with
the frozen cone parameters. All final selections converged. The sharper gate
improved12:39 fitted/zero errors7.056/7.059 ->4.840/5.034km, but moved14:39 fitted
error29.197 ->138.808km. Its comparable uniform frequency likelihood worsened in
both captures. It changes the detection model and promotes a different bad bank;
it is not a general repair. Do not adopt hard east/west rejection from this test.

2. THE SPECIFIC SECOND-CAPTURE FAILURE: IDENTITY AND RECEIVER DRIFT CONFUSED
Each original observation chooses a satellite independently in the final mixture.
The receiver offset/slope terms can absorb different mistaken assignments on the
two receivers. Convergence only certifies a local stationary fit of that model.

We extracted actual retained top-one pairs on the same visit/probe/exact RF,
with inferred acquisition pilot/frame epoch separation<=100ns:173 pairs in12:39,
236 in14:39. Pair admission uses no frequency-agreement test and no orbit or known
position. These are inferred signal epochs, not processing-window timestamps.
Actual frequency support centroids differ by at most90/57ns, negligible here.
Common750Hz pilot phase is soft common-emission evidence, not decoded satellite ID.

In the original14:39 fitted-c solution, these observations contradict the fitted
receiver difference by a robust -5.520Hz/s residual trend. Same-RF c cancels, so
the discrepancy directly tests receiver/assignment consistency rather than c.
The earlier frequency-width continuation produced an apparently attractive5.899km
zero-c solution, but its paired residual median was6000.6Hz and trend+125.59Hz/s.
Its extra RX slopes were approximately-2.56 and-129.15Hz/s in the paired successor.
That near position was not evidence of a sound physical fit.

We fitted the differential receiver intercept/slope directly from the paired
measurements, without orbit or reference position. Keeping RX0's own offset and
slope while initializing RX1 from that measured difference escaped the bad mode.
We also tried the opposite anchor with identical budgets; it lost by score.
Position, satellite timing, c and all observations/candidates were preserved in
the initializer. No solution was passed between c arms. The location was then
allowed to move normally during optimization.

3. WHAT THE PAIRED LIKELIHOOD CHANGES ACHIEVE
Two tested research models replace the independent likelihood for an admitted
pair with one joint likelihood; the measurements are never counted twice.
The first favors a common catalogued satellite when both measurements are real
signals, with10% independent-identity escape. It preserves total prior mass.
The second also permits a common unidentified emission when neither measurement
is explained by a catalogued satellite. Its unknown frequency is uniform over
the alias interval, but the receiver difference still supplies clock evidence.
This added gradient acts only on receiver corrections, not position/orbit timing;
same-RF global c has zero added gradient. The90% sharing assumptions are research
choices, not measured identity probabilities or validated deployment settings.

4. WHY THE FIRST CAPTURE REMAINS UNSOLVED
The main cone-conflicting candidate61946 has substantial coherent support:
273 effective signal windows, four bootstrap tracks with228 active memberships,
and both receivers across late channels1/2/4. Only eight hard-assigned windows
have an alternative satellite posterior>=0.2. It is not just isolated noise.
Candidate58947 also has a coherent late track;48396 in the second capture has
much weaker cross-receiver corroboration. None is a decoded identity.

Integer CFO alias representatives do not fix this: adding the227272.727Hz period
leaves the circular likelihood unchanged. Timing resets/grids and temporary
500->250->125Hz frequency continuation return the same7.06km first-capture mode.
A joint quadratic receiver correction improves its error to4.622/4.741km, but
an independent paired-frequency time-holdout diagnostic worsens162.7->195.9Hz
under a generic quadratic. That is insufficient evidence to adopt curvature.
Stronger cones also help this capture alone while severely harming the other.
The physical cause of the remaining common frequency/identity/position bias is
therefore unresolved; the two captures should not be assigned the same cause.

A final read-only coverage check also argues against blindly enlarging the bank.
At the14:39 fitted background solution, the regional bank has860 candidates
versus34 selected. Adding omitted candidates with a small timing grid finds a
two-receiver frequency match for153/154 previously unexplained pairs. However,
arbitrary common frequency shifts of5 or10kHz still find151–153/154 matches.
The witness IDs scatter across123 satellites, at most four pairs per ID. This
dense-bank coincidence rate does not identify a missing real satellite; track
coherence and independent evidence are necessary before adding such hypotheses.

5. IMPLEMENTATION DIRECTION SUPPORTED BY THESE TESTS
Retain the1s timing prior. Use paired receiver differences to initialize and
check differential receiver clock states before accepting a spatial fit. Try
both receiver anchors, retain incumbents, and rank by the full objective.
Carry common unidentified signal evidence explicitly when satellite identity is
uncertain; do not silently discard its receiver-clock information as independent
noise. Preserve alternate timing/c modes and expose a failed nested-model check.

For continuous tracking, these checks should inform measurement acceptance and
receiver-clock state estimation. They are not yet calibrated thresholds or a
validated Kalman measurement covariance. A near ground-truth point with an
inconsistent clock, or a converged low-score point, is not sufficient validation.
The remaining work concerns coherent track identity, candidate-bank coverage,
and common receiver/frequency model errors. Independent or held-out evidence is
needed before selecting a more flexible model based on these two bad examples.
