# Frozen-method acquisition transfer and stopping decision

The root authorizes metadata preparation for the twelve validation recordings
already fixed in `split.json`. Scoring requires a separate source/hash-bound
receipt after the development outcomes and protocols are sealed. This is an
application of the existing two prototypes, not a third tuning round.

Use the original fixed panel rule: CH1 and CH4 controls, CH5–8 target channels,
four native-counter fractions per recording and both receivers. Keep missing
cells in the denominator and never substitute a more favorable target. Use
only original, strictly earlier observations as donors, with the exact R01
predictor and exact R02 conservative quadratic/fallback transformation. Reload
serialized prediction objects before native metadata enrichment; independently
verify stream labels, integer hardware generation, source ownership and exact
numerical equivalence to the frozen methods.

Evaluate the unchanged R01 point/search and R02 consistent-linear, quadratic
and descriptive legacy controls separately. Do not choose the best method per
target, feed recovered measurements back as donors, alter a gate, combine a
union into a new algorithm, or use geography. R01 search must preserve durable
selection before disjoint confirmation. R02 uses its frozen full-window point
policy. Retain original acquisition and add only absent native owners when
reporting operational coverage. Original and new outcomes have different
selection/support exposures and must remain labeled.

Verify unchanged runtime and original paired-detector parity authority,
current native index/IQ/generation/receiver identity, and full public-reader
exhaustion for each requested window. Reused detector parity is global evidence,
not a claim of a new blind detector replay on each validation window. A bounded
worker may reuse one verified raw read for both frozen methods. At most two
numeric workers, BLAS1, 580 seconds per recording, no outcome-based retries.
Record every failure, domain cap and unavailable donor.

Report per-recording, channel and receiver counts: original detections,
confirmations, continuity-compatible admissions, genuinely new native owners,
retained/lost original detections, alternate pilots, FFT-bin innovations and
read/scoring runtime. Apply the existing additions-only graph construction
separately if new owners qualify, retaining all original measurements. Do not
equate a pilot detection with correct satellite identity or independent precise
Doppler. These recordings were exposed to earlier studies; validation is held
out only from this revision and does not test a new geographic site.

The sparse development position test already shows that a few additions alone
do not support a strong localization claim. The separately frozen 24-second
coherent application on scans 0011 and 0097 is the final discriminating test
of persistence and positional information. If it produces qualified graph
evidence, compare fresh baseline and additions using matched c=0/fitted-c
arms, full causal catalogue, original regional search and conservative frequency
weight sensitivity. Freeze those downstream inputs before GPS evaluation.

Conclude this goal with either a validated localization improvement satisfying
the original held-validation criterion, or an evidence-backed negative finding
that distinguishes missing arrival-time proposals, unstable history identity,
frequency precision, lack of curved persistence, and lack of new positional
information. Do not promote an ALGO version from detection counts or two
development scans. If dense development additions remain ineffective or
weight-sensitive, avoid a broad position campaign that merely repeats the
same unresolved frequency-information problem. Publish all conclusions and
the narrow next experiment, retaining the baseline algorithm.
