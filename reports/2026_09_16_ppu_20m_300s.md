# Direct PPU Ethernet capture at 20 MS/s, 300 seconds of delivered IQ

16 September 2026, 14:57:06–15:03:35 UTC. User-authorized bounded experiment.

**Measured source-counter coverage was 78.125%, with 62.22 MB/s payload throughput.**
The first 300 seconds of source time had 78.4% coverage. This reproduces limited
throughput without adaptive decisions or IQ disk writes, but does not isolate
the responsible transport stage or prove a physical Ethernet limit.

The PPU probe used direct-async metadata ABI 3, HOLD mode, 1-million-sample
blocks, 50 attested DMA buffers (200 MB), no RAM extension, and drop-backlog on
overrun. IQ was decoded and discarded; every delivered block's counters and
gap metadata were saved. There is no retained waveform for scientific replay.
The duration means 300 seconds of delivered IQ, not a 300-second wall-clock stop.
Every internal counter gap closes against its reported missing-sample count;
received samples plus missing samples equal the observed source span.

The production scanner's in-progress capture was allowed to finish. The
supervisor was then stopped for exclusive radio access. PPU restored the
original settings exactly, verified temporary iiOD removal and stock endpoint
health, and the supervisor restarted at 15:03:38 UTC, active/running.

## Runtime and comparison

This successful run used the deployed iiOD binary, staged through PPU's
serial-attested userspace lifecycle on Ethernet port 30432. Its SHA-256 was
`daff83f62708514169d08a2ca91210d9eda63a066d774fdee738f5bf159c737d`.
Host libiio was the receipt-verified `948d6a246bb937fe3a62ed15233d9f592ab71d6a`
runtime from production release `af88cba2ec302eae9dfc8553f04e647fde12d7be`.

Two setup attempts returned no IQ: the already-closed scanner endpoint refused
the initial connection; the stock firmware endpoint admitted the geometry but
returned EINVAL on its first refill. The latter restored settings successfully.
Their evidence is retained separately, and neither contributes to these metrics.

Follow-up source inspection explains the stock rejection: v0.49's bundled
libiio/iiOD `5cb2389719d46d12463daa0371d1fda19eb25fa7` limits finite direct
requests to 4096 frames; `rw_buffer()` returns EINVAL above this limit. The
newer runtime permits 8192 after commit `0d60b270ee28545f799b3dd8154e291f5f388157`.
This experiment requested 6000. The mismatch is a peer-limit negotiation and
deployment compatibility issue, not evidence of an FPGA/DMA fault. Both peers
use metadata ABI 3; that flag does not establish their frame-target limits.
Tracked with full reproduction and proposed boundary tests in
[libiio #8](https://github.com/misko/libiio/issues/8) and
[plutosdr-fw #102](https://github.com/misko/plutosdr-fw/issues/102).

The [earlier 30-second test](2026_09_12_ethernet60_measurement.md) measured
73.58 MB/s and 100% coverage over its finite observed span. Its runtime differed,
and buffering concealed the input/output deficit over that short interval.
This new test confirms the lower approximately 62 MB/s throughput also occurs
in standalone PPU with the deployed iiOD, without adaptive processing or IQ
storage. A controlled runtime comparison is needed to explain the difference
from 73.58 MB/s; this experiment does not establish that cause.

The scanner's roughly 18–19% duty counts retained uninterrupted 120 ms visits,
whereas the 78.125% here counts all delivered source samples. HOLD has no channel
hops. Its long contiguous runs and 2.4-second backlog drops cannot be assumed
to match the scanner's gap distribution or retained-visit duty.
