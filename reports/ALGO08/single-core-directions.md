# ALGO08v2 on one core

ALGO08v2 promotes the two-scan receiver used for the optimized arm in the fixed 16-recording DS30 panel. The compact v5 solver and its search region are the same as v1. Both builds returned the same 16 fixes and all 32 fitted and fixed-zero endpoints agreed exactly. Median paired elapsed time fell 6.3%. The run used up to 20 concurrent recording pairs, each pinned to one core. These observations describe that shared Gauss workload. [Bench report](ds30-optimized/README.md)

## Where a redesign could pay

Summing process CPU across the 16 v2 recordings gives 2,264.8 seconds for acquisition and 1,769.9 seconds for the solver plus diagnostic grid. That is 56.1% and 43.9% of 4,034.6 seconds, or an average of 252.2 CPU seconds per recording. The acquisition clock includes reading, decompression, integrity checks, Python orchestration and native detection. The solver clock includes orbit preparation and the grid. Neither measures PNG encoding. [Recording-level timings](ds30-optimized/runtime.csv)

At these shares, halving acquisition alone gives about **1.39×** total CPU speedup. Halving the solver plus grid alone gives **1.28×**. Achieving **2×** by changing only acquisition would require eliminating about **89%** of all acquisition CPU. These are arithmetic bounds at the observed workload mix. No proposed redesign below has a measured speedup.

## Ranked experiments

Rank reflects which experiment to do first, combining potential savings, the size of a decisive test, and the risk of losing fixes.

| Rank | Change | Work it could remove | Smallest test that could rule it out |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Only rescan a residual that could contain a verifiable second carrier.** V2 already skips cancellation if the first pass fails. For confirmed first carriers, try a cheap residual bound or probe before the second timing search. | A full search on single-carrier visits. A bound on the actual acceptance statistic could preserve exact decisions. A heuristic needs independent validation. | On saved two-carrier, single-carrier, weak and empty visits, count missed *verified* second carriers and compare probe cost against calls saved. Include cases where the runner-up later owns a track. |
| 2 | **Keep two peaks from the first timing search and fit them jointly.** Verify them against the original samples. Use the existing residual search for ambiguous or nearly coincident peaks. | Most second searches and full-window residual buffers, even on some two-carrier visits. | Sweep relative power, delay and CFO separation on saved mixtures. Check whether the weak carrier appears in the original shortlist at all. A carrier hidden under the strong peak is a decisive counterexample to unconditional replacement. |
| 3 | **Compress a Doppler track into local fit statistics.** For a fixed association and noise model, accumulate the small normal matrix, right-hand side and residual sum once; reuse them during compact position updates. | Repeated passes over thousands of observations as the solver revisits a few parameters. | Replay one saved solver input. Compare objective, gradient and both endpoints against v2 at the center, near a boundary and after an owner change. Rebuild statistics when the linearization, owners or weights change. |
| 4 | **Share proposals across antennas.** Search one lane, verify its shortlist independently on both, and do a full search of the second lane when its independent evidence is unexplained. | Duplicate timing and frequency searches on simultaneous samples. | Swap antenna order on saved visits with strong lane imbalance. Count second-lane-only verified carriers and downstream owner changes. Preserve independent amplitudes, delays and confirmation. |
| 5 | **Replace five fixed solver refreshes with a convergence test.** Stop when associations, position and objective stabilize; retain the v2 budget when any test fails. | Late refreshes on easy cases. This can simplify the convergence contract as well as save CPU. | Trace the five existing rounds on a saved solver input. Start with a case where ownership changes late. Check both calibration arms, withheld decisions and boundary solutions. |
| 6 | **Use a joint Doppler-curve solver.** Fit position against whole tracks, profile out conditionally linear clock/calibration terms, and revisit competing satellite assignments when their likelihoods overlap. | Repeated construction of related nuisance fits and parts of the multi-stage association chain. | Start with frozen v2 owners to isolate numerical error, then remove the owner oracle. Include track swaps and a wrong offered region. Keep prior, robust-weight and determinant terms wherever they change the objective. [Variable projection](https://www.nist.gov/publications/variable-projection-nonlinear-least-squares-problems) |
| 7 | **Stream cancellation straight into the next search.** Fit each block amplitude, then feed its quantized residual into the timing front end without copying an entire interleaved window. | Memory traffic and intermediate storage, with the same two scientific searches. | Compare the exact residual samples the second detector consumes, including clipping, partial blocks, both pilot edges and both lanes. Time just this step; the buffer may be too small a share to matter. |
| 8 | **Track in a narrow window after independent acquisition.** Predict the next visit's CFO and timing uncertainty per channel. Widen or reacquire on a gap, retune, unexplained residual, or failed verification. | Repeated global searches on persistent tracks. | Start mid-recording with an empty tracker, drop a visit and introduce a new overlapping carrier. Measure reacquisition delay and missed births. Use receiver evidence and capture time rather than a learned roof location. |
| 9 | **Replace the dense diagnostic grid with an adaptive grid.** Refine cells around modes and edges using a bound or measured interpolation error. An on-demand dense grid is a separate latency-only option. | Objective evaluations after the fix. Merely deferring the same grid changes time to fix while consuming the same total CPU if every plot is requested. | Time grid construction alone. Compare cell scores, secondary minima, boundary behavior and all eight PNGs on a saved fit. Record time to fix and time to finished figures separately. |
| 10 | **Try a small frequency shortlist with exact fallback.** Reuse a coarse CFO estimate to evaluate only a few plausible bins, using the full bank when uncertainty is high. | Full-band scoring for well-constrained visits. A sparse direct transform can cost more than a dense FFT if the shortlist grows. | Measure actual shortlist width and coarse-estimation cost across weak and aliased visits. Compare the exact winner and false confirmations. Benchmark the crossover on the target core. [FFTW discussion of sparse outputs](https://fftw.org/pruned.html) |

## First comparison

Start with counters and stage timers on a few saved visits and one saved solver input. Measure first-pass confirmations, second searches, unique verified second carriers, solver refreshes, and grid CPU. Use the receiver experiment with the largest measured removable cost and test track statistics independently on the solver side. An earlier timing-hint experiment recovered pilots but worsened position on both fresh recordings, so more detections alone cannot qualify a change. [Earlier waveform and position comparison](../2026_11_19_receiver_screening/acquisition-frontier/README.md)

Move to four matched recordings on one low-priority worker only after a component shows savings. Record process CPU, elapsed time, decision changes, owner changes and paired errors for both calibration arms. Use saved weak signals, interrupted tracks and wrong-region examples to challenge approximate methods. Expand to 16 only for a question the first four leave open. Claims about embedded runtime need a measurement on the embedded core.
