# Recover masked fragments once from the collapsed curved fit

The one-pass salvage nomination recovered the original three missing fragment identities on DS30-0374 and again offered all 411 stock predictive points. The paired refit failed the stock track gates. It fell back to the curved-only estimate, with 526 fewer owners and worse position error than the original solver. The independently tested linear-only add-back kept more observations and used less CPU on this case. [Linear-only comparison](solver-linear-addback.md)

The predictive add-back left 115 missing curved observations from three masked stock salvage fragments on DS30-0374. It also left 31 missing predictive rows whose satellite had lost its curved support. This single-pass recovery experiment targets both dependencies. The masked tracks are determined entirely by the collapsed fit and its track gates. Their catalogue search includes the same candidates and original coarse and fine salvage checks. [Identity audit](solver-linear-addback.md)

Run stock salvage nomination once from the already fitted curved `c = 0` problem. Every fully masked eligible curved fragment enters the original coarse catalogue search, per-fragment fine shortlist and track gates. Rebuild the causal priors and family support weights for the proposed recovered identities, then run the normal predictive entry selector at the existing fitted position. Both calibration arms use one common stock predictive refit. The existing paired curved and predictive gates control adoption. Gate rejection retains the original curved fit. No masked rows leads directly to the single predictive add-back. The policy uses the supplied region, capture-time measurements and causal TLE data. Region and recording identity have no case-specific branches or thresholds, and evaluation coordinates are scoring inputs only. DS30-0374 proposed 277 recovered observations across nine fragments. All seven original salvage proposals reappeared with the same satellite identities. Six got the same support, while one included two extra observations. Two additional fragments contributed 64 observations across new candidate supports. The rebuilt c0 likelihood offered the complete 411-point predictive set. The refit failed six c0 track gates and four fitted-`c` track gates even though both orbital phase updates settled below 3 microseconds. Thus the recovered proposals and predictive evidence never entered the published result. Final solver RMS was 101.415 Hz with `c = 0` and 101.378 Hz with fitted `c`, the same curved-only values previously observed. Salvage and predictive work took 4.81 wall seconds.

North-containing had zero masked curved rows and used the stock predictive add-back. Both ownership hashes, endpoints and RMS matched that previous add-back exactly. Solver RMS was 5.386 Hz with `c = 0` and 5.151 Hz with fitted `c`. Both experiments completed all 179 objective grid points and produced eight verified PNGs. Four targeted tests passed. The candidate failed its full-size basic control, so there was no near-edge or omitted-region replay.

On DS30-0374 the linear-only add-back had already improved both curved-only estimates and retained a 12.43-second solver saving. The all-fragment paired refit shows why successful local salvage nominations alone cannot establish that an entire recovered mask will pass both global fits. The existing retained-point gates computed before the predictive selector offer a possible way to screen recovered fragments before attempting a single global refit. No result from that variation is included in these measurements.
