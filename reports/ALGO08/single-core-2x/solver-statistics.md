# Group statistics inside the ALGO08v2 variational fit

The prototype replaced **42.5 million** observation-level residual evaluations during intermediate variational updates on DS30-0374. Saved-detection-to-grid CPU time fell from **28.39 to 28.10 seconds** against the reverse-order control with causal reuse and salvage statistics. The elapsed effect is about 1%. Endpoints moved less than 0.1 mm on DS30-0374 and less than 1 mm on the wrong-region simulation. Both kept the same final observation owners, and the wrong-region offer still produced a false fix. This result does not support a 2.5× solver redesign on its own. [Previous solver experiment](solver.md)

## Why preserve the outer phase schedule

The archived fixed two-refresh experiment ran 1.24× faster by median CPU on six matched pairs. Its independent 21-recording confirmation increased median position error from 0.674 to 0.841 km with `c = 0` and from 0.758 to 0.956 km with fitted `c`. The stable-phase experiment skipped 14 of 120 updates and kept both endpoints on six recordings. It reduced position objective evaluations by 0.89%. The one-center seed experiment showed a stronger tail risk. One fitted error moved from 0.747 to 2.136 km for a 1.12× median full-worker gain. Resuming the two-round winning branch through five updates preserved both endpoints on six archived trials and saved six of 15 initial fits. Those studies used archived same-site recordings. They supply evidence against cutting every branch or refresh to a fixed smaller budget. ## Native rewrite

For each track, the stock native backend already accumulates a weighted Gram matrix and right-hand side. During each variational update it calculates individual residuals again to get that track's normalized residual energy. For normalized design `A`, normalized response `y`, and nuisance mean `m`, that energy is `yᵀy − 2mᵀAᵀy + mᵀ(AᵀA)m`. The prototype accumulates the needed contractions once per native call. Where a track has a private design column, it shifts the response by an anchor on that column and evaluates the equivalent quadratic with the shifted mean. Where the estimated numerical error budget is too large, it restores the row operation for just that track. The terminal likelihood still uses the original observed rows. The update order, `1e−5` convergence target, covariance checks, publication gates, and regional candidate searches use the original budgets.

The first experiment used an unshifted quadratic with one fallback for the entire batch. It replayed all 25,090 intermediate updates on DS30-0374, avoiding zero row work. With centered, track-local guards, 29.6% of intermediate track updates replayed on DS30-0374 and 71.4% replayed on north-far. The difference establishes sensitivity to the actual signal and nuisance design. The floating-point guard is experimental, and changed precision updates can still move a decision near a numerical gate. The prototype generates a separate binary from the hash-verified stock native source. ## Observed parity and runtime

Runs used identical stored factors and offered regions, matched `c = 0` and fitted `c` settings, one numerical thread, and low-priority CPU 2. Both arms ran through diagnostic grid construction. The control already incorporates causal reuse and coarse salvage statistics. Grid deviations below use the 81 common stock coarse positions. The endpoint-centered grid locations shift slightly with the changed coordinates, so the complete grid differs.

On DS30-0374 both arms retained 2,905 owned points, the same seven selected salvage trims, and no cavity offer. Solver RMS was 101.535913 Hz at zero calibration and 101.519803 Hz with fitted calibration in both runs to the displayed precision. North-far retained 228 owned points in both arms and exercised a cavity offer. Its respective stock RMS values were 37.951187 and 37.533276 Hz, and the maximum RMS change was 9e−7 Hz. Both north-far coordinates remained on the offered boundary, about 52.455 km and 52.491 km from the simulated receiver. The prior scorer supplied the simulation reference after inference. The earlier DS30-0374 control used 29.71 CPU seconds. The reverse control repeated its scientific results and reduced apparent savings from 1.61 to 0.28 seconds. This scale of timing variation limits a stronger speed claim.

## Remaining cost

In the instrumented DS30-0374 control, the native backend used 9.19 of 40.50 profiled solve seconds. Its zero-cost ceiling was about 1.29×. The grid and cavity nomination each used about 4.5 profiled seconds, and their time overlaps with the native backend. Accelerating their kernels independently would save less than adding their apparent durations. At the 34.01-second unprofiled stock input, a 2.5× solver would have 13.60 CPU seconds. The first two experiments still spend about 4.86 seconds on preparation and 4.46 seconds constructing selected evidence. Retaining their measured costs leaves only about 4.28 seconds for all inference.

A cross-stage design worth testing is a single per-family physical Doppler representation with a sparse joint position/phase fit. Build the static radio design once per recording and assign orbital features to family blocks. Reuse the factorization and family block contractions for held-family alternatives and a fixed-owner grid. Broad all-catalogue nominations and exact physical checks at crossed score or acceptance margins are part of the test. This requires reducing repeated geometry and posterior setup across initial, conservative, cavity, and grid work. The current prototype addresses only one inner contraction and provides no timing evidence for that redesign.
