# What fractional pilot arrival time can resolve across a region boundary

On the saved physical DS30-0016 factors, the positive propagation relationship gives **52.26 samples** of held code–carrier error. A clock-only fit gives **25.95 samples**, and reversing the predicted range increment gives **1.75 samples**. Both receivers reproduce this ordering. The sign reversal recurs in earlier physical studies with independently audited timing extraction. It blocks use of these timing factors as a calibrated geometric range observable.

## Evidence already available

An earlier chronological test matched the original v5 owners to 9,867 native winning frequencies and fractional epochs. The compiled waveform displacement test established that the saved epoch moves with the incident pilot, and an injected physical delay recovered its original sign. The measured positive delay nevertheless lost to clock alone and to reflected delay. A second study used anonymous causal CFO association and 1,565 held intervals from four roof recordings. It measured positive-code RMS of **112–431 samples**. Its correctly phased generated control yielded **1.073 samples** and still lacked the prescribed geographic lead. A successful next-epoch forecast from the previous two epochs establishes short-term tracking coherence. The propagation sign and independent spatial information require separate tests. ## Current saved-factor check

The runner joins each Graph observation to the exact gated native factor by hardware counter-derived identity, receiver, channel, RF, midpoint and folded CFO. For each point it reduces the integer hardware counter difference exactly modulo the nominal 750 Hz frame lattice before adding the integer and fractional pilot epoch. Consecutive same-track points at most 2.5 seconds apart provide an arrival increment modulo the frame period. The first two-thirds train an independent constant clock rate per track and the final third tests it. Intervals crossing that split are omitted. At least four training intervals and two testing intervals are needed on each track. A constant transmitter epoch cancels in the increment. The controls fit the same clock rate with no predicted range, physically signed integrated carrier, and reflected integrated carrier. The frequency Graph uses the complete recording but its track membership uses no fractional epochs or scoring reference. The simulated receivers both favor the positive control. Their profiled mirrored signals are each about 0.018 samples, or 1.8 ns per held interval. This is 5–6 times smaller than the positive closure RMS even under the generated waveform. Adjacent intervals share endpoints, and receiver pairs view the same sky. The per-interval ratio does not establish how much independent evidence could accumulate. The profiled far separation remains measurable in the simulated geometry. For candidate sites separated by distance \(d\), the difference in slant range at any one epoch is at most \(d\), by the triangle inequality. For two epochs the difference in range increments is at most \(2d\). At 10 MS/s these loose geometric bounds for a 200 m shift are 6.67 samples at one epoch and 13.34 samples for an increment. The actual intervals have much smaller differential signals because the line of sight changes little in 2.5 seconds and an unknown rate absorbs much of the remainder. The matched physical check measures code–carrier closure rather than a timing-based geographic estimate. The reflected sign is an empirical control and supplies no validated physical model for extrapolating the measurement to another site.
