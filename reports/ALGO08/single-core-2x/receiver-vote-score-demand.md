# How often continuation needs a second exact score

On the complete DS30-0016 saved factor ledger, the vote rule reduces requests for exact scores from **720 to 594**. On the southern simulated ledger both policies require **456**. All 440 physical and 456 simulated retained observations reproduced their original published bytes in both candidate orders, including when all margins presented to continuation had their signs reversed. These are counts of requests on previously verified factors. Some requests in the physical receiver return immediately because the factor already carries its exact score. [Saved requests and selection digests](measurements/vote-score-demand.json)

Each candidate gets votes from past frequencies on its RF lane. With at most two verified candidates the deployed selector has three cases. When neither has three votes it withholds regardless of the scores. When their vote counts differ by at least two and the higher count is at least three, that carrier always wins. In the remaining two-candidate case it compares their exact margins, breaks a tie by scan rank and retains the winner only if it has at least three votes. The future vote calculation uses both verified frequencies even if the current row is withheld. The ordering of those two frequencies in history does not affect the count. These rules depend on the observed RF history and apply identically at both sites.

At the earlier **0.63 ms per physical recovery**, 126 avoided calls would be worth approximately **0.08 CPU seconds** on this recording if every one required a native recalculation. This projection cannot establish an end-to-end gain. The smaller test provides a bound on how much an order-independent policy can recover without relying on numerical margin bounds. The running four-recording candidate uses the original two-factor repair policy. [Measured cost per recovery](receiver-fractional-score-consumers.md)
