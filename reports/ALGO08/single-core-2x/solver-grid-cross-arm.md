# Exact sharing between calibration arms on the publication grid

The measured ceiling for exact cross-arm projection reuse was **0.137 CPU seconds** on dense DS30-0374 and **0.010 seconds** on the public northern simulation. The dense ceiling falls below the 0.25-second prototype gate even before lookup and copying. Both audited replays reproduced their frozen arms, owner maps, full numerical grids, and optimizer paths exactly.

The sampler evaluates both arms at each deduplicated east–north coordinate. Both models use the offered region and therefore have the same observer and ENU frame at those coordinates. The selected packet reconstructs one curved model per arm from the common owner decisions and that arm's final phase reference. The nominal and ±0.01 s orbital states depend on those references. The mode projection additionally depends on the same interpolated physical perturbations. Keeping orbital identities and observation times fixed does not force those projected inputs to agree. [Grid sampler](../../../src/leo/analysis/position_grid.py) · [Selected model reconstruction](../../../src/leo/analysis/localization_v3/recovery_inventory.py) · [Projection](../../../src/leo/analysis/localization_v3/marginal.py)

The fitted arm adds an RF-dependent design column and has its own phase linearization and robust posterior. The predictive handoff already shares the checked curved posterior with the predictive rows within each arm. A shared position, orbit bank, mask, or layout leaves arm-specific nuisance coefficients, weights, and factors. This audit reads the final noise precision from each arm's existing per-point likelihood cache. It independently checks byte identity of positions, position/velocity pairs, and center states with both mode arrays. It checks matches across all fixed-zero rows, including different row ordering, phase offsets, and curved or predictive roles. Position identity alone qualifies a block for the angular and range part of the bound. ## Measured ceiling

The audit permits the entire timed observer and receiver-frame budget across both arms. It also permits all time in each fitted-arm look-angle or mode kernel if it finds even one eligible row among the inputs to that kernel. This deliberately counts nonmatching rows and imposes no lookup, gather, copying, or scatter cost. The result bounds the tested opportunity for exact reuse of projected rows and scalar observer calculations. It does not rely on projection time scaling linearly with matching row count. [Audit runner](solver_grid_cross_arm_audit.py) · [Audit tests](../../../tests/pipeline_eval/test_solver_grid_cross_arm_audit.py)

Position-only and full position/velocity matches gave identical counts at all three phase offsets on each input. The physical mode inputs also had 299 exact row matches. Both pairs used identical curved masks, assignments, aliases, and orbital support. The fitted RF column was nonzero for every retained curved row, 2,496 on the roof and 285 on north. Every robust precision vector differed between arms. The 1,969 equal roof precision slots were zero on both sides, 11 masked track slots at each of the 179 grid positions. [State overlap, robust precision counts, and per-kernel clocks](measurements/solver-grid-cross-arm.json)

The roof bound allows 117.719 ms for all fitted curved look-angle and mode projections, including the roughly 90% of rows with different inputs. Predictive projections contributed zero reusable rows on either site. The full instrumented grids took 3.245 and 0.704 CPU seconds, and their frozen posterior handoff used 356 and 272 factors, respectively. The audit reproduced 2,907 roof owners and 302 northern owners in each calibration arm, with 179 and 137 complete grid locations. The replays also reproduced both endpoint positions and frequency RMS values. Three component tests cover byte identity, the ceiling's accounting, and rejection of incomplete timing coverage. The measured ceiling closes the experiment at the audit stage. [Parent publication](covariance-publication.md)
