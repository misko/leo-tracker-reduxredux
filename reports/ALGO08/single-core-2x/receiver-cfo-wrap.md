# Keep PGO for the scout and restore wrapped refinement

The wrapped refinement candidate uses the original pre-PGO arithmetic when the nine-point delay stencil crosses the 13,333-sample pilot period. The two valid wrapping intervals are `0..3` and `13329..13332`. Interior epochs and invalid epochs follow the archived PGO implementation. The rule uses only pilot geometry. The signed cancellation object, FIR and normal batched refinement remain the packed receiver's original inputs. [Native dispatcher](receiver_cfo_wrap_dispatch.c) · [Earlier attribution](receiver-cfo-lineage.md)

## Native build

The builder pins the original PGO and pre-PGO delay-product objects and relinks the packed receiver byte for byte before creating the experimental library. The PGO object keeps the existing calls to `delay_refine` from both the small-bank and large-bank paths. GNU `objcopy` weakens just that public definition and adds an alias into its original machine code. The dispatcher supplies the strong public definition and calls either the retained PGO body or the renamed pre-PGO body. Both aliases are private to the library. The build checks the published dynamic symbols and compares the instructions of the PGO batched refinement, bank entry and hot FIR before and after editing the object's symbol table. Unchanged objects include the signed cancellation kernel. [Build driver and pins](receiver_cfo_wrap_build.py)

The experimental library is `/tmp/leo-algo08v2-2x-bench/receiver-cfo-wrap-fix/wrapped.so`, SHA-256 `d97b603faaf3eb63b246706cfa8dfeca0b7e329b0cc23b227e7de946de48b0e3`. The receipt records every linker input and the complete commands. [Diagnostic receipt](measurements/receiver-cfo-wrap-trace.json.gz)

## Bounded verification

The physical check uses the same three saved DS30-0008 diagnostic windows at visits 2661 RX1 CH6, 3298 RX0 CH3 and 3869 RX0 CH6. It reuses the paired detection receipt and the original verified I/Q binding. The wrapped build matched the full restoration's FIR, ordered refinement requests, every refinement result and the complete scout output. It reproduced every exported field for all three primary factors and the two residual factors byte for byte. The primary CFO and epoch fields therefore also agree exactly with the deployed receiver. The check stops at the receiver. [Diagnostic receipt](measurements/receiver-cfo-wrap-trace.json.gz)

The synthetic check matched the numerical output of the appropriate source kernel byte for byte for fourteen requests through both the direct and bank entry points. It covers both wrap boundaries, the first and last interior epochs, signed CFO and zero, repeated interior epochs, single- and mixed-request banks, and large-bank scalar coverage. A separate linker fixture exercises invalid epochs, nonfinite CFO, invalid bounds and empty-bank semantics. [Synthetic checks and timing runner](receiver_cfo_wrap_trial.py) · [Component tests](../../../tests/pipeline_eval/test_receiver_cfo_wrap.py)

The timing comparison uses the existing 18-window DS26 weak archive, both lanes, and eight balanced repeats on CPU 2 under the Gauss lock. The wrapped build used **2.45% less CPU** than full restoration by the median paired ratio, with medians of **144.04 ms** and **147.07 ms** per 36 lane calls. All eight repeats showed lower CPU. Both arms exported the same 36 primary and 22 residual factors and identical continuation evidence. No probe in this short slice reached a continued selection. CPU time covers the normal two-scan receiver entry point. This limited sample measures native receiver cost. Wider archive performance and receiver parity remain unmeasured.
