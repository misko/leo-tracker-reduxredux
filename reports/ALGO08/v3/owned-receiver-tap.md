## The producer and verifier

The capture application already hands the sole writer a narrow `ShortWindowSink.append` call with the owned CI16 array, target, acquisition metadata and powers. The writer validates the array and stamps the ordered index, the individual window hashes, the whole decoded input hash and the compressed stream hash. It flushes the index and compressed file, writes its own fresh manifest, and synchronizes the rename. The compressed writer hashes the actual encoded bytes passed to its sink. This is producer evidence for newly emitted bytes. A valid compressed frame can still decode to the wrong logical words and have its own correct encoded SHA-256. Independent readback detects that discrepancy using the per-window and whole decoded commitments from the original input. ## Consequences for a one-core tap

A safe tap could borrow the immutable capture array synchronously through the existing native pointer and retain only provisional signed factor rows and their immutable window bindings. It would have to keep chronological hardware counters and delivery clocks, distinguish invalid visits from admitted RF/edge/channel counters, retain both antenna calls and full-anchor cadence, and defer the existing held selector until EOF. The signed result would use the newly emitted inventory, window digests and actual detector recipe. A replayed capture has its own manifest and session, even when the signed physical rows and I/Q digests match the fixture. The original reader remains the independent authority and the ordinary fallback when a tap is absent or unsupported. Failed archive verification cannot release provisional output.
