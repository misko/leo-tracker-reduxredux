# What an exact full-aperture receiver would need for half-time

The available compiler and transform work does not support an exact receiver change on the scale of the first-30 timing deficit. The current 20/8 ms successor would need **3.207×** across both complete native arithmetic paths if other elapsed work stays fixed. A model that grants the entire timed native wall scope, including preemption, still needs **2.735×**. Returning to the original 20 ms detector and retaining the charted successor's measured outside-native costs would need **3.52–5.06×** across its complete native calls at four observed all-full donor rates. The independent exact-packet control implies **3.848×**. These are break-even requirements, with no new launches or claimed receiver acceleration. The one-core field envelope is **439.759 s process CPU** or **477.043 s elapsed** around both native callback types, inside **1,043.227 s** total authenticated acquisition. The observed successor needs to save **302.627 s** from 1,696.681 s total, with both gates at 0.5. The required cuts are **68.82%** of measured callback process CPU or **63.44%** of callback elapsed. Running a faster kernel cannot be assumed to remove all overlapping prefetch or off-CPU intervals. Process CPU may include prefetch thread charges, so the arithmetic model gives that credit generously. The observed exact compiler and transform savings supply no qualifying large change on both positive and empty lanes. The earlier empty-only study already failed even at zero classifier cost. A new DSO would need verified factor and continuation parity and its own genuine authority.
