# Price short searches between two empty full checks

Enroll the genuine saved 20/12 ms packets for DS30-0016, DS30-0008 and the public North simulation, in that order, from the existing signed anchor-exposure census. Use their unchanged calibrated receiver pass/fail gates. Inspect no waveform, survey reference or position. Freeze the rule before its first replay.

On each exact RF/channel/edge stream, always run both full anchors at slots 0 and 8 and the short search at slot 4. In a complete interval where those three checks are empty, predict that slots 1, 2, 3, 5, 6 and 7 are empty too. Count two alternatives. In the first, evaluate this rule independently per radio. In the second, both radios must have all three checks empty before either can skip. Keep trailing incomplete intervals at full cost. A single-pass implementation would have to retain the potentially skipped samples until the closing full check, or pay for recomputing them. The test grants that buffering and scheduling for free.

Count short lane calls avoided, missed native positive lanes, total positive short lanes, and complete intervals containing each miss. Also price an ideal veto which knows in advance whether any of the six proposed skips in the group would lose a positive and then runs all six if needed. For the stereo rule, veto the whole group across both radios. Short-lane counts are exact. Cost estimates use measured average short-call CPU from the same weak raw packet, with an explicit equal-cost assumption. Compare with the 0.692 s reduction required by the illustrative 2.700 s weak acquisition target. Advance this schedule to implementation only if it preserves all observed positives on the three packets and projects enough savings on the weak case. Otherwise stop before a new solve or raw replay. Other ways of reducing receiver work can be evaluated independently.
