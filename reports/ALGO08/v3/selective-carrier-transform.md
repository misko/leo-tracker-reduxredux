# Selective frequency evaluation in the frozen 8/20 ms receiver

The live broad carrier search uses **2,621 of 4,096** signed output bins per 704-sample mode. The eligible outputs require every inner subtransform in the archived FFTW plan. A previous exact-full-receiver audit also found weak data-dependent selectivity. Its 2,048-point coarse bound could omit **170 of 1,968** complete carrier rows in hindsight and the 1,024-point version omitted none. On the first 30 field pairs, even making every carrier FFT free would credit only **30.432 s** at the saved scalar kernel rate, leaving **272.195 s** above half-time. The budget and exact-output constraints stop this proposal at the source and receipt audit. ## What the actual receiver consumes

The checked full binary is `2258f592…` and the checked 8 ms sparse binary is `c5b57b06…`. The full timing object is `5ffc8eb3…` from its exact relink. The 8 ms sparse source is `10d8ec75…`; the installed full and retained short verifier sources both have the same transform lengths and full-grid peak loop. The broad scout takes the same 10 MS/s CI16 lane at stride four, with the short and full scouts independently constructing the two complex modes from five and fourteen pilot frames respectively. These are native scout dimensions. The final fractional verifier determines its actual frames from the measured epoch, aperture and interpolation bounds. ## What exact pruning can and cannot remove

The archived actual scalar plan is `dft-ct-dit/32`. It uses 32 length-128 inner DFTs and a radix-32 AVX combining codelet. Splitting the time sequence by residue modulo 32 means output `k` requires each inner sequence's coefficient at `k mod 128`. The eligible carrier set touches **all 128** residues. There are 67 residue groups needing 20 of their 32 outer outputs and 61 needing 21. Therefore output selection alone can only trim calculations in the final combining codelet or stores under that plan. It cannot omit an inner coefficient or an inner DFT. This supports a dependency claim. It does not assume exact savings for an unpublished selective AVX codelet. On a simple radix-two comparison, the band also covers all 2,048 inner residues. At most 1,475 of 49,152 complex additions disappear, about 3.0%, with both halves and the last layer's twiddle product still needed for at least one retained output in each pair. Input zero-padding specialization is a distinct way to change the algorithm and is subject to the same overall transform budget below. [Archived scalar plan and transform clocks](sample-major-fft.md)

Direct evaluation at every eligible bin is **1,845,184** complex sample contributions per mode versus **24,576** butterflies in a radix-two 4,096-point reference. These are operation counts, not CPU estimates. Direct evaluation of a few bins would need a separate certificate for unqueried competitors and the exact released rounding. A length-704 complex sequence can contain a polynomial that vanishes at any smaller list of prescribed frequencies and remains nonzero elsewhere. A focused test shows two toy sequences with identical measured bins and energy but reversed order at omitted bins. The current time-only nomination, seed input energy and previously chosen signed frequency cannot prove the required first/second carrier order on a new lane. Recomputing shortlisted bins by a different Fourier summation can also change Float32 scores and near-tie ordering. [Component tests](../../../tests/pipeline_eval/test_selective_carrier_transform.py)

An earlier bound tested the stronger approach of whole-period coarse Fourier samples followed by an explicit trigonometric interpolation enclosure. The exact full weak and South traces omit 99/864 and 71/1,104 carrier rows using the 2,048-point anchors. The ideal branch-reuse model puts their complete carrier FFT opportunity at 5.73% and 3.22%. The best separate coarse-plus-original fallback orders model **26.97% and 32.04% more** transform arithmetic for the two whole scopes, even granting the first exact seeds to avoid separate coarse work where possible. The hypothetical shared half-transform also needs bounds on the Float32 arithmetic and fallback on uncertain ranks. Nothing was deployed. The result is stronger than a synthetic demonstration from a few direct selected bins and gives no target-sized opening for a second prototype. ## First-30 transform budget

The frozen first-30 receiver audit has **262,770** short calls and **37,752** full calls, including both radios and invalid-visit exclusions. The full source requires a confirmed first scout to reach the residual scout. Every one of the **18,715** positive full outputs paid that work. Some final full empties can also have reached the second scout, so the exact call count is bounded by **18,715–37,752** residual scouts. The original held selectors do no 20 ms remeasurement. For these scout counts, the carrier FFT count is **7,661,688–8,118,576**. [Sealed first-30 native counts](first30-empty-call-roof.md)

The earlier aligned standalone controls compare scalar FFTs on saved actual transform arrays and reproduced every captured output byte. The 4,096-point scalar FFT CPU was about **3.75 µs per transform**. Full donor input comes from the exact installed 20 ms binary, with independent raw and residual operands. The short donor used the older 12 ms search with the same plan shape and zero-padded 704-slot carrier support. Those are measured kernel times and transferred costs for the current 8 ms field. No current receiver per-transform measurements exist on the first 30. In the upper-count scenario below, all credited CPU reduces wall one for one while every other cost stays fixed.

| All carrier FFTs and both 4,800-point lag directions | 59.699 s | 19.73% | 242.928 s |

This grants the complete scout transforms. A true subset can save only part of that work, and the transform clocks exclude ranking, modes, filtering, refinement, the 64/128/512-point checks, intake and publication. The previous 20 ms bound's best ideal reuse fraction would be worth only about 1.74 s if it applied to every projected carrier FFT.
