# Where to look for a second doubling after the weak-fix replay

The target is half the elapsed time of the named ALGO08v3 release on one core, from original signed samples through process exit. Both the median paired time ratio and the ratio of summed times must pass. A fresh positive fix makes the remaining gap clear.

| DS30-0180 and DS30-0102, complete warm recordings, earlier time-support build | 1.772 → 0.805 s and 2.136 → 0.921 s | Both implementations withheld on both recordings. A short recording can prove before the solve that the observations span too little time. |
| DS30-0402 and DS30-0169, starting at identical saved v3 detections | 73.351 → 29.440 s and 17.893 → 11.822 s | Fast discovery and the original final fit recovered the original positions and assignments on both. Recording and acquisition costs are outside these clocks. |

The weak candidate spent 5.608 s in authenticated acquisition, 4.382 s in the decision and publication phase, and 0.508 s elsewhere. Its actual decoder input was resident before and after both processes. The separate conditioning pass cost 7.266 s and read 992 MB. The candidate incurred eight process major faults against zero for v3. Its asynchronous reader used 1.352 CPU s over eleven successful reads, while demand waited only 0.066 ms in total. More lookahead at those demand points has essentially no remaining opportunity on this run. The earlier candidate acquired this recording in 3.392 s. Even if that acquisition time repeated with the new downstream clock, the combined time would be about 8.28 s. This is a diagnostic calculation using separate runs. On the weak fix the candidate reduced the zero-calibration horizontal error from 1.644 to 1.311 km and increased the fitted-calibration error from 1.661 to 1.694 km, measured against the operator's site reference. Its RF RMS rose from 109.38 to 121.53 Hz and from 109.48 to 120.29 Hz respectively. Of 109 original and 116 candidate assigned observations, 106 were shared, with unchanged satellite identities and roles on those 106. The candidate matched the saved mixed-packet solution in both arms, grid and path. Each run emitted eight images that matched its own renderer revision's reference, both as file hashes and decoded pixels. On a dense mixed packet an additional 3.040 s in the saved process improved each arm's position by less than four metres. Two small tests narrowed the search. On the second original DS30-0169 member, direct cold acquisition took 701 ms for 78.62 MB (112 MB/s). The descriptor alone took 420 ms, against a 379 ms working allowance for the whole member. A resident repeat took 875 ms with a particularly costly descriptor read. The two runs returned identical signed factors. An archived three-member 0008 trace also showed only 21 ms between successive background read calls. Simply issuing the second request earlier offers little on that trace if the device still serves both reads sequentially. Sustained bandwidth and storage variability deserve separate treatment. The sparse three-frame repetition screen missed 13 primary detections on the active mixed receiver. Even choosing the threshold afterward to retain every observed detection would have skipped zero of 41 empty, eligible radio windows. The all-full comparator also lost secondaries. Retire this score for rejecting windows. The next receiver experiment should target expensive computations within a window or recover observations that matter to the solution. ## Ranked small experiments

| Rank | Direction | First measurement and decision |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Find out why moving resident input into the decoder is sometimes expensive. | An earlier two-buffer pool did not produce a repeatable speedup. Replay one staged member and separately time fresh allocation, first writes to pages, the read syscall and verified detection. Charge pool initialization and compare reversed orders. Retire buffer reuse if it only moves the same cost to startup. [Prior buffer experiment](signed-ingress-reuse.md) |
| 2 | Recover a weak observation where it could change the position. | Use archived full-vs-short substitutions to identify whether frequency offsets, timing offsets, missing detections or extras drive the fitted-arm regression on 0016. Then test a rule using observable competing peaks or unstable track assignment, with a full receiver fallback. Charge the new solver workload and verify it on untouched data. |
| 3 | Specialize the common positive-fix path. | Attribute the weak run's 4.382 s decision and publication phase to imports, regional proof, solver, rendering and output. Check whether lazy preparation or direct use of validated inputs can remove work on a real fix and still emit the full numerical grid and all eight figures. |
| 4 | Make the original final fit cheaper to run. | A scoped dense replay reproduced both endpoints and all 4,367 assignments per arm. All 78 optimizer requests were unique within their phase. Profile covariance and matrix construction within the actual objectives. The 2.168 CPU s replay versus 3.591 s in the archive needs a same-session control before crediting the difference. On weak 0016 qualification plus refit was only 0.130 s. [Exact-fit study](named-phase-reuse.md) |
| 5 | Carry a frequency uncertainty with each short measurement. | The earlier even–odd frame estimate had absolute rank correlation below 0.09 on both cohorts. Try the measured frequency peak's width together with the strength of the best separated alternative. Test whether these predict full-aperture discrepancies on held lanes before spending any extra scans. Compare recovery to equal-cost uniform retries. [Earlier uncertainty result](split-spectrum.md) |
| 6 | Share calculations between the primary and cancellation scans. | Identify the linear portion of the local correlations, then measure reuse on genuine secondary-positive samples. Apply the full original calculation wherever clipping, integer rounding or normalization prevents reuse. |
| 7 | Bound the expensive search before discarding a hypothesis. | For a small group of timing or frequency candidates, accumulate part of the pilot score and bound the maximum remaining contribution using the observed signal energy and known template norm. Compare the exact winning and runner-up peaks, including ties, and charge bound evaluations. Stop if bounds stay loose. |
| 8 | Keep ambiguous satellite assignments live until measurements distinguish them. | Build a shortlist using Doppler changes, score its branches on all observations in both calibration arms, and fall back when ambiguous. Measure whether the original winning branch survived proposal and the complete cost of failures. |
| 9 | Advance verification and receiver work incrementally during cold reads. | Prototype bounded compressed chunks feeding decoder, hashes and provisional DSP. Publish evidence only after every original seal passes. Price arrival-to-use overlap, memory, discarded work on corrupt input and last-member latency. Existing whole-member prefetch already overlaps some reads. |
| 10 | For future recordings, bring the storage unit closer to the receiver's unit of work. | A seekable 12 ms prefix with an 8 ms extension already avoided 35% of compressed bytes on one mixed-schedule member. Test writer cost and full-window regressions on a duplicate. Legacy DS30 conversion time belongs in its own budget. |

On the historical 52 fixes with split clocks, acquisition accounted for 67.1% of elapsed time. Across the full 55-recording planning panel, reducing acquisition by 55% and solving by 50%, holding the historical publication tail and unsplit runs fixed, projects median and summed ratios of 0.480 and 0.497. These are joint engineering targets. On the 52 fixes, dividing each original compressed size by half its original elapsed time also gives a median minimum acquisition rate of 147.3 MB/s even if everything after acquisition were free. The aggregate equivalent is 150.3 MB/s.
