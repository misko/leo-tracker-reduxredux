# Can the shape of a short frequency peak identify worthwhile full-aperture retries?

Enroll the signed short-primary and full-rank-zero rows from the existing dense DS30-0008 and weak DS26 split-spectrum study. No waveform replay. Use only the short primary's two archived exact-template half spectra for selection. The old experiment tested disagreement between their frequency estimates. Here the candidate measures local peak shape and competing peaks within each spectrum.

For each positive lane compute the smaller, across halves, of the negative second difference of log power at the peak times one minus the ratio of the strongest separated local maximum to the peak. Treat a missing separated maximum as zero competing power. Treat a missing or degenerate peak as zero confidence. In the dense cohort set the threshold to the observed lower quartile by nearest rank (ceiling of one quarter of the number of positives). Freeze that threshold from short-side information only. Apply it unmodified in the weak cohort. A positive with confidence at or below the threshold would receive a full-aperture retry. Empty lanes stay empty. This policy requires neither a lookahead nor a per-recording quota at inference.

Compare against equal-count uniform random selection among all short positives in each cohort. Price a hypothetical perfect correction to the full frequency for every selected same-signal pair (wrapped timing within two samples, signed frequency difference within 250 Hz). Measure the share of summed squared frequency discrepancy captured and the remaining RMS on all matched lanes. Separately count different-signal and absent full counterparts among paid selections. Compare references only after sealing predictions. Advance this selector only if it captures more correctable squared error than uniform in both cohorts. Estimates from these small, previously studied inputs require independent confirmation. Downstream fix rate, localization, achievable correction, and actual full-retry wall cost are outside this diagnostic.
