# Dead-work limits in the released 12 ms receiver

Removing unused FIR writes and verifier copies saved **8.86 µs per 12 ms lane** on public North and **14.11 µs** on saved weak physical clips using the median of paired repeat means. That scales to **0.105 and 0.167 CPU s** at 11,834 short calls. Both are far below the **3.49 CPU s** dense acquisition gap. Giving away the *entire* FIR and verifier-load stages at observed panel rates would still save only **1.89 and 2.21 s**. The prototype preserved the full signed factor bytes in all 660 compared calls. ## Exact bounds and observed price

- The released short timing object converts 120,000 input samples, forms eight original frame planes and reaches sample 119,776 in its late confirmation support. The quarter-rate FIR produces 30,000 outputs, while the planes consume `[22, 28095)` at the frozen rounding. The original four-output tiling requires only `[22, 28098)`. Removing the other **1,924 outputs** preserves the value, tap order and halo of every used output. The relink redirects only the timing object's FIR call to that span. The independently callable full FIR and its CPU fallback remain available. The stage test compared bits in every retained output and left sentinels outside them. Whole versus scoped FIR took **103.3 → 88.5 µs** on North and **98.7 → 82.4 µs** on weak. This standalone measurement ran full then scoped. The complete native controls rotate order. [Exact stage measurements](measurements/short-primary-dead-work.json.gz)
- The released primary has eight frames in both ordinary integer and fractional verification. The last possible fractional source tap is sample **109,577**, including the integer epoch, fractional offset bounded by ±1.5 samples, and the original 16-tap interpolation. The primary-specific loader copies `[0, 109600)`, preserving more than 16 positions beyond that tap. The separate acquisition and conditioned-verifier entry still loads its original 120,000 samples. The measured loader means were **56.2 → 58.2 µs** on North and **88.3 → 91.1 µs** on weak. This experiment found no measurable gain from the shorter copy in the staged call. Neither scout support nor verification math changed. [Original short source relink](short-signed.md) · [Prototype and source pins](short_primary_dead_work.py)
- The short call does not run a residual search. Its shared context retains synthesis and scratch storage for that separate API. Warm short context creation took **1.32–1.37 ms** per edge, once per context. The two scout modes each contribute to the timing and carrier statistics. The fixed 4,096-point carrier transform pads a possible 704 nonzero input samples, and the portable verifier uses zero padding for its interpolated frequency search. Those padded inputs prescribe the existing transform and grid. This audit found no proof that changing the transforms preserves exact outputs. The candidate keeps the same FFT dimensions, eligible bins and PCA modes. [Earlier FFT bound](carrier-fft-bound.md) · [Previous context preparation census](short-shared-prep.md)

At the North and weak panel rates, the measured scout stages scale to **9.39 and 8.74 s**, the loads to **0.67 and 1.05 s**, and the final verifier to **2.54 and 4.72 s** over 11,834 lanes. The FIR is a subset of the scout. Removing it and the load entirely is an intentionally generous bound. The complete paired gain is the relevant estimate for this prototype. The archived dense acquisition paid 32.403 CPU s, so even the more favorable granted 2.21 s leaves about **30.19 s** against the current **28.92 s** allowance if the panel clocks transfer. No dense runtime, new raw run or position measurement followed. [Dense acquisition clock](dense-full-short-raw.md) · [Six-member warm ingress evidence](warm-six-prefetch.md)
