# What can a receiver spend on the visits it skips?

Keep the original full anchor at ordinal zero in each eight-visit cycle, separately for each edge, tuning and RF. Measure the even-numbered visits on both radios. Withhold the original short callbacks on odd-numbered visits. For the first 30 and the next 16 original DS30 pairs, model the omitted time using the observed per-recording mean short-callback wall time. Keep every other interval at its measured time. Separately consider unchanged solver time and the previously published 1.4× whole-solver target. Include incomplete final cycles and all outcomes. Use the observed lane counts to bound the omitted count without reopening any raw capture.

Report summed and median paired wall speedups if all replacement work is free, followed by the maximum extra wall microseconds per omitted lane that satisfy both 2× gates. The replacement allowance includes any extra track building, second buffer access, frequency/timing search and independent verification. Candidate positions are held fixed in this timing calculation. A new receiver also needs measured coverage and position tests. [Prior two-stage budget](receiver-solver-targets.md)

## Results on the frozen DS30 pairs

| Recordings | Omitted short radio calls | Summed speedup with free replacement and unchanged solver | Summed speedup with free replacement and 1.4× solver | Replacement allowance per omitted lane for both 2× gates, with 1.4× solver |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| First 30 | 149,568–150,240 | 1.847–1.848× | 2.042–2.043× | 190–195 µs |
| Next 16 | 89,076–89,434 | 1.967–1.968× | 2.130–2.131× | 571–574 µs |
| All 46 | 238,644–239,674 | 1.890–1.891× | 2.074–2.075× | 332–336 µs |

With the original solver, even free replacements leave the 46 recordings **128–130 s over half time**, and the paired median speedup stays below 1.95×. Under the separate 1.4× solver assumption, the projected median reaches **2.126–2.127×** and the summed time has **79–81 s** in hand before additional costs. The narrower allowance on the first 30 matters for workload transfer. If a receiver checks only some omitted opportunities, the budget applies to the total extra work divided by every omitted lane. These are end-to-end cost targets. Neither the reduction in solver time nor the availability of the original positions is measured for this design. The short-call average is specific to each original timed recording. Odd visits might systematically cost more or less than its average. The ranges in the table cover uncertainty about incomplete anchor cycles, and do not cover this timing-model error. Each cycle omits the first, third, fifth and seventh short visit when present. With both radios counted, the number omitted equals the total number of short *visits* plus the number of cycles with an odd short-visit count. The observed full and short call counts bound that latter quantity. Exhaustive enumeration through three cycles checks the bounds, including partial and empty cycles. ## What the receiver could predict before running those searches

On DS30-0086, a separate masked-input test removed every odd-visit short result before building the Graph. The surviving detections generated 214 narrow search proposals covering 183 of 1,674 omitted installed positives in both frequency and timing. Six of those 183 were genuine misses of the original short receiver. Simply skipping the unproposed lanes gives up most of the omitted signal evidence. Falling back to the broad search on every unproposed lane leaves only 214 of 8,372 short callbacks available to replace. At the archived mean cost, this permits **0.280 s** gross wall savings on the recording, before charging for any directed checks. On DS30-0049 the corresponding ceiling is **0.052 s**. The 46-recording budget above assumes a future receiver can replace the work on every odd visit. This masked-input test reaches only a small part of that opportunity.
