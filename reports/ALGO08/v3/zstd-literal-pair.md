# Cross-block Zstandard literal decoding

A native eight-stream decoder reproduced every byte of two previously detached original first members. The dense DS30-0008 member exercised the mixed-block seam and paired **780 of 782 blocks**. The weak DS30-0016 member paired **782 of 782**. The checks used only fully resident private tmpfs inputs and ran off the benchmark core without timing. Both outputs matched system Zstandard and the installed reader in original order, including all 64 independently authenticated windows per member. The research decoder requires a single immutable, checksummed, dictionary-free frame with four-stream Huffman literals. Each pair keeps separate current table generations and exact reverse stream termination. At the only admitted mixed block there are no preceding sequence-coded blocks, so the normal decoder starts with the original frame header and initial sequence state. It receives the original mixed block header and payload. `ZSTD_insertBlock` lends the contiguous decoded prefix capped at the original frame window, 524,288 bytes on dense. The mixed block declares its own distinct table generation, which also enters the paired route for subsequent reuse. Complete encoded, frame checksum, whole decoded and every window check still lead to the same original commitment. Unfamiliar layouts and failed tentative native plans restart from the unchanged encoded bytes in the ordinary decoder. Profiles with another sequence-bearing block, reused mixed-seam tables or an isolated last mixed block with a declared content size use that fallback. A generated control exercises a real cross-block backreference and subsequent table reuse. The current prototype allocates a **102.4 MB decoded arena** and parses an original Huffman header again for terminal symbol widths. The untimed correctness processes peak at 454 to 458 MiB because they also allocate full stock comparable output and invoke the installed reader. Those RSS figures describe the check, and the candidate arena is the relevant known incremental allocation. The parity check loads from the unchanged strict manifest and index; a normal-entry port still needs to preserve the existing private owned-window lifetimes and reproduce these checks against the actual fused streaming reader. The earlier dense CPU comparison measured **47.646 ms** for upstream X2 isolated Huffman traversal, with **0.963 ms** for its table construction. Its stock reader spent **67.680 ms** in complete decode calls. Halving only that traversal and weighting by paired block count yields a modeled **23.76 ms/member**, or **2.52 s across 106 members**, before altered table parsing, planning, output/copy/handoff, fallback and any cold effects. The same-table-only 360/782 restriction would model **10.97 ms/member**. Reaching a full 1.0 s at 106 64-visit members requires roughly 9.43 ms of paid gain per member. The delivered 62 pairs paid 458.486 s across 5,758 members in complete decode. The remaining modeled total gap of 213.127 s after the paid map projection and hypothetical 1.5× native acceleration would require a **46.49%** reduction in that broader stage. There is no measured cross-block speed result or field-wide Huffman eligibility census. [Existing dense paid decoder study](zstd-wide-huffman.md)

The bounded comparison should use these exact two detached members in six alternating order-balanced repetitions under the common benchmark lease when available. Compare the original verified-once owned-window reader against a bounded candidate with the same immutable encoded custody and signed hash implementation. Charge all plan/table/seam setup, ordinary fallbacks, the full window handoff, final stream and EOF checks, and own each yielded array until release. Record native and complete paid CPU/wall by order, decoded/window bytes, scratch size, input blocks and whether the output arrays remain valid after another read. The historical one-shot stock comparator serves as a byte oracle.
