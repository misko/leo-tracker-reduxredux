## Why one grid coordinate does not supply a common factor

At a fixed linearization and fixed noise weights, let `H` be the common receiver and orbit precision, `g` its right-hand side, `u` the coupling to the RF column, `d` the RF diagonal including its prior and `h` its right-hand side. If both arms have exactly the same `H` and `g`, the fitted arm can use the scalar Schur complement `d − uᵀH⁻¹u` and right-hand side `h − uᵀH⁻¹g`. Its posterior covariance, determinant and conditional residual still need their own readout. Identical normalized responses, common design, prior precision and weight state would establish equality of both `H` and `g` before rounding in a new implementation. The next Gamma update uses each arm's separate conditional residual and covariance. [Existing native Schur and group-weight design](../single-core-2x/solver-schur.md)

The earlier same-packet grid audit found **zero matching full response rows and zero matching full common design rows** among 3,661 curved observations at each of 177 shared locations. The two arms shared retained track owners and initial noise weights, but their phase references differed on every retained row and none of the 14,514 final active precision slots matched. Some unweighted Gram entries agree (18,450 of 32,134 per point), and zero complete right-hand-side operands agree. Those matches are upstream of the weighted factorization. The archive's **1.000 s native / 1.200 s bound-adapter** ceilings already give away an entire more expensive peer at every shared point, including incompatible operands. The released curved grid already reuses phase interpolation, and the predictive cache saved **0.213 CPU s** across the two separately cached arms. The earlier cross-arm projection ceiling on DS30-0374 was **0.137 CPU s**. Combining the two robust objectives would need a new algorithm and independent scientific evidence.
