# Frozen crossed occupancy audit of the held-anchor selector

Use only the original sealed, unfiltered 20/12 ms North and South simulated receiver packets. Cross each packet with its unchanged roster and one common imposed dropout, keeping all admitted opportunities, epochs, radio/channel/edge identities, receiver clocks and surviving exact candidate values. The previously published offering variants reuse the same detector packets and supply no independent RF impairment control. North and South already differ in site, edge and signal environment. This crossing isolates a common **additional** availability edit within and across the two waveforms.

Before observing any route outcomes, fix a synchronized stereo blackout of complete blocks of 16 issued visits. Erase a block when the first byte of SHA-256 of `anchor-cycle-occupancy-v1:` plus the block's zero-based decimal index is below 64. The key does not include any site, receiver, RF, measurement or owner. Retain rows and original cadence. Remove every candidate on both radios of each affected opportunity. Model loss of detector outputs even at anchor slots. A treated erased anchor is a known negative of the synthetic blackout, with no claim that a real full receiver would return no factor in new noise at that visit. Treat both original and edited rosters within each site identically for complete-cycle scoring.

Apply the existing Jeffreys-smoothed held-cycle rule unchanged. Always erase all held anchor candidates before replaying training history. Compute the primary choice over every complete original cycle, with the explicit synthetic negative labels. Also compare baseline and edited predictors using only the identical untouched held anchors and their original native binary outcomes. Report results on the same tested anchors separately for each radio, plus blackout rates, lost positives, paired log-score and Brier changes, and the unchanged paired block diagnostics. Test no other masks. Treat a route flip as sensitivity to this imposed occupancy. A flip on the common native-anchor denominator shows that altered nonanchor support alone can change the choice. A stable choice is evidence for only this mask and these two simulations.

Inputs to selection contain no survey truth, offered coordinates, orbit identities, stock owner data or other reference labels. The test replays no raw or synthetic waveform and computes no regional or position solves. The blackout is a detector-output proxy and cannot evaluate missed carriers, noise and interferer-induced false peaks, or amplitude-dependent phase and CFO errors. A physical simulation of those effects would need separately generated and verified waveforms at matched geometry, which the available recordings do not supply.
