## Why the exact filter keeps every candidate

For the first, lower-middle and last times of one prepared fixed-RF curve, let `w = (t₂−t₁, t₀−t₂, t₁−t₀)`. The weights annihilate both a constant and an affine trend. This covers the scan's per-group frequency offset, linear frequency trend and the fitted-`c` contribution at that fixed RF. A common alias offset on the prepared lifted track also cancels. Any other alias choices can change the residual `r` in the equation below, with the same result for the independent contrast. [Track construction](../../../src/leo/analysis/localization_v3/tracks.py) · [Hardware and association model](../../../src/leo/analysis/localization_v3/scan.py)

The installed receiver-smooth association subtracts `q B s`, where `q` is the positive actual RF ratio, `B` is the prepared track's receiver basis and `s` is a shared coefficient vector for that radio. At unknown `s`, a nonzero column in `wᵀ B` lets one choose a scalar `sⱼ = r/(q (wᵀ B)ⱼ)` for any finite `r`. The residual of this one contrast is then exactly zero in the real-valued model, independent of position, phase and candidate orbit. Multiple options for the other nuisance terms cannot increase this lower bound. Computing the products and sums as rationals in the prepared binary64 times and basis ruled out a rounded zero on all 127 curves. The weakest maximum unit-contrast coefficient across the columns was 0.0469 on dense and 0.1430 on South, before RF scaling. [Receiver basis](../../../src/leo/analysis/research/retained_track_likelihood.py) · [Filter implementation](curvature_filter.py)

This is a bound on the independently relaxed single-contrast data loss. The solver shares the coefficients across a radio and charges their Gaussian prior. The prior contributes a positive penalty to a fit that needs a large coefficient. It supplies no hard feasible range. A joint contrast using several tracks, a certificate charged against the same full objective and a valid incumbent upper bound, or a stage that already holds the smooth state fixed could still have power. Initial phase nomination uses `shared_phase` and subsequent refinements use `receiver_smooth`; a certificate for the former needs to preserve its actual top-k ranking, phase selection and split-track logic. These measurements never converted a low individual contrast into a whole-branch rejection. [Original discovery sequence](../../../src/leo/analysis/localization_v3/discovery.py)

## What survives a joint projection across tracks

For each radio, stack `qᵢ wᵢᵀ Bᵢ` from all its active tracks into a matrix `H`. The projection onto `null(Hᵀ)` cancels the same unknown smooth coefficients for the entire radio. Each triple has already canceled its own offset, drift, common alias and fixed-RF term. The released smooth basis has 11 columns per radio. It spans the whole South triple vector at numerical rank, while dense has an exact positive dimension even before computing a singular value. The projection uses scan-wide sharing. Restricting it to simultaneously active tracks leaves much less support, particularly on South.

| Dense radio 1 | 63 | 2,736 | 14 | 11 | 52 |
| Dense radio 2 | 49 | 2,160 | 12 | 11 | 38 |

The first two dense ranks each use all 112 curves and have 42 and 40 distinct orbit phase parameters. Even if the joint triple model also projects out a first-order phase tangent for each satellite in both alternatives, at least `112−22−42−40 = 8` dense directions remain locally. This counts columns conservatively, with one phase per NORAD across both radios. A whole-row construction has more room. Allowing two separately affine pieces per active track, two sets of 11 shared smooth columns, and both candidates' first-order orbit phase columns leaves lower bounds of 4,344 dense and 183 South dimensions. A common alias per modeled piece is absorbed by that piece's constant. An unresolved choice of alias at every epoch can invalidate that model and would require explicit hypotheses. Finite nonlinear phase motion remains outside this tangent count. Applying a fixed whitening transform before constructing the nullspace would preserve these dimension bounds when the transform is invertible. It changes the projected scores and must follow the relevant original objective. [Profile and whitening](../../../src/leo/analysis/research/retained_track_likelihood.py)

Rebuilding the small projected matrices from the sealed native epochs, checking exact witnesses and computing numerical ranks cost 17.02 ms dense and 2.39 ms South. Standalone signed-row selection and joining cost 80.06 / 9.07 ms. Initial import of the research package cost 0.579 s once. An earlier timing included that first import in the dense matrix scope. The two measurements ran under the shared lock with no propagation, new grid or fit. Two additional component tests cover dependent columns and the phase and split-affine dimension accounting. [Joint census](curvature_joint_dimension.py) · [Dimension receipt](measurements/curvature-joint-dimension.json)

A bounded follow-up would use just the first two genuinely scored dense finalists. Seal each branch's finite position, original ordered track assignments and aliases, orbit reference and score state together, then compare the joint projected data residual on common native rows at those two exact states. Include several samples per curve for the South transfer since its one-triple-per-track nullspace has no measured dimension. Extract candidate phase states before scoring rather than substitute a phase for a branch whose archive only stored a prefix score. For each pair compare the sign and size of separation against the original score, and retain the full stock search on ambiguity or missing state. This is a proposal-ordering falsifier. Projected residuals omit prior and multihypothesis effects, and a nonzero nullity alone says nothing about useful discrimination or saved search time.
