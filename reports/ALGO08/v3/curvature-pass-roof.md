## When three-time differencing is safe

For ordered actual times \(t_0<t_1<t_2\), use \(w=(t_2-t_1,t_0-t_2,t_1-t_0)\). Then both \(w^T\mathbf 1\) and \(w^Tt\) are zero in exact arithmetic. The familiar \((1,-2,1)\) misses the latter condition when visits have unequal spacing. Choose strict time separation using only native epochs and predeclared grouping. For groups of rows, use the same fixed row weights to compute measured CFO, predicted CFO and the mean epoch. Evaluating the orbit only at that mean epoch is generally wrong even for quadratic frequency. Changing grouping affects sensitivity and cost. More than one contrast can be used through the maximum of valid lower bounds, or through a correctly whitened joint contrast. Summing overlapping contrasts as independent double counts evidence. [Exact rational component tests](../../../tests/pipeline_eval/test_curvature_pass_roof.py)

Within a single original track with one receiver, channel and actual RF, the released affine receiver offset and drift, the constant bias, the common signed integer alias and the fitted RF coefficient cancel. The installed profile adds a single integer multiple of the native period to every residual in that curve. Candidate-specific common alias choices therefore cancel in the contrast even when the original alias is unknown. The result cannot identify the winning integer alias. A branch that folds each sample independently, crosses a track boundary, or changes actual RF violates the cancellation unless every allowed row-level alias and frequency change is accounted for. Fitting `c` does not remove these restrictions. In particular its term is constant only at fixed RF. [Released receiver correction](../../../src/leo/analysis/localization_v3/scan.py:145) · [Native alias and profile](../../../src/leo/analysis/research/retained_track_likelihood.py:27)

The smooth receiver term is a projected Gaussian basis, with more freedom than a constant and a linear drift. A three-time contrast normally leaves some of it. An exact fixed-state proposal can subtract that state's original smooth prediction once. A joint spatial or phase cell must carry an enclosure of compatible smooth coefficients, or use additional rows with weights orthogonal to the allowed basis. Changing absolute phase, orbit modes, and spatial projection also changes actual curvature. The bound must keep these changes and actual time and horizon support in its interval. Three valid sampled elevations do not certify all-time visibility. [Smooth receiver basis](../../../src/leo/analysis/research/retained_track_likelihood.py:67) · [Released smooth subtraction and visibility](../../../src/leo/analysis/localization_v3/scan.py:215)

If the predicted contrast is enclosed by an outward-rounded interval \(I\), the squared whitened residual at that state is at least \(d(w^Ty,I)^2/(w^T\Sigma w)\) under the original fixed covariance \(\Sigma\). This is Cauchy–Schwarz with the original row weights. For deployment the numerator and denominator also require certified rounding. The associated white-profile RMS and cost gates must keep their respective units and constants. A fixed-covariance bound does not carry over automatically to separately reweighted Gamma scores. The included arithmetic checks cover the conditional contrast identity and interval calculation. They do not certify a physical orbit enclosure. Choosing a contrast after seeing a development result can retain the inequality if each candidate contrast is valid, but it invalidates an independent test of selectivity. Freeze row groups, minimum spacing, allowed state ranges and fallback before opening alternate results.
