# Exact installed-detector timing budget on ten verified field pairs

The full installed detector **alone** fits the two 2× gates in the same-recording anchor-rate model. It misses both after adding the successor's paid solver CPU, even with free ingestion and publication. A favorable correction using the complete exact DS30-0016 receiver measurement clears both if ingestion is free and misses both once even the optimistic retained-ingress estimate is paid. The field receipts do not isolate full installed detector CPU or the successor solver on its exact packets, so neither model proves a hard detector floor. ## Actual accrual and explicit models

This snapshot uses the ten verified field pairs with recorded stages at enrollment, including DS30-0124, DS30-0086, both withholds on DS30-0107 and the installed solver failure on DS30-0397. The failure still published its bound signed native packet and retains its real charged denominator. The complete-long sensitivity uses only the seven pairs with complete named publication and more than 2,000 valid radio lanes. Neither scope forecasts the full cohort. The gates are both a median of paired candidate-to-installed elapsed ratios of **at most 0.5** and a ratio of summed times of **at most 0.5**.

The scorer checks both recorded attempt digests and both full native packet digests. It reconciles the original recording authority, detached offer and cutoff identity, original stereo visit metadata and declared full installed binary identity across arms. The charted successor runs that exact binary for its full-20 ms anchors, with process CPU timers around each native call. Projection scales those timed anchor calls to every originally admitted radio lane. Anchor selection, signal support and concurrent prefetch CPU can bias the sampled rate relative to the original all-visit detector. It is a projection. The only complete directly measured exact installed receiver control here is DS30-0016. Its **2.233 ms per lane** of separate native thread CPU was **74.68%** of the **2.990 ms** process CPU per lane on the same recording's mixed anchors. Transferring that ratio across cases is a favorable sensitivity test, with no assertion that the bias stays constant. The modeled full-detector CPU is charged in seconds against wall on one CPU. The successor's real solver clock is nested in decision and publication. Each row charges one or the other. Its solve and publication times come from the mixed packet and the observed scientific outcome. Applying them to the installed packet is an explicit assumption, especially where ranks, support or the route differ. The original installed launch-to-native-file clocks provide an observed envelope, including input verification and startup. Those envelopes do not isolate native CPU. The earlier cohort stage audit priced improvements inside the actual mixed pipeline. The table here retains that evidence and adds a counterfactual with the installed full detector. [Mixed-path stage audit](full_successor_stage_budget.py)

The optimistic ingress remainder subtracts the mixed native process-CPU envelopes and the full prefetch-worker CPU from acquisition CPU. Their scopes can overlap, so this intentionally removes some CPU twice to leave a conservative remainder for the measured mixed route. Treating that remainder as a proxy for a new reader gives away all storage wait, prefetch cost, startup and extra sealing. It does not establish a floor for a future intake algorithm. Even under that model, the full detector would have to cost at most **61.50%** of the anchor projection to meet both gates with fixed decision/publication. If ingress were free, the allowable detector fraction would rise to **82.88%**. At the weak-corrected rate of 74.68%, only **38.37%** of that optimistic intake remainder fits both gates, or **65.06%** in the successful-long sensitivity. These are break-even requirements for the specified assumptions.

## An exact intake prototype using saved member sizes

Prefetch on the long observed field cases uses **10.878–14.366 CPU s** within acquisition, including kernel copy and filesystem activity. The earlier resident attribution put its avoidable growth cost inside `readv`. It found **233.8–248.2 ms** across two post-initial slot expansions on DS30-0016, with no input-block charges. That time also paid required copies. The rest of each field producer envelope remains unpriced for the reservation. Already tested exact native replacements, including the sparse carrier sweep and selective pilot-support loading, showed smaller or order-dependent gains. Guarded fractional oscillation changed some score bits. A further native replacement would need its own source identity and output-parity qualification. [Prior native optimization inventory](receiver-activation-audit.md) · [Readv and growth attribution](rolling-input-budget.md)

The new bounded reservation prototype passes a single exact maximum for the eligible signed member roster into both lazy slots. The allocator lends only the requested exact view. The original descriptor, read, digests and decoder continue to consume that view. Existing live-owner and oversized fallbacks stay in the path. The two slots still allocate on first use, so the reservation only removes later growth. The new option remains at the pool binding and is not wired into the service or any launch flag. The component fixture drives it with the authentic saved ordered speculative member sizes. It removes **2 / 3 / 8 / 6** growth allocations on DS30-0016 / 0049 / 0178 / 0019, while giving each slot exactly one allocation. This demonstrates capacity and lifetime behavior. It reads no sample bytes and contributes no measured speedup to either gate.
