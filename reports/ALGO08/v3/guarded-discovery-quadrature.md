## What a certificate can guarantee

At a fixed original state, the white curve profile is a least-squares objective minimized over discrete alias and a continuous bias with a Gaussian prior. After removing its residual-independent term, the square root of that minimum is a distance from the augmented scaled residual to the union of allowed affine bias lines. Thus an outward-rounded bound \(\delta\) on the original-row interpolation error in noise-scaled L2 gives a profile distance interval \([\max(0,\hat d-\delta),\hat d+\delta]\). Exact-rescore any contender whose interval overlaps the best admissible upper bound, preserve stable tie order, and separately resolve the original all-time visibility and RMS gate. Use separate bounds on the original compatible and ungated minima. The profile bound only applies with the original noise, prior, and track support. The earlier disjoint-contrast bound already used exact full rows whenever the nomination or the original compatibility gate could still change. It left dense paired science and finalist scores unchanged while cold rank went from 5.085 to 5.106 CPU s. On weak DS30-0016, it went from 0.123 to 0.128 s. Some cold diagnostic hashes changed in both pairs. [Certified profiles and diagnostics](full-family-amortized.md) · [Fixed-region North profile comparison](../single-core-2x/solver-nomination-bound.md)

An adaptive knot rule based on endpoints, observed CFO curvature, and midpoint interpolation error cannot supply \(\delta\) for every allowed orbit. For a fixed phase the released ECEF state is piecewise linear in time and radial velocity is a quadratic numerator over the square root of a quadratic range. A geometry certificate could bound derivatives or the residual over each covered state segment, splitting at orbital knots and at the offset queries used in the phase tangent. It would also have to enclose horizon signs and finite-precision score and alias decisions. Recomputing every omitted projection to validate the interpolation pays the geometry cost in full. An earlier orbital-bank interpolation sentinel also underestimated the error elsewhere. No measured cheap bound covers both the upstream phase updates and all omitted rows. A distinct small optimization is to tile the original full-row reductions at the repeated centre, retaining every time, original RF value and candidate. On each track the geometry, phase tangent, visibility and centered time are already fixed at that anchor. Let \(z\) be measured CFO minus nominal Doppler, \(h\) the phase tangent, \(B\) the original RF-scaled 11-column receiver basis, and \(u\) the per-receiver smooth coefficients. In real arithmetic a centered residual at another receiver seed is \(z_c-B_c u\). The new RSS is \(z_c^T z_c-2u^TB_c^T z_c+u^TB_c^TB_cu\). The mean, time cross and phase cross have analogous linear updates. A native full-row reducer can start by fusing those reductions with the original operation order. The factored version would also need a binary64 enclosure and exact replay at affected proposals, ties and gates because cancellation in the RSS formula can change the released ranking. It must charge preparation, cache footprint and fallback. The independent same-packet cold-rank pair already reused eight of 13 nominal projections and retained 119.8 MB of centre track terms. The **entire** fused-statistics scope in that pair was **0.703 CPU s**, including corners and initial statistics. Repeated centre smooth updates are only a fraction of it. This makes the full-row reducer a possible local optimization with its own cost budget.
