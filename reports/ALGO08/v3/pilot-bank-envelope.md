# CPU available to another full-rate pilot correlation bank

The signed 20/12 ms dense receiver spent **17.351 native CPU s**, including **4.716 s** on 1,696 full antenna calls and **12.635 s** on 11,834 genuine short calls. The current first-four timing model requires **3.490 s** of additional acquisition CPU savings even at zero wait, or **20.1%** of every native operation at the recorded ingress cost. The evidence does not support another correlation-bank prototype at that scale. The coarse kernel already packs eight of nine offsets and shares in-lane work. Short convolution costs more at the actual coarse request sizes under an optimistic arithmetic model. The later verifier also uses neighboring-offset SIMD and already interpolates once for both signed templates. ## Actual pilot requests and break-even arithmetic

The 2.5 MS/s timing and carrier searches come first and keep their existing FFT banks. The following native coarse bank accepts four signed CFO and epoch seeds at normal bandwidth. Each seed needs **nine** nearby timing offsets with **eight 352-tap blocks per frame**, over 14 full or eight short frames. Existing AVX2 fills eight lanes with independent offsets, preserves the four-chain and serial reduction orders, and handles the ninth separately. Equal epochs share I/Q loads and power, and bit-identical CFOs share oscillator setup. The saved installed trace contains **592 dense requests in 148 scouts** with 63 repeated epochs, zero repeated CFO bit patterns, zero exact repeated requests and zero distinct epoch pairs whose nine requested offsets overlap. The weak trace has **288 requests in 72 scouts**, with 43 repeated epochs, two repeated CFOs and zero exact repeated requests. At most two requests share an epoch in either sample, so the existing two-request grouping already covers every measured epoch repetition. Short selection can choose different seeds. The exact short DSO already links the same bank compiled for eight frames. [Existing packed bank](../single-core-2x/receiver-coarse-bank.md) · [Signed trace](measurements/refine-bound-audit.json.gz) · [Across-visit setup ceiling](short-shared-prep.md)

For `K` consecutive valid correlations of `L` taps, an alias-free circular correlation needs at least `L + K − 1` samples of received support. The table compares scalar-equivalent real arithmetic with a heuristic complex FFT cost of `5 N log₂N` per transform plus six real operations per spectral product. It grants every template transform for free. The coarse FFT further shares one forward input transform for every observed equal-epoch pair. Coarse direct already folds the fixed CFO into the template once, so each term counts as eight real operations. This comparison excludes timing scores and received-energy reductions on both sides. It describes arithmetic, with no measured FFT or SIMD runtime for a new kernel.

At length 360 the coarse FFT model reaches break-even at **11.05 retained outputs** at the same transform size, which fits at most nine for these taps. Keeping nine instead requires sharing **48.3%** of forward transforms, versus **10.6%** observed and already served by the original paired direct bank. Length 512 would need more than one full forward transform saved per request. The integer verifier computes its five offsets with one four-wide packed stencil and one ordinary path. Keeping the two ordered complex products in that direct model raises its arithmetic to **3,080**, only 3.6% above the ideal length-48 FFT before any template preparation, layout work and exact fallback. The useful 48 support is tight and the model already grants a reordered phase/reference product. The production path separately runs five integer score spectra, then two final spectra at one interpolated vertex, including the exact and rolled normalizers and signed residual frequency. Convolution would need to preserve floating-point rounding through those reductions and tie cases instead of relying on equal complex algebra. This is a sensitivity under equal issue costs, without a hardware bound on the scalar ninth.
