Ten designs for embedded Doppler positioning

Build the streaming receiver, try a fixed satellite-consensus budget, and move the next hardware measurement to the pilot detector. The simple streaming estimator ran in 0.62 s median on one x86 core starting with saved pilot candidates. Adding the consensus step gave 2.04 km median error across 158 scans and took 0.68 s median for that complete post-detector path in a separate single pass per scan. It used up to five minutes of observed radio. The DS26 accuracy cost and the detector’s ability to keep up with incoming samples drive the decision.

Starting points and timing

DS26 contained 159 fast scans. DS26-0172 lacked a native input. The five anonymous-graph models and the ordinary streaming solver produced 158 coordinates each; v5 repeat zero completed 143 runs. The published performance report uses a stricter common cohort of 127 successes across four configurations and two repeats. A returned coordinate records numerical availability. The simple solvers still need a calibrated way to withhold weak fixes.

For the closest hardware-neutral comparison, I timed two code paths from the same saved GLRT candidate file through both calibration fits, including parsing, track construction, a fresh causal catalogue read and orbit propagation. Four predetermined recordings were each run five times with alternating order on one pinned Intel Core Ultra 9 285K core, one BLAS thread, and a busy host. The streaming version took 0.621 s median (range 0.537–0.797). The graph version took 3.704 s (range 2.835–4.947). Each timed output reproduced its earlier coordinates in both arms. The process high-water mark across alternating paths was 222 MiB. A packed float32 orbit table for a typical scan would occupy 1.12 MiB, plus buffers, tracks, catalogue and solver storage. Actual inference used floating-point host arrays.

The original v5 mean of 445 s includes additional scientific stages and figures. Across the 254 matched baseline workers, initial fitting, conservative refitting and family comparison averaged 228 s in total. Figure evidence and rendering averaged 36 s. Deleting figures alone would leave most of the computation. Both v5 and these backend clocks start downstream of raw pilot detection.

DS26 fast scans visited a pair of receivers every 44.30 ms at the median. In a separate physical PLUTO+ RAM-replay test, a sparse detector at 10 MS/s averaged 302.71 ms per dual-receiver dwell with a maximum of 424.70 ms on eight dwells repeated twice. Its 120 ms dwell schedule differs from DS26. The directed-search tests here restrict the available candidates in an archive. Only an on-board test can convert the allowed search area into a processing rate.

What the experiments could distinguish

Association. The cold solvers use anonymous curved tracks, the configured 25 km square and the newest available snapshot preceding the start, with only Starlink elements whose epoch is also within the preceding 48 hours. They fit shared receiver/band offsets and drift, an optional RF slope and a timing correction per satellite. Selection in the main compact fit is capped at 64 tracks, 12 samples and 12 possible IDs per track, followed by four reweighted linear solves. Satellite-family consensus adds 16 six-family proposals. The comparison with v5 owner labels runs afterward. Of 1,423 curved tracks with ≥80% ownership by one v5 satellite on the 32-scan panel, its ID appeared in all 1,423 shortlists. Of the 1,359 such tracks accepted by the compact solver, 1,321 selected that ID. Agreement with v5 is evidence about pruning, not independent satellite identity verification.

Causal operation. All 158 native files and graph files matched their frozen hashes. On DS26-0002, deleting every future observation reproduced exactly the tracks from the 60 s prefix. Across 158 scans the online algorithm tested 1.24 continuations per arriving winner and produced its first confirmed fragment at 3.20 s median. Final chronological linking agreed with 90.0% of offline graph points at 86.0% precision under one-to-one matching. Completed fragments are retained in the Python prototype. The eight-state cap applies to active tracks per lane.

Pilot acquisition. A detector search using up to three recent peaks per lane and ±8 kHz / ±25 µs windows retained 85.28% of original winners across 2,133,140 probes. Reacquiring every fourth visit raised recovery to 90.29% while expanding the candidate search area to ≤40.61%. On a separate 2.5 MS/s DS9 raw-IQ development set, a two-lag folded correlator with five frequency aliases put the reference timing and frequency in its top four for 103/105 positives. Verification still matters. A held-session score threshold correctly accepted 62/105 positives and accepted 3/55 reference-empty probes. The five-alias choice used this development panel. Direct access to DS26 10 MS/s raw IQ was unavailable; its front-end numbers use saved candidates.

Reference leakage and geography. Error evaluation uses the operator-confirmed site and the same distance formula as the frozen report. No GPS location, previous position or v5 identity enters cold inference. The event-only diagnostic supplies archived identities on purpose. The stationarity experiment reads prior v5 coordinates only after the anchor could have finished and supplies no current RF. As a plumbing check, a synthetic fixture generated exact SGP4 measurements with clock offsets, drift, per-satellite timing errors, noise and injected outliers for four displaced sites in each of four epochs. Both c arms recovered the correct quadrant on all 16 cases. These simulated sites exercise sign and position sensitivity; field evidence still comes from the single DS26 roof.

Decision on the device

Replay stored 10 MS/s IQ at the measured arrival cadence on the intended board. Compare full blind acquisition and guided acquisition with a blind fallback through the same streaming backend. Log maximum queue depth, dropped visits, peak RAM, time to a useful fresh fix, position tails and abstentions. A board that needs a sub-kilometer median has a harder accuracy requirement than the fresh single-scan prototypes demonstrated. A permanently fixed antenna can instead offer an explicitly aged position between anchor solves.

The bundle includes the per-case outputs, scripts and calibration-arm summaries. Reprocessing requires the frozen DS26/DS9 radio archives referenced by the scripts on the analysis host. The publication data in the bundle can be inspected independently.
