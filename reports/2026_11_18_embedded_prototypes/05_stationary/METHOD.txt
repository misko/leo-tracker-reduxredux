Stationary forecast with sparse fixes

Generate a fresh position fix every 16th member of the chronological DS26
fast-only schedule, starting at slot zero. The eight panel scans are held out in
full. At each panel start, forecast the componentwise median east and north of
up to four eligible anchors from the preceding 48 hours. Both calibration arms
share the schedule, tracks, candidate bank, proposal, selected observations,
priors and fit iteration limits. Each arm maintains its own completed fixes.
The newest eligible anchor alone is the control at the same forecast times.

The newest fix expires when its capture age exceeds 24 hours. The state then
drops its history and abstains until a fresh fix initializes it. An older capture
that arrives after a newer one is discarded. The startup forecast can use a
single anchor. Stability depends on the current installation remaining in the
same place and the anchor fixes having small systematic errors.

The fast-only membership manifest supplies capture starts. Each historical fix
uses the archived anonymous graph and the candidate/TLE inputs declared in the
shared protocol. The cold solver uses a TLE snapshot and element epochs from
before each anchor capture. The archive loader runs only once a scheduled
six-minute capture envelope has passed. Modeled fix publication follows the
later of that envelope and actual archive capture end plus 60 seconds, with the
measured paired anchor job time added to both arms. The 60-second allowance
is a replay assumption for detector and graph availability. The matched
availability cutoff is strict and the implementation accesses zero panel RF,
evaluation coordinates or v5 endpoints.

From this directory, run the following command on the repository host.

    OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1 \
      PYTHONPATH=/home/mouse9911/.cache/accuracy-repair-20261115/frozen-001/src \
      ../../../.venv/bin/python stationary.py

The same environment and `../../../.venv/bin/python test_stationary.py` run the
synthetic checks alone. They exercise a cold start, anchor expiry and reset,
failed-anchor cost, capture and fix cutoffs, the bounded history, and a 10 km
outlier or move. The median rejects the single synthetic outlier. After the
first valid relocated fix, the forecast still misses the new site by 10 km.

Foreground wall time covers completed-fix updates and both forecasts. The
historical job ledger measures host graph reads, TLE propagation, proposal and
fits and includes all attempted jobs. Standalone per-arm cost charges the
shared work once to each arm. The ten jobs used about 50 minutes of already
archived RF, and their graph-to-fix timing excludes detector and graph creation,
process startup and RF capture. All eight panel starts produce a forecast in
both arms using two or three anchors, with newest ages from 0.84 to 11.13 h.
Forecasts carry null RF residuals. The fitted coefficient lowers the in-sample
anchor RF RMS on all ten jobs, with median paired reduction of 17.75 Hz.
Geographic scoring is performed by the shared study evaluator. The replay
benchmarks a stationary installation on a host and includes synthetic outage
checks. Hardware collection and embedded latency are outside this host replay.
