Tiny coordinate network

A 51→32→2 tanh network returns east and north displacement in the fixed 25 km
search box at scan close. It consumes 49 normalized Doppler shape scores plus
the peak and contrast of the score field. For up to 40 curved tracks it samples
12 points, tests visible causal TLE predictions and retains the 12 lowest
center-position shape costs. Each candidate cost profiles a local satellite
phase offset with a 2 s Gaussian penalty. The grid score averages bounded
shape votes across tracks. The grid peak is a same-input, no-network control.
Reported hertz are in-sample demeaned RMS after satellite and phase selection.

Training uses 288 synthetic examples from six early DS26 TLE snapshots.
Validation uses 144 from three subsequent snapshots. Both sets precede the
real eight-scan panel and have disjoint TLE content hashes from one another
and from the panel. Each example places the receiver in a jittered geographic
cell, simulates exact Doppler using SGP4 and adds clock/RF offsets, receiver
drift, satellite phase errors, smooth and white noise, and distractors.
Early graph tracks provide times and RF channel schedules. Training uses
simulated positions and Doppler. The real scans use the anonymous archived
graph tracks and causal TLEs named by the shared protocol. The DS26 campaign
reference was read only after all network predictions and RF diagnostics.

The stationary RF calibration term is constant within each archived track.
Demeaning removes it before satellite nomination, so both reported arms are
the same estimator. Adding ±200 kHz/GHz and relifting the alias branch
changed no predicted coordinates across eight scans. The maximum feature
difference was 9.4e-15 and the largest frequency RMS difference was 1.1e-12 Hz.
This checks fixed-track features. The upstream graph was already assembled.

Displaced synthetic validation gave 2.86 km median and 6.15 km P95 for the
network, 2.20 and 4.27 km for the grid peak, 10.13 and 14.87 km for the no-radio
search center, and 14.40 and 23.05 km when permuting maps across epochs.
The real panel gave 3.25 and 9.50 km for the network and 5.68 and 11.69 km for
the grid peak. All eight real scans share the campaign site. Permuting the
scan-to-map pairing preserved aggregate real errors. A constant output fit
from the same campaign reference predicted all eight labels exactly without
radio. That control intentionally reuses the test geography.

The frozen model has 1,730 float32 parameters (6,920 bytes) and 1,696 MACs.
One host pass averaged about 3.5 µs in a 1,000-call warm loop. Per-scan wall
time included graph read/parse, causal catalogue read, SGP4, nomination, map
generation, network and RF diagnostics, with one BLAS thread. The median was
0.181 s and included 0.158 s for TLE/SGP4 and 0.015 s for nominations/map.
The timing starts from archived graphs. It excludes acquisition, detector,
graph construction, sensitivity checks, and offline training. The optimizer
returned finite weights at the prespecified 160-iteration budget.

The network recovered information in simulation, where the grid peak gave
smaller held-out errors. The eight real scans cover one receiver site.
Different-site field validation and target-device timing remain unmeasured.

From the repository root run

    export OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1
    export PYTHONPATH=/home/mouse9911/.cache/accuracy-repair-20261115/frozen-001/src
    .venv/bin/python reports/2026_11_18_embedded_prototypes/10_network/network.py --check
    .venv/bin/python reports/2026_11_18_embedded_prototypes/10_network/network.py

The first command checks profiled shape cost against explicit least squares
and verifies agreement between single and batched network inference. The
second writes result.json and the packed float32 weights.npy.
