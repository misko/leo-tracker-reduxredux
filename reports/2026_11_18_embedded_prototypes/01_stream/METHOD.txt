01 Streaming track and position

Primary design chosen before scoring geography

The tracker consumes archived fractional-margin-passing candidates in sample
counter order. Each probe can supply up to four distinct candidates in
descending margin order. Candidates separated by under 350 Hz modulo the
1/4.4 us alias period merge at the strongest candidate. Each receiver/channel/
RF lane uses at most eight constant-rate alpha–beta states. The first
continuation initializes the rate. Subsequent gains are 0.8 and 0.35 with a
rate cap of ±8 kHz/s. The innovation gate depends on time gap and track maturity.
Greedy assignment uses normalized innovation, a first-hit penalty, and a small
rank preference. Each peak and track can link once per probe. A candidate is
skipped when its two best confirmed track costs differ by under 0.12.

Confirmation requires six hits over at least 1.5 s. States expire on the next
probe in their lane after 2.5 s idle for one/two hits or 6 s otherwise, and at
most 180 s of elapsed support is linked per segment. On overflow, an unexplained
peak may evict a tentative track, with age and hits breaking ties. Frequency
payloads keep actual alias-lifted samples. When at capacity, the tracker
retains every other stored point, doubles the sampling stride, and separately
keeps the latest point. It can retain up to 64 retired curved segments and
submits up to 64 ranked by hit count, including eligible live segments. A
segment must span 20 s, have at least 12 retained samples, and have at least
250 Hz RMS around a straight line.

The compact solver in ../support/cold_replay.py uses a prior-available TLE
snapshot, a 48 h element age cutoff, five-second orbit knots, and the common
25 km square. It samples 12 points per submitted track, retains 12 satellite
alternatives, votes for receiver-side biases, and searches a 7 by 7 position
grid. Tracks with cost below 12 enter refinement, which requires four tracks
and three distinct satellite assignments. Both arms reuse the same data,
assignments, grid start, alias policy, and four robust solve passes. One fixes
c to zero and one fits a shared RF-dependent term with the support solver's
prior. The retained RFs vary within a receiver-side group on every case and
deadline, so the term has variation beyond group intercepts. Physical
calibration and per-fix integrity remain unverified. The shared solver returns
absolute in-sample RF RMS and flags box-edge fixes.

Verification and cost

An incremental replay freezes solves at the 60- and 120-second air deadlines
and reads observations only when the 20 ms probe is complete. The archive
reader may buffer raw text and extract the next record's timestamp to detect
the cutoff. Its candidate payload stays unparsed until eligible. All 16
deadline fits exactly match independent reopened-prefix runs in selected
observations, satellite assignments, grid score, and both RF/location
estimates. Continuing through both checkpoints reproduced the uninterrupted
full tracker on all eight cases. A synthetic malformed-future-record check
guards the parsing cutoff. Four tracker checks cover duplicate alias peaks,
multi-track unwrapping and compaction, ambiguous competition, and expiration.

Full host wall time includes opening native JSON, decoding and ranking probes,
state updates, segment selection, TLE bank construction, shortlist, bias/grid,
and both c solves. CPU times and stage wall times are recorded separately. The
prefix and graph control runs are outside the primary full clock. Both arms
yielded estimates in all eight cases at each air deadline and at full duration.
The primary full median was 0.426 s wall (range 0.406–0.528 s), with median
0.035 s state updates, 0.129 s archive decode/candidate ranking, 0.173 s orbit
bank, and 0.075 s satellite shortlist. Median absolute RF residual RMS was
230.0 Hz at c=0 and 202.2 Hz with fitted c over the full scans. The full-scan
graph control produced 225.8 and 208.0 Hz median RF RMS, respectively. All
geographic scoring is external to this replay.

The nominal tracker bound is 16 lanes, 128 live states, 32 continuation tests
per probe, and 128 time/CFO pairs per live or retired segment. The persistent
numeric payload bound is 384 KiB at two float64 values per pair, before state
scalars, Python objects, archive parser buffers, and temporary freeze copies.
The peak seen was 51 simultaneous states and seven in a lane. The JSON reader
buffers chunks of 64 Ki characters plus boundary text and one pending record.
The full-scan float64 orbit banks occupy 2.14–2.85 MB in the eight runs. The
packed shortlist arrays occupy 0.41–0.43 MB. Whole-process RSS, SGP4 scratch
space, and optimizer scratch space remain unmeasured.

IQ acquisition, pilot detection, live retuning, target hardware latency, power,
and integrity qualification were outside this host replay. Results use the
native detector's archived probes and the panel's regional search prior.
