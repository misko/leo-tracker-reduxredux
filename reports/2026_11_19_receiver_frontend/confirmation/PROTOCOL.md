# Late pilot confirmation protocol

Test a decision for each passing fractional candidate from the ordinary C GLRT on the sealed 10 MS/s, 20 ms dual-CI16 roof recordings DS26-0036 and DS29-0082. Preserve the narrow ordinary proposal set, the original order, numerical scores, fractional epochs, CFOs and the capture exclusion policy. The new decision may remove an originally passing candidate from tracking support.

At the candidate's fixed fractional epoch and fractional tracking CFO, test the odd Qin symbols 79–141 and 199–261. Each region uses 32 symbols with 36 interior raw sample times per symbol. Include only frames with both complete regions. Compare coherent projection power onto the exact published edge pilot sequence and the existing 17-symbol rolled sequence, with independently unit-normalized templates. Normalize the paired difference by measured within-region IQ energy and the nominal white-noise variance including the exact/control template inner product. Each frame and each region uses its own unknown complex gain. The decision requires both signed region scores to exceed zero and their sum divided by √2 to reach 2.5. The standardization sets the engineering scale for the threshold. It is not a calibrated false-alarm probability under colored receiver noise or proposal selection.

The ordinary final GLRT uses symbols 2–65. Its ordinary fine acquisition FFT uses even symbols. Native coarse proposal folding uses phase-invariant lag correlations over the full frame, and the conditioned boundary fallback can use full-frame coherent correlation. The late-symbol statistic therefore adds coding and fixed-frequency evidence outside the fractional GLRT and fine FFT, with residual statistical dependence on proposal selection and greater overlap for fallback rows.

Before consulting position outputs, replay sequences 0–7 from segments 0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 and 12 in each frozen recording. Verify the manifest, per-window IQ, capture exclusions, producer identities, native library hash, and archived producer probe hash. Compare native positives to the capture-era producer positives one-to-one at the same receiver and visit, within two samples modulo a frame and 1 kHz modulo 227.273 kHz. Report retention separately for archive-matched positives, native-only positives and native candidates below the original margin. The archive comparison measures agreement and has no independent satellite truth. Record fallback use and CPU time spent on original positives. The decision advances only if each recording retains at least 85% of matched native positives, exceeds the native-only retention rate by at least 15 percentage points, and adds less than 10 ms median and 25 ms P95 host CPU per valid visit.

Synthetic checks exercise physical exact and rolled waveforms, a fractional epoch, both receivers and pilot edges, complex Gaussian noise, wrong time and frequency, missing energy and unchanged input rows. If the decision advances, only fully verified, whole recordings enter the original v5 solver, with the ordinary native result as the matched control. Both calibration arms use the same retained factors, causal catalogue, search budget and solver policy. Position error and conditional Doppler RMS are scored separately.

After the original decision missed its 15-point agreement contrast on both recordings, repeat the same fixed measurement on sequences 0–7 from alternate segments 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11 and 13. No decision threshold changes between the two samples.

Outside the candidate sample, reproduce one known capture exclusion from each producer through its installed authenticated segment reader and capture validator. Compare visit, IQ hash, sample counters and exclusion reason to the frozen archive, and verify the detector callback is never invoked.
