# Transmitter frequency dynamics: source-only hypothesis audit

**Channel-dependent transmitted CFO corrections are supported by primary research, but their presence in DS21-039 and any localization benefit remain unproven.** This audit performed no dataset predictor, likelihood, gradient, optimizer, raw-IQ, held or reference calls and changed no scientific source.

Saroufim, Hayek and Kassas, *Mind the Beam: Starlink PNT Challenges and Mitigation Strategies*, ION GNSS+ September 2026, reports measurements of different channels from the same satellite with distinct Doppler-bias dynamics. Its publisher abstract identifies small zero-order CFO corrections, larger sawtooth corrections and synchronized channel clusters. These are observed frequency behaviors; the abstract does not establish a universal beam-center Doppler-precompensation law, cadence or correction amplitude applicable to our capture. [Publisher and DOI](https://www.ion.org/publications/abstract.cfm?articleID=20898), [author publication index](https://ece.osu.edu/aspin/publications), [author PDF](https://people.engineering.osu.edu/media/document/2026-10-04/kassas_mind_the_beam_starlink_pnt_challenges_and_mitigation_strategies.pdf). The author index links an 11-page paper; full-body retrieval timed out and a direct request returned HTTP 403. The new paper's full text was therefore **not verified** here.

Accessible full primary text provides a useful limit. Kozhaya, Saroufim and Kassas, *Unveiling Starlink for PNT*, Section 7.2, pp. 19–20, observes one-second OFDM CFO steps in its pre-2024 data, but reports their absence in its July 2024 cohort. Its unmodulated **nine mid-channel pilot tones** differed from the OFDM observable. Those tones are not our **eight modulated Qin edge-pilot subcarriers**. Consequently neither correction immunity nor a universal one-second correction schedule transfers to DS21. The paper's orbital-error correction also used known receiver position, which is unavailable as inference authority here. [Full primary paper](https://people.engineering.osu.edu/media/document/2025-08-06/kassas_unveiling_starlink_for_pnt.pdf).

Qin et al., *Pilots and Other Predictable Elements of the Starlink Ku-Band Downlink*, Sections V-A and V-C, separates transmitter carrier, sample and frame clocks. Its effective CFO includes carrier-clock drift as well as geometric Doppler; its effective sample-frequency offset need not equal CFO. This supports a distinct transmitter frequency term, without identifying a particular steering algorithm. [Primary paper and equations 17–19](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02627v1).

The current native prediction is geometric Doppler

`f_pred(i,j) = -RF_i * range_rate_j(t_native_i + timing_shift_j, position) / light_speed + receiver_correction(i)`.

The compiled kernel computes this at `src/leo/analysis/_regional_orbits.cpp:27`; the Python oracle agrees at `src/leo/analysis/regional_position_score.py:90`. `src/leo/analysis/joint_association_sparse.py:34` applies the satellite timing shifts and `:39` adds native-time receiver correction. The surrounding group model supplies receiver offsets/drifts, fitted common c and receiver low/high contrasts; Model B also has smooth receiver clocks. There is **no explicit satellite-by-channel/beam transmitted frequency correction**. A constant shared fractional term can be absorbed by existing calibration, but different channel-dependent time variation generally cannot. Orbit timing, geography and mixture assignments can then compensate for it. That last statement is a model-confounding inference, not evidence that it occurs in DS21-039; arbitrary frequency offsets would also weaken geographic identifiability.

The existing [temporal metadata audit](TEMPORAL_EVIDENCE.md) binds 2,798 original training windows and 3,714 retained native hypotheses. It records receiver, channel, exact RF, visit and native support-center timestamps, but no true satellite, beam, service-cell or persistent frame identity. Every original receiver/channel/visit/RF family is a singleton. Same-receiver/channel/RF winner revisits have minimum 0.352 s and median 0.356 s; gaps can be long. Alternative native candidates within a window overlap in recorded sample support. The first-sample UTC bracket is about 250 ms. Thus current MAP satellite labels, equal channels or nearby revisits cannot establish same-emitter continuity, and a precise UTC-aligned one-second correction test lacks authority. Earlier local slope reports list transmitter steering among unresolved explanations; they do not establish it. The completed block-residual correlation audit already found strong but endpoint-dependent structure and negligible-support pitfalls; it is not proof of transmitted corrections.

One bounded falsification diagnostic is defensible **before adding any nuisance term**: test whether residual changes between visits contain a reproducible component shared across receivers on the same exact channel/RF. This differs from the earlier block-mean correlation audit and from fitting a temporal persistence prior.

1. Use exactly the sealed original-all and seven-call refined-all states, fitted c only. Reuse `tools/ds21_039_association_diagnostic.py:119` to obtain native residual moments and the full branch/satellite/clutter mixture at each fixed state. Do not take any MAP label as identity or fit geography, calibration, channel offsets, correction periods or phases.
2. Within each original TRAINING 20-second block and exact receiver/channel/RF stratum, form consecutive-window residual increments from posterior-weighted native residual means. Marginalize all native branches and candidate satellites. Retain signal-mass weights, elapsed times and sampling-alias uncertainty; omit only exactly zero signal mass. Never average barely supported conditional means with equal block weight. Do not join held/quarantined blocks or change their original labels.
3. Match RX0/RX1 increment intervals only on the same channel/RF and overlapping receive-time intervals. Use deterministic one-to-one nearest-midpoint matching, with original IDs breaking ties. Report every interval width, overlap and unmatched count; overlapping intervals are sampling support, not same-emitter evidence. The prespecified descriptive readout is signal-mass-weighted increment covariance/correlation for every channel, with weight equal to the minimum of the four endpoint signal masses. No best-channel selection or free jump-size fit.
4. Compare with 100 seeded whole-TRAINING-block permutations of RX1 within each channel/RF, preserving its within-block order, retunes, gaps and increments and translating block-relative times only for matching. Seed 20261012; report permutation distributions and support counts rather than treating correlated pairs as independent samples. These are descriptive controls, not calibrated p-values: changing mixtures and support do not guarantee block exchangeability. Require the readout to be interpretable at both saved states; do not choose the favorable state. A support preflight may conclude that the capture cannot discriminate this hypothesis.

Proposed private API: `audit_shared_increments(endpoint_audits, original_training_metadata, *, seed=20261012, permutations=100)`. If approved, a separate source-bound runner would permit **two fixed-state native prediction calls, zero fits**, and at most **90 s including training preparation**, with external TERM/KILL at 105/110 s. No raw IQ or new RF collection is required. This is a proposed diagnostic only; neither implementation nor execution occurred in this audit.

A shared excess would challenge a purely independent-receiver smooth-error explanation. It could also arise from synchronized detector ambiguity, retune transients, changing emitter mixtures or shared acquisition behavior; it would not identify a satellite or uniquely establish transmit precompensation. Absence would constrain only a component shared by these receivers at the available sampling cadence and support, not all beam-specific corrections. The evidence justifies checking this model mismatch; it does not justify a new prior, universal correction law or accuracy-selected channel tuning.
