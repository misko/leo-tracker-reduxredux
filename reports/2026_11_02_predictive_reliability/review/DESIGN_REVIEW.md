# Independent design review before predictive outcomes

This review recommends a bounded explanatory comparison of SD1 versus SD4 with half damping on the fixed five-case contrast panel. It does not run fits, change sealed inference sources, or choose a rule using new predictive scores. The panel was selected for known localization contrasts, so success would be mechanism evidence on a challenge panel, not independent generalization or a validated production selector.

## What can transfer between receivers

Literal whole-receiver withholding leaves that receiver's band offsets, drift, band contrast, and smooth calibration columns unsupported by training observations. A posterior based only on their declared engineering priors is mathematically proper, but does not demonstrate learned calibration transfer. Fixing those parameters to zero or using their full-data posterior means would make the test invalid.

The proposed **target-family × receiver** transfer is a useful different experiment: withhold all target-family observations on one receiver, retain that family's observations on the other receiver to learn shared phase/orbit, and retain other families on the held receiver to learn its hardware. Reverse the direction for the second cell. This tests whether the widened family model transfers to another receiver after independent-family calibration; it does not test zero-shot transfer to an uncalibrated receiver.

Before fitting each direction, require target-family training support on the other receiver and at least two other training families on the held receiver in the held target's band, with training support overlapping the held interval. If these structural conditions fail, return `inconclusive: insufficient transfer support`; do not replace the split or target. Record actual time overlap, counts, and shared receiver/band support rather than relying on one overall source count. Remaining prior domination is an interpretation limit even when these minimum conditions hold.

Report training design singular values and posterior-versus-prior predictive hardware variance on the held rows. Break down the offset, rate and smooth contributions when practical; one trace ratio can hide an unidentified slope behind a well-estimated offset. Raw rank of the complete design is not by itself a useful pass/fail test because inactive receiver, phase and fragment columns can be present by construction. Priors can regularize a null direction without supplying new calibration evidence. Retain the same existing causal physical-mode columns for SD1 and SD4; do not create support or tune priors using held residuals.

## Leakage controls

Common starts should be `x=[0,0]` at the pre-existing regional-box center, and zero phase-reference starts (or one recorded causal-only phase-mean choice), with identical budgets for both models and c arms. Full-data position and phase starts carry held information through local basins, stopping and phase-reference schedules even when train-only refitting follows. They are suitable only for a separately labeled transductive sensitivity experiment.

Use fixed 150 Hz point noise and unit likelihood powers in the clean primary arm, as proposed by the preprocessing audit. This avoids fitting a per-observation polynomial scale, heteroscedastic switch, residual-derived weight, or family influence weight using held observations. Coefficient, noise-Gamma and phase-prior-Gamma posterior updates must use training observations only. A wholly withheld fragment keeps its declared prior noise distribution; its noise cannot be estimated from its held polynomial fit. Original empirical noise/powers may be retained as an explicitly inherited/leaky sensitivity, not quietly substituted for the primary clean policy.

The fixed identities, aliases, graph joins, point masks, causal history, RF-selected target family and prior model roster still originate in full-data development. Freeze and hash them before the splits. This makes the experiment conditional on that graph/identity inventory. It cannot establish blind candidate discovery, global mask quality, or identity uniqueness. Synchronized whole-time groups and guards must include every receiver/channel at the same times; alternative peaks and overlapping acquisition support cannot be split independently. The receiver-transfer experiment deliberately allows other-family contemporaneous calibration and should say so.

## Predictive scoring and uncertainty

For each matched fold, score the exact same held native IDs using a normalized **joint** held-data density, retaining shared calibration, phase/orbit and position uncertainty. Correlated fragments and receiver modes cannot be counted as independent point predictions. Do not place the fractional training likelihood powers on held sampling densities. If the predictive noise model remains white conditional on shared modes, state that residual correlation is not fully modeled; guards protect train/test separation but do not make held samples independent.

The existing diagonal-plus-low-rank Gaussian score is a proper normalized density for its declared Gaussian approximation. The local position Jacobian must differentiate the total training posterior mean, including training nuisance adjustment, if its covariance is conditional on position. Otherwise adding position uncertainty can double-count or omit position/nuisance cross-covariance. The local Hessian belongs to the same training-only objective and the reference used by that endpoint.

The previous bounded-box correction is exact only **within the frozen local joint Gaussian model**: multiply its unbounded predictive density by posterior-box probability divided by training-posterior-box probability. It is not integration of the nonlinear regional posterior. A box correction does not rescue a nonstationary optimum, negative curvature, a curvature floor, or a nonclosed phase reference. Preserve and qualify those flags. Prior/posterior box probabilities and deterministic quadrature agreement should be recorded, especially for near-boundary solutions.

Conditioning on fitted Gamma precisions does not integrate their posterior uncertainty. A withheld fragment's Gamma(2,2) prior predictive is heavy-tailed; using variance `2 sigma²` in a Gaussian matches a moment, not that distribution. A normalized Gaussian approximation can be compared as such, but neither its score gaps nor transformed model weights are calibrated probabilities. A bounded optional precision/noise sensitivity can reuse sealed train endpoints; if it changes the ordering, report model sensitivity or inconclusive rather than choosing the favorable noise policy. Such a sensitivity and its budget must be frozen before new scores.

The proposed moment correction is verified against the actual `hierarchical_bound` implementation: `alpha0=beta0=2`, `alpha=2 + power*n_train/2`, and the returned precision mean is `alpha/rate`. Therefore `E[1/tau] = alpha / ((alpha-1)*mean_precision)`. With unit powers this is `alpha=2+n_train/2`; the wholly unseen group uses its prior value **2**, bypassing the returned zero placeholder for an absent fitted group. Counts refer to the original noise group's retained training points, not a satellite family or held group count. This moment correction is preferable to reciprocal mean precision, but still produces a moment-matched Gaussian rather than a Gamma-mixture predictive density. Require finite positive scales and a converged inner update (`final_log_precision_change <= 1e-5`); reaching the 256-update cap without that convergence cannot be hidden by successful outer position optimization.

Primary all-held, target-held, and other-family-held densities are marginal densities over different subsets. Their score gains are useful separate diagnostics but do not add to the joint score because shared nuisance uncertainty couples them. A conditional chain-rule decomposition is possible if implemented explicitly; it must not install a held-driven calibration estimate into subsequent training.

## Suggested predeclared qualification and decision rule

Use three fixed random whole-time-group folds and the two fixed target-family receiver-transfer directions. Preserve every failure. Keep SD1/SD4 and c=0/fitted-c observations, starts, prior families, budgets, preprocessing and partitions matched. A ten-minute initial execution cap and four BLAS=1 workers are adequate bounds; stopped cells remain unrun or capped, not failed statistical evidence.

For an interpretable score comparison, require **both models in both c arms** to have optimizer success, raw phase gap ≤0.01 s, converged inner Gamma updates, no position-boundary flag, positive unfloored position curvature, valid finite normalized densities, valid box quadrature, and the exact frozen train/held ID hashes. Require the declared structural training support. A disqualified cell remains visible but cannot cast a predictive vote. Residual adequacy and active prior pulls should be reported separately; do not fit a new noise scale to make a vote qualify. A concrete numerical box criterion is relative quadrature error ≤1e-6, with seeded-CDF agreement when that CDF remains numerically resolvable. Score wins should exceed the propagated integration-error bound and a fixed small arithmetic tolerance rather than merely being positive in floating point.

An optional stronger local-Gaussian credibility check can be frozen before outcomes: evaluate the **training-only** objective at the four ±1-standard-deviation covariance-axis points, keeping the endpoint's reference and model fixed. All four must remain inside the box and have `abs([F(probe)-F(mode)]-1) <= 0.5`; otherwise report the cell inconclusive under the local approximation. Here `F` is the training -2 objective, so its local Gaussian predicts a rise of one at those points. These are evaluations, not refits or held-data fits. The check is conservative and does not prove Gaussian accuracy. If omitted, retain the explicit local-approximation limitation rather than claiming that an accurate box integral establishes nonlinear posterior validity.

Define positive gain as `(-2 log p_SD1(held|train)) - (-2 log p_SD4(held|train))`. Before scoring, choose either the conservative rule below or another fully specified rule; do not choose after seeing the five outcomes:

- `SD4 conditionally favored`: all three whole-time-group folds and both receiver-transfer directions qualify, joint held gain is positive in **each c arm** in every cell, and the marginal other-family-held gain in the randomized folds is nonnegative in each c arm. Require gains to exceed recorded numerical integration error rather than treating a sign at floating-point precision as a win.
- `SD1 conditionally favored`: the same requirements hold after exchanging SD1 and SD4.
- `Inconclusive`: missing/invalid support, caps, failed numerical qualification, cross-arm or cross-cell disagreement, target gain paid for by other-family harm, or an unresolved uncertainty-model sensitivity. Do not discard a disagreeing cell or break ties using GPS.

This all-cell sign rule is intentionally conservative and has no claim to a calibrated significance level; the overlapping training folds are not independent replications. If too conservative to choose a model on this small panel, the proper outcome is inconclusive. Report continuous gains, point/group counts and all qualification reasons even when the categorical rule is inconclusive. Freeze any less stringent majority rule, practical effect threshold, or fallback policy before scoring, not as a response to inconclusive results.

A predictive preference is about the declared RF model. GPS should be released only after the RF scores, qualifications and categorical outputs are sealed, and used to describe the fixed contrast panel. Better prediction alone is not a guarantee of better position, particularly with weak geometry and correlated receiver/orbit modes.
