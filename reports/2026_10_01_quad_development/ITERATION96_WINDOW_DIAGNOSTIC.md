# Fixed-point acquisition diagnostic after the iteration pilot

The 96-iteration pilot recovers numerical acceptance but DS9-B05-S2 remains about 46 km from the operator reference. Its final position moves only 2.9 m after the original iteration limit, while the saved accepted first-start quad is about 493 m from the reference. This motivates a post-result diagnostic of competing location evidence, separate from the preregistered cap experiment.

Evaluate the original acquisition score on all four DS9-B05 scans at four saved coordinates: single S2's acquired first seed and accepted 96-iteration endpoint, plus the quad's acquired first seed and accepted first-fit endpoint. Set every nuisance coordinate to zero, exactly as acquisition does. Sum candidate/background log-sum-exp track scores by scan and across the window. Also compare original and matrix-product scores/masks at these points. No search, nuisance profiling, new localization fit, reference-coordinate scoring or parameter tuning. Cap the diagnostic process at 90 seconds and use the shared lock.

The main contrast is each scan's score at the quad endpoint minus its score at the S2 endpoint. Positive values favor the quad endpoint under this fixed zero-nuisance acquisition score. A sum is an implementation score contrast, not a calibrated Bayes factor; the fits themselves use different, optimized nuisance vectors and hard associations. These four data-selected coordinates and this error-selected case cannot establish geographic generalization, mode probabilities, or a deployable confidence rule. Freeze source/input bindings and retain all contrasts, regardless of direction.
