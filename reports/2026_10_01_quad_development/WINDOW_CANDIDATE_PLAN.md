# Existing location candidates versus acquisition ranking

The 96-iteration experiment recovered numerical acceptance without solving location ambiguity. A post-result DS9-B05 diagnostic showed complementary scan evidence favoring the accurate quad location over the distant S2 mode. Test the scope of this observation across all sixteen frozen blocks, without new localization fits.

Freeze two candidate sources per unit: its accepted first fit, and its original three-start winner if that winner passed the original audit. For exactly the three originally unresolved first fits, use the newly accepted 96-iteration receipt. No other substitutions. Keep duplicate coordinates as labeled candidate entries; do not interpret entry count as distinct posterior modes. This mixes archived estimates with three new cold estimates and is not a fresh benchmark of a unified implementation.

Evaluate all 112 targets: 64 singles, 32 disjoint pairs and 16 quads. A candidate is eligible only when its source scans are a subset of the target's scans. Thus singles cannot use pair/quad or other-single locations; pairs cannot use the other pair or the quad. Compare (a) target first-fit coordinate, (b) acquisition-score selection among target-unit candidates, and (c) acquisition-score selection among all eligible contained-window candidates. Candidate generation may have substantial existing inference cost: this no-refit experiment does not establish an end-to-end budget.

Compute each scan's original acquisition score at every saved candidate coordinate in its block: sum the trackwise log-sum-exp over satellite/background branches, with every nuisance coordinate fixed to zero. Target score is the sum over its scans. Compare original and optimized implementations at all points, requiring exact finite/visibility masks and per-track score differences below 1e-6. Use original scores for decisions. Prefer the target first fit if it is within 1e-6 of the maximum; otherwise use the first eligible entry within that tolerance, ordered by first-fit units then original-winner units in frozen selection order. Do not read error fields or reference coordinates in candidate construction or scoring. Seal ranking decisions before separate evaluation.

Verify source/input bindings. Score blocks sequentially under the shared lock, each with a 90-second external process cap; stop on missing output or a failed numerical gate and retain all outcomes. There are no fits, retries, proposal searches or nuisance optimization. Unit tests must reject future/sibling scan leakage and verify deterministic tie handling and independence from error metadata.

Only after all sixteen blocks are sealed, evaluate reference errors of the frozen coordinate decisions. In a separately labeled diagnostic, compute best reference error among the target-only and contained candidate sets. These oracle choices use the answer and must never feed inference. They measure finite-pool headroom, not attainable accuracy or a bound on all possible models. Report planned denominators, all five policies, dataset variation, candidate counts and paired changes. Figures must distinguish the oracle from score-based policies.

Candidate positions from accepted source fits are not automatically audited full target-window fits: a transferred coordinate is merely a proposal. Zero-nuisance acquisition is not a nuisance-profiled/marginalized posterior or calibrated confidence measure. All windows are correlated exposed development evidence at one unsurveyed reference site. This diagnostic can distinguish absent candidates from poor ranking within this pool; it cannot alone promote a runtime policy or establish generalization.
