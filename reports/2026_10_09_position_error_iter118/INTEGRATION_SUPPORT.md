# Finite observation support supplies a time scale, with an estimator caveat

The positioning observations come from original passing fractional GLRT64 candidates, not instantaneous samples. `application/regional_position_inputs.py:prepare_position_windows` projects the scanner candidates, chooses one winner per source group by measured margin/rank, and stores the **support centre** as `PositionObservations.times_s`. The resulting positioning contract drops support start/end and moments. Those remain available through the public scanner projection and its candidate IDs; no new IQ collection or reference position is necessary to recover them in a research adapter.

`application/scanner_trajectory.py:project_scanner_candidates` maps device counters plus fractional template positions to UTC. It retains nonoverlapping 20 ms probes within each visit/receiver; that rule concerns probe overlap, not continuous pilot occupancy. `application/persistent_hop_trajectory.py:fractional_glrt64_support_geometry` reconstructs symbols 2 through 65 in each frame, skips incomplete/guard-invalid symbols, and takes the arithmetic mean of their centres. It also stores bounding source samples and second/third factorial moments. Frame rate is 750 Hz; symbol duration is 4.4 microseconds (`analysis/starlink/templates.py`). Thus a complete 64-symbol burst spans approximately 281.6 microseconds, repeated about every 1.333 milliseconds through the 20 ms probe. Fractional interpolation guards slightly enlarge the bounding source range. The support is sparse, not a uniformly weighted continuous 20 ms CPI. A bounding start/end pair or a few moments cannot reconstruct its full weighting law.

## A parameter-free geometric occupancy calculation

For known support intervals and weights, define

`v_ij = integral w_i(t) 1{(p_j(t+tau_j)-r) dot u >= 0} dt / integral w_i(t) dt`.

This replaces an arbitrary angular bandwidth with the actual measurement-support time scale. Candidate receiver position/normal and orbit shift determine geometry; known receiver coordinates are absent. For each orbit interpolation segment, the **unnormalized** horizon numerator `h(t)=(p(t)-r) dot u` is affine. Its zero is exact within the segment even though normalized sine elevation is not affine. Split every pilot interval at ephemeris nodes, compute the single crossing when signs differ, and sum visible lengths (or the correctly weighted integral). This needs no orbit root-fitting or angular smoothing parameter. Uniform density on a continuous interval gives a clipped linear ramp as the crossing moves through it; sparse pilot intervals give continuous piecewise ramps with gaps. It is generally nonsmooth at interval/node endpoints, not C1. A sum over isolated centre samples would retain step discontinuities and is not a replacement for interval integration.

For an interior simple crossing `t*`, implicit differentiation gives `dt*/dtheta = -(dh/dtheta)/(dh/dt)`. Observer spatial derivatives include both `-dr/dtheta dot u` and `(p-r) dot du/dtheta`; total satellite time shift enters `dh/dtau = position_secant dot u`. The derivative of visible length is the appropriate signed crossing derivative. Tangential/zero-slope horizon contacts and interval-boundary coincidences require explicit event handling; do not divide by a nearly zero slope or hide these cases behind a numerical gradient.

## Occupancy is not yet a validated detection probability

The measured CFO is `fractional_tracking_cfo_hz` from the native conditioned GLRT estimator. Passing the margin gate conditions on detection. A satellite visible for 10% of the pilot samples does not automatically have 10% of its ordinary detection probability: coherent/incoherent aggregation, per-symbol normalization, signal power and the candidate gate affect that relationship. Likewise its returned CFO and uncertainty can shift when only part of the support contributes. The existing projected factorial moments use equal symbol-centre weights; they are a documented approximation to support, not proof of an estimator influence function.

Consequently `p=q*v` in the coherent soft likelihood is a **declared occupancy-to-detectability model assumption**, even if the occupancy calculation itself has no free width. Before recording comparison, trace native GLRT accumulation/normalization and either justify this approximation under a fixed constant-amplitude model or test it with bounded synthetic partial-visibility signals and the actual estimator. Predict frequency over the same contributing support, or explicitly disclose retaining centre-frequency prediction as an approximation. No position-error-based adjustment is needed or allowed.

Practical next source experiment: recover exact pilot intervals from the existing epoch/sample-rate/probe authority, implement affine-segment occupancy and analytic crossing derivatives, test an interval crossing/ephemeris node/gap/tangency with synthetic geometry, then audit native detection aggregation. Freeze the resulting estimator model before matched c=0/fitted-c recording comparisons. This is more physically grounded than choosing an angular width, but cannot yet be called a validated smooth detection law. No recording inputs, objectives or fits were opened for this audit.
