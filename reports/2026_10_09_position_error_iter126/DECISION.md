# Frame convention matters, but fixed endpoints do not support switching it

All 12 members completed in both arms, with zero original-score reconstruction differences and no failures. Replacing satellite-relative ECEF query timing with constant-rate relative orbital phase changes predicted frequencies substantially: median RMS difference is 97.830 Hz fitted-c and 97.704 Hz c=0. The alternative NLL is higher for every endpoint (median +1556.500 and +1455.717 respectively). No visibility mask changed and the normalizer difference is zero throughout.

These results isolate a frequency-model change rather than the horizon-normalization discontinuity. They do not choose which convention is physically correct: endpoints and their satellite shifts, RF calibration and clocks were optimized under the original convention. Holding them fixed naturally disadvantages a changed parameter interpretation. Conversely, a future refit with better in-sample score would not by itself demonstrate geographic improvement.

Keep production unchanged. A further experiment needs independent evidence that satellite-relative timing represents orbital phase rather than timestamp uncertainty, plus a separately frozen matched-arm fit/control if authorized. Reusing the current shifts as direct physical phase estimates is not justified by this audit. The result also does not establish this mechanism as the common southward displacement found in124; no reference position or accuracy was evaluated here.

Summed receipt costs are 141.388 seconds, including reconstruction. One worker ran the two shards serially; measured job wall time and peak memory were not separately recorded. All original sources and receipts remain immutable.
