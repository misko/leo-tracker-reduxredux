# ECEF query time versus orbital phase: source-only audit

Current `hard60_score.predict_orbits` queries the quarter-second ECEF bank at observation receive time plus common and satellite-relative timing. Position and velocity are interpolated separately. This is a **timestamp/state-query shift**: both propagated orbit phase and Earth rotation advance together. It is internally differentiated correctly for that convention. The source alone does not establish whether the fitted satellite-relative nuisance represents measurement timing, TLE along-track phase error, or a mixture. A common receive-clock offset and a satellite orbit-phase correction are physically different.

[Iteration112](../2026_10_09_position_error_iter112/README.md) explicitly identified this distinction. [September24 DS1 orbit-arm research](../2026_09_24_ds1_orbit_arm/REPORT.md) already held Earth rotation at receive time plus global timing while advancing per-NORAD orbital phase. Four bounded runs improved frequency criteria without recovering a new position, and retained negative blind-transfer evidence. Therefore this is not an untested concept or an established B7 bug. No gain should be inferred from changing conventions.

For a simple constant-z Earth rotation approximation, an orbit-phase-only relative shift delta can be represented from the existing ECEF state by `p_phase(t)=Rz(+omega*delta) p_ECEF(t+global+delta)`, where the sign assumes the usual inertial-to-ECEF `Rz(-theta)` convention. With constant delta and Earth rate, the corresponding rotating-frame velocity is `Rz(+omega*delta) v_ECEF(t+global+delta)`. Global timing still advances Earth rotation. The relative-shift position derivative is `Rz(+omega*delta) [position_secant + omega cross position]`; velocity derivative similarly uses the velocity secant plus `omega cross velocity`. Derivatives must follow the separately interpolated position/velocity arrays, not substitute one for the other. Actual frame conventions must be verified against the bank's transform before adopting this approximation; polar motion, precession/nutation and precise UT1 transformations are not supplied by a z-rotation shortcut.

Earth's nominal angular rate is about7.292115e-5 rad/s. A phase-only versus ECEF shift differs by about0.465 km per second at the terrestrial equator, times cosine latitude for a receiver-like horizontal radius, or about0.504 km per second for a6900 km equatorial satellite radius. A1 s offset therefore has a potentially hundreds-of-metres geometric scale; a20 s bound permits about10 km satellite-coordinate rotation. These are frame displacement scales, **not receiver position errors or expected improvement**, and use no actual receiver reference. The orbit line-of-sight projection and nuisance refitting can change their effect dramatically.
