DS16 optimizer boundary root cause and three prototypes

Completed 8 October 2026 UTC. Existing recorded data only; production code and published baseline results are unchanged.

Recommendation: directly bounded satellite timing variables (B) are the strongest numerical recovery prototype: 570 of 678 previously failed coarse fits converge within the original budget. Use targeted recovery and retain good existing candidates. The prior bounded retry still has a slightly lower cohort position error.

What actually failed

S27 is scan-fw-2917f7344e48ba39, captured 7 October 2026 at 18:13:03 UTC. At coarse point (−80, −80) km relative to the prior centre, satellite 65019 approached its −20-second timing bound. The orbit bank already covers shifts from −21.0824 to +21.1095 seconds, so crossing the policy bound by 0.277 microseconds does not require ephemeris extrapolation.

Nevertheless, the original wrapper jumps from a scientific score near 46,806 to 1012 whenever that policy boundary is crossed. The likelihood and its gradient are replaced with a feasibility-only expression. The guard is discontinuous despite its “smooth” source comment.

The SLSQP trace shows the lower-score point was accepted at iteration 4. At iteration 5, after 11 line-search trials, the optimizer advances from the infeasible penalty point. At iteration 6 it returns to a feasible point with score 49,838.97, then converges at iteration 78 to score 49,512.44. The terminal independent KKT residual is 0.000094, which passes the 0.001 threshold. The wrapper instead returns its earlier score 46,806.09 point with KKT residual 5,660.09, retaining the terminal success message.

Both the 5-second/200-iteration and 20-second/600-iteration runs stop along the same path after 156 real likelihood evaluations and 61 penalty evaluations. The extra budget does not change the stopping rule. This is a confirmed boundary-handling and result-bookkeeping failure; it is not evidence that SciPy incorrectly classified that final stationary point.

Source evidence: SciPy 1.18.1 line-search fallback; original regional_position_fit.py lines 145–148 and 179–184; recorded trace_initial.json and boundary-continuity-S27.json.

The three prototypes

  1. A — orbit margin: retain SLSQP and the existing parameterization. Evaluate the true likelihood through the hard timing boundary using the bank’s existing margin. Beyond actual interpolation coverage, use a continuous clipped-score guard with its chain-rule gradient. The ±20-second hard constraint remains.
  2. B — bounded timing variables: optimize each satellite’s total timing shift directly with native ±20-second bounds. Recover common timing as the mean and relative timing through the original zero-sum basis. The original likelihood, timing priors, common ±10-second limit, and receiver slope bounds are unchanged.
  3. C — feasible trust region: use trust-constr with analytic derivatives and keep_feasible=True. This avoids timing-infeasible evaluations, but usually needs more than the original 200 iterations.

All prototypes record the terminal point and best feasible point separately and select an independently stationary candidate when one exists. The promising best feasible point remains available for grid ranking and continuation.

Controlled experiment

Every prototype refits all 678 failed 40-km grid points from their saved original bootstrap vectors using 5 seconds and 200 iterations. One previously converged coarse control per scan is also refit. The repair reranks the existing 400 points, keeps three separated basins, and preserves the original final candidates. It does not use reference coordinates for seeds, candidate selection or retries.

Relative timing sigma remains 2 seconds; common timing sigma remains 3 seconds. The ±60 Hz/s limit applies to each stage’s added receiver frequency slope. Calibration uses at most two starts, with 20 seconds/600 iterations per prefit and postfit. Both final c arms share observations, upstream calibration and satellite candidates and each receive three starts with 20 seconds/600 iterations. c=0 is a final-fit ablation.

The existing randomized whole-scan split is retained (seed 2026100801; 16 development and 32 validation scans). Source digests and policies were frozen before validation. All recordings’ previous outcomes had already been inspected, so this is a repeated-data regression experiment, not unseen validation.

Why this is not a blanket replacement recommendation

Many successful control points find worse-scoring basins when the optimizer path changes. With B, 28 of 48 controls have a best score more than one unit worse; 46 of 48 still converge. The existing successful fits are therefore retained by the tested repair policy. Two original-control replays (S09 and S40) exactly reproduce their saved scores and vectors, confirming those differences are solver-path effects rather than changed inputs.

The prior bounded retry achieves 2.515 km on S27 and 1.877 km mean fitted-c error across DS16. The new variants reach 2.752–3.060 km on S27 and means of 1.880–1.898 km. They address the numerical mechanism directly, but do not establish better localization than the prior retry. S27’s lower frequency RMS and model scores with the new variants coexist with slightly worse position accuracy.

Verification and artifacts
