# Full-cohort discovery comparison: source and cost review

Source/report review only. No model, recording or closed-outcome access.

Iteration 151's predeclared consumed-pilot screen passed. It is evidence to
justify a separately frozen comparison, not deployment authorization or proof
of independent generalization. Its single discovery pass retains three regions
at 12.5 km separation; it is explicitly not the deployed B7 multi-radius
12.5/25/50 pipeline. A full 193-member successor would compare fresh matched
single-pass native and zero-led policies, not establish current B7 parity.
Keep latest ordinary 107 results as a distinct historical comparator, without
splicing new diagnostic successes into its official metric.

Apply one unchanged policy globally: separate fitted-c/zero-c queues, 400 points
each, 40/20/10/5 km levels, original edge priority and three retained regions.
Keep the frozen 150 handoff, both matched final c arms, no 154 repair and no
163 held-score selector. This compares discovery plus its conditional handoff
opportunities, not an isolated grid effect. Never compare cross-bank objectives
to choose a branch.

## Cost and operational limit

The published 151 report records 11,842.522 worker seconds for twelve members:
10,080.223 search, 900.151 native continuation and 862.148 zero-led continuation.
That averages 986.877 seconds per member. Naively extrapolating to 193 members
at two workers gives **26.45 hours**, excluding controller overhead and imbalance.
Recorded member sums range from 551.733 to 1,822.109 seconds; two members exceed
1,800 seconds. Shared-host I/O contention contributed to these costs, so this
is neither a stable forecast nor an embedded benchmark.

The actual declared slice allowance is much larger: six 500-second search
slices plus two 500-second slices for each continuation branch, or 5,000 worker
seconds per member. Across 193 members this is **965,000 worker seconds**:
268.06 worker hours, approximately **134.03 hours at two workers** before
imbalance/overheads. Deadlines are soft, so even that is not a strict wall-time
bound. A claim that the entire experiment is bounded to 27 hours would be false.

The earlier shard-0 interpreter alias deviation must remain disclosed. Its
observed loaded numerical libraries matched the pinned release, but whole
installations differed. The successor should use the immutable 47e executable
for both shards, retaining a fresh execution environment rather than silently
calling the earlier run strictly identical.

## Recommendation

Full consumed-cohort evaluation is scientifically appropriate if the user is
prepared for the measured multi-day ceiling; it addresses whether a small
motivating pilot generalizes across the already consumed 193 recordings.
It is operationally expensive for a lean iteration. Prefer a separately frozen
staged launch of the same full membership and unchanged policy, with a fixed
initial member cap and a resource-only checkpoint before continuing. Do not
change membership or stop for favorable/unfavorable accuracy. Publish incomplete
coverage honestly until all members terminate; no full-cohort means on a subset.

Do not substitute old native endpoints to save one branch: that loses the fresh
matched control. Existing caches can be reused only through explicit immutable
physical/source/stage identity and exact native trace parity, with zero-c cache
aliases prohibited and historical versus incremental costs separated. The
current 151 policy has historical point import off; enabling a shortcut requires
a new reviewed protocol. Running fewer full members with transparent pending
coverage is preferable to pretending a cached historical result is a fresh
matched native control.
