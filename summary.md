## What matters across the reports

- **We already have PSS evidence.** Narrow 2.5 MS/s PSS experiments recover frame timing; the later native-25-MS/s study reports roughly 38 ns timing-fit RMS on a selected strong track. The older edge-pilot-only concept description is incomplete for these later results.
- **PSS timing and long-pilot frequency estimates have different strengths.** A 750 Hz measurement opportunity, 20 ms detection window, 50–125 ms local rate support, and 31.25/62.5 ms PSS update spacing describe different aspects of the system. Sample spacing, search grids, estimator residuals and absolute accuracy are not interchangeable.
- **Source time comes first.** The historical ~104.86 ms sawtooth was traced to receiver refills and missing RF time. Later clean, device-counter-audited captures exist; September high-rate transport tests also show explicitly measured gaps. Judge each recording by its own counters and continuity segments.
- **FPGA arithmetic has worked; acquired feedback remains the milestone.** The September branch review records scheduled native pilot work at 60 MS/s and 750 Hz. It does not establish autonomous acquisition, causal handoff and sustained physical tracking. Combined designs exceeded routing/timing or startup budgets.
- **Keep ambiguity and uncertainty visible.** Symbol-rate CFO aliases, multiple timing modes, intermittent carrier phase, and unreliable absence labels explain several apparent tracking failures. Good waveform matching is not secure spacecraft identity or calibrated PNT.

The relevance column says what each report contributes now. “Historical,” “superseded,” “conditional,” and “duplicate” are deliberate: repeated or older analyses do not automatically add independent scientific support. Generated JSON, figures and source snapshots are supporting artifacts, not hundreds of additional independently reviewed reports. Supporting artifacts were inspected selectively.
